Your IRR with and without the subscription line

A subscription line lifts IRR and leaves the multiple alone. Rebuild the cash flows to see the return underneath.

Cristian Arenas28 October 2026Test it in the Fund Terms Auditor

A subscription line lets a fund buy a company before it calls capital from LPs. The fund borrows against LP commitments, invests, and calls the money months later. LP capital is at work for less time, so IRR goes up. The multiple stays the same.

Rebuild the cash flows

To see the return without the line, move every capital call back to the date of the investment it paid for. Keep distributions and NAV where they are. Interest on the line stays in, since the fund paid it either way.

In the Fund Terms Auditor, Ostrel Capital Partners VI calls capital about six months after each investment. Restated for fees and carry, its net IRR is 4.1% with the line and 3.6% without it. TVPI is 1.14x in both cases.

Why it matters in manager selection

Half a point matters when funds get ranked by IRR. Two funds with the same multiple can land in different quartiles because one runs a longer line. In the Manager Selection Copilot, the line adds 1.0 point to Hasturn Fund II's net IRR, and the 3.4% underneath looks a lot weaker.

Ask the GP for both numbers. When they don't have them, the capital call dates and the investment dates in the capital account history are enough to rebuild it.

A quick check

  • Compare call dates with deal close dates. A gap of three months or more means the line is doing real work.
  • Look at DPI and TVPI first. Neither moves with the line.

The numbers come from the base case of the linked demo. The funds and managers are made up.
