Pacing a private program when the NAVs are a quarter old

Reported NAVs lag public markets. Nowcast them before you size next year's commitments.

Cristian Arenas28 October 2026Test it in the Private Capital Lab

Private fund NAVs arrive about a quarter late. When public markets fall, the public side of the portfolio reprices the same day and the private marks don't. The private allocation looks high, and a pacing model run on those numbers says commit less.

That answer is usually too cautious. The allocation is high because the denominator fell, and the private marks will follow it down over the next two quarters.

Nowcast first

I roll each fund's last reported NAV forward to today. I add the calls and take off the distributions since the statement date, then apply the fund's sensitivity to a public index. I estimate that sensitivity by regressing reported NAV returns on the index at lags of zero, one and two quarters. The lags matter because appraisal marks catch up slowly.

In the Private Capital Lab base case, the reported allocation is 26.5% against a 25% target. Rolled forward, private NAV is $232m and the allocation is 24.0%. The program sits below target.

Then pace

The pacing rule picks each year's commitment so projected private NAV hits the target four years out, after the existing funds call and distribute. On the nowcast it gives $59m for 2027. On the reported numbers it gives less, and the program drifts under target once the marks catch up.

Size the buffer for the bad path

One plan hides the spread. I run the same fund rules through a Monte Carlo with public returns, private returns, calls and distributions drawn together. In the base case, 62% of paths touch the 30% upper band at some point, almost always right after a drawdown. In 17% of paths liquid assets fall below unfunded commitments. That second number sets the cash buffer.

What to watch

  • The nowcast assumes a fund's sensitivity to the index holds. It is weakest right after a big move, so the band around it should widen with each stale quarter.
  • A write-down at one company won't show up in an index beta. Read the statements when they arrive and rerun the plan.

The numbers come from the base case of the linked demo. The funds and managers are made up.
