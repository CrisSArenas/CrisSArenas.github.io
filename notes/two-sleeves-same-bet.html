When two sleeves are running the same bet

Low correlation between sleeves can hide a shared factor bet until a crowded unwind. Factor loadings show it earlier.

Cristian Arenas28 October 2026Test it in the Multi-Strategy Builder

Two hedge fund sleeves can show low correlation for years and still carry the same factor bet. The overlap shows up in a crowded unwind, when both fall together. By then the book has already lost the money, and the correlation matrix that sized it said this should not happen.

An investment committee approves a multi-strategy book on the promise that the sleeves diversify each other. If two of them run the same trade, the book holds one position at a larger size than anyone agreed to. I want that found from factor loadings before the stress month, because a stress month is a poor time to learn it.

How I look for it

I start from each sleeve's monthly net returns and its mandate. In the Multi-Strategy Builder the factors are equity, momentum, value, credit spread and rates. Five factors stand in for a full risk model, so a shared bet outside them will not show.

  1. Label every month as calm or stress, and keep the two sets apart for every correlation I compute.
  2. Regress each sleeve on the factors over a long base window. The demo uses the first seven years.
  3. For each pair, compute the correlation the shared factor loadings explain. A pair overlaps when both sleeves load heavily on the same factor.
  4. Refit the loadings on a recent window, the last 30 months in the demo, and test each loading for drift.
  5. Test whether calm-month correlation between each pair has moved, using a Fisher z.
  6. Read every flag against the mandate line that covers it.
overlap(A,B) = b_A' S_f b_B / (vol_A vol_B)

drift z = (b_recent - b_base)
          / sqrt(se_r^2 + se_b^2)

Fisher z = (atanh r1 - atanh r0)
           / sqrt(1/(n0-3) + 1/(n1-3))

Here b_A and b_B are the two sleeves' loading vectors, S_f is the factor covariance matrix, and vol_A and vol_B are the sleeve volatilities. The overlap number is the part of the pair's correlation that comes through the factors alone. The demo flags drift above a z of 3 and a calm-month correlation shift above 2.5.

The base case

The demo book has five sleeves weighted by equal risk contribution over July 2016 to June 2026. Relative value holds 29.5%, credit 23.1%, macro 19.6%, quant 14.8% and equity long/short 13.0%, each carrying 20.0% of the risk. The book returned 3.4% a year net with 4.8% volatility and a maximum drawdown of 17.0%.

On the full history the book looks diversified. Average correlation between sleeves is 0.10 in calm months. In the 18 stress months it is 0.47. With the same weights, book volatility is 3.3% on the calm covariance and 8.3% on the stress covariance, and the diversification ratio falls from 1.88 to 1.32.

Equity long/short and quant are the only pair with a shared factor bet. Equity long/short loads 0.32 on momentum and quant loads 0.62. The factors alone explain a correlation of 0.44 between them, more than the 0.30 they show in calm months. In stress they correlate 0.67. The Veyl mandate names momentum as a source of return. The Quillmere mandate says the sleeve has no systematic style tilts. The regression disagrees with Quillmere, and I trust the regression.

The worst stress episode ran 11 months from June 2017. Replayed on today's weights the book loses 17.0%. Quant contributed the most at −5.5%, and equity long/short gave −4.7%. Macro made 0.4%.

Drift

Loadings also move. Between the first seven years and the last 30 months, macro's calm-month momentum loading went from 0.10 to 0.79 (z 5.3). The Tarsen mandate rules out a momentum overlay. Over the same windows its calm-month correlation with quant rose from 0.13 to 0.69 (Fisher z 3.0). The overlap table still shows macro and quant with no shared bet, because it uses the first-seven-year loadings. Relative value drifted too, on rates, from 0.08 to 0.78 against a 0.1 cap. That one is a mandate breach on duration and a separate question from momentum crowding.

Three of the five sleeves now carry momentum. I size equity long/short and quant as one momentum position, and I count macro's overlay against the same budget until it is gone.

What changes the answer

The weighting scheme moves the risk around. Under equal risk contribution each sleeve adds a fifth of the variance. Switch the demo to equal weight and the two momentum sleeves take more of the risk than their weight suggests. At 20.0% weight each, equity long/short carries 31.7% of the risk and quant 27.5%, so the two sleeves carry 59.2% of book variance between them. The maximum drawdown deepens from 17.0% to 19.2%.

Risk controls change the drawdown more than the overlap. With volatility targeting and drawdown stops both on, the book returns 4.8% a year with a 10.2% drawdown, against 17.0% with neither. They do not remove the shared bet. Stops act after the loss, and volatility targeting reacts on a 12-month lookback.

The regime labels matter as much as either. Every correlation in this note depends on which months are called stress. If you load your own returns without a regime column, the demo labels the weakest tenth of equity months as stress. A different split will give different calm and stress matrices, so I check the labels before I read the numbers.

Pitfalls

  • Sizing on a full-history correlation matrix. It blends calm and stress months and hides how the book behaves when it matters.
  • Taking the mandate at its word. Quillmere says it has no style tilts and loads 0.32 on momentum.
  • Fitting loadings once. The base overlap table misses macro's new momentum overlay entirely.
  • Testing correlation shifts on all months. Then a crowding episode looks like a lasting change in how the sleeves move.
  • Expecting stops to handle crowding. In an unwind that snaps back, they sell near the low.
  • Forgetting that the weights are in-sample. The demo weights use the full ten-year covariance, so the base book has hindsight built into its weights.

What I check

  1. Split the months into calm and stress, and write down how the split was made.
  2. Compute the calm and stress correlation matrices separately, and book volatility on each with today's weights.
  3. Regress each sleeve's net returns on the factors over a base window, and keep the standard errors.
  4. Compute the factor-explained correlation for every pair, and list the pairs that share a heavy loading.
  5. Refit on the most recent window, and flag loadings whose drift z is above 3.
  6. Test calm-month correlation shifts with a Fisher z, and flag pairs above 2.5.
  7. Put each flag next to the mandate line it touches, and send a written question to the manager when they disagree.
  8. Replay the worst stress episode on today's weights, and rank the sleeves by what they lost.
  9. Treat each overlapping cluster as one position when sizing, and rerun the book on the new weights before any money moves.

What I do with it

I treat two overlapping sleeves as one position when I size them. In the demo that means cutting quant, the weakest and most crowded sleeve, from 15% to 5%, and moving the ten points to credit. Quant made 3.1% gross and 0.6% net a year on average, with a 36.7% drawdown. Credit has calm-month correlations of 0.17 or less with every other sleeve. It has no drift flag and shares no factor bet. The cost is that credit and relative value correlate 0.76 in stress, so I want the rerun before the subscription goes in.

You can run every step above in the Multi-Strategy Builder and change the weighting or the risk controls to see what moves. It also takes your own sleeve returns. The full case for the cut, with the fee and liquidity work, is in the multi-strategy book memo.
