Is this track record one deal?

A strong record often rests on one exit. A few numbers tell you how much of it the GP can repeat.

Cristian Arenas28 October 2026Test it in the Manager Selection Copilot

A strong fund track record often rests on one or two deals. Before I back a re-up, I want to know how much of the record the GP can repeat.

The numbers I start with

  • The share of total gains from the best deal and from the best three. I use gains so that losses count.
  • The loss ratio, meaning the share of invested capital in deals below 1.0x.
  • The fund's multiple with the best deal taken out.

In the Manager Selection Copilot, Hasturn Fund I shows 2.03x gross across 13 deals. Brightmere Dental made 42.5% of the gains. Without it the fund is 1.65x gross, which is ordinary once fees and carry come off.

Where the value came from

A value bridge splits each deal's equity gain into EBITDA growth, multiple change and debt paydown. EBITDA growth is the part a GP can call skill. Multiple expansion in a rising market is mostly the market.

Check the interim numbers

Re-ups get decided on interim marks. Hasturn Fund I was top quartile when Fund II was raised and is second quartile now. Among the synthetic 2016 peers in the demo, only 55% of top-quartile funds stayed there once their portfolios matured.

The record can still hide real skill. Hasturn's healthcare deals look like it, and I'd want two more realizations outside healthcare before committing.

The numbers come from the base case of the linked demo. The funds and managers are made up.
