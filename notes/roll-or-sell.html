Roll or sell, an LP's math on a continuation vehicle

How to compare the CV price with rolling, once the new fees, the carry reset and the longer hold are priced in.

Cristian Arenas28 October 2026Test it in the Secondaries Desk

In a GP-led continuation vehicle, an LP in the old fund can sell at the CV price or roll into the new vehicle. Rolling brings new fees, carry that restarts at the transaction price and a longer hold.

Set up the comparison

Take $1 of sale proceeds. If you sell, you reinvest it at your own expected return for the same period. If you roll, the $1 grows with the CV's assets, less the management fee and the carry.

The useful number is the gross multiple the assets need for rolling to match selling. In the Secondaries Desk, the Fenmoor CV charges 1.0% a year and 12.5% carry over an 8% preferred return, rising to 20% above 2.5x. Over four years at a 10% opportunity cost, the break-even is 1.57x gross.

Stress the hold

CVs usually come with extension options at the GP's discretion. A house case of 1.70x over four years clears the break-even by about 0.11x per dollar. Stretch the same multiple over six years and rolling loses 0.19x per dollar against selling.

Read the process

  • How many buyers bid, and was the fairness adviser paid on completion?
  • Does the GP take carry at the transaction price on the way in?
  • Is there a status quo option, and what happens if you don't reply?
  • Does the election window match the notice period in the LPA?

A low price hurts sellers and helps rollers. A weak process is a reason to push back on the terms whichever way you elect.

The numbers come from the base case of the linked demo. The funds and managers are made up.
