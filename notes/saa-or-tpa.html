SAA or total portfolio approach, what actually changes

A total portfolio approach swaps fixed asset class targets for a reference portfolio and a risk budget. Here is what that changes day to day.

Cristian Arenas28 October 2026Test it in the Allocation Lab

A strategic asset allocation sets a target and a range for each asset class. A total portfolio approach replaces those targets with a simple reference portfolio, usually stocks and bonds, and a risk budget around it.

What changes in practice

  • Every investment competes against the reference portfolio. A private equity fund gets judged by what it adds to total risk and return.
  • Rebalancing follows the risk budget. You trade when active risk gets too high.
  • Private assets enter through their exposures, so stale marks need explicit handling.

In the Allocation Lab, the endowment measures active risk against a 70/30 reference. After an equity fall it runs at 4.31% against a 4% budget. Most of it is the public equity underweight, which is the part you can trade.

What stays the same

Expected returns still drive the answer. The optimizer's best Sharpe ratio in the demo is 0.26 against 0.25 for the current policy, and the gain depends on the private equity return assumption. Liquidity rules still bind. After a GFC-sized stress the portfolio needs $254m of liquid assets and has $416m.

How I'd run it

Keep the policy ranges as guardrails and report against the reference portfolio. Desmooth private risk before it enters the risk budget. Use the budget to decide which trades go first.

The numbers come from the base case of the linked demo. The funds and managers are made up.
