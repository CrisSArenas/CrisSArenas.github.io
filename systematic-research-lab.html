How it works

Data-generating process, method, formulas, assumptions and limits
  • The data is synthetic. Sixty fictional stocks in six sectors start in January 1996 and run for 360 months. Each month's excess return is a market factor times the stock's beta, a sector shock, an idiosyncratic shock and an expected return built from four known premia. The market switches between a calm and a turbulent volatility state with fixed transition probabilities (3% a month from calm to turbulent, 12% back). The market factor earns 0.5% a month over cash with 3.8% monthly volatility when calm and 6.8% when turbulent; sector shocks have 2.2% monthly volatility and idiosyncratic volatility has a median of 7% a month, both 35% higher in turbulent months. Betas centre on 1 with a standard deviation of 0.25 and a 0.3 correlation with idiosyncratic volatility.
  • The premia are set in the assumptions register, per 1 cross-sectional standard deviation of the characteristic. Momentum pays on the 12-1 return, falls to a quarter of its size in turbulent months, and reverses sharply in turbulent months when the market rebounds. A market-level trend term gives time-series momentum, with the same weakening. Value pays on a true valuation spread that the researcher sees only with measurement noise. Low volatility pays on true idiosyncratic volatility, and reversal on last month's return. All premia shrink linearly by the decay setting over the 30 years, as crowding would shrink them.
  • Stocks delist. Each stock has a 0.08% monthly chance of delisting, rising by 1.5 percentage points after a fall of more than 40% over 12 months. A distressed stock leaves with the delisting return in the register (-25% by default); the others leave through takeovers at +15%. A new stock lists in the same sector the next month. Delisted stocks stay in the history up to their last month, delisting return included.
  • Momentum 12-1 is the log return from month t-12 to t-2, skipping the latest month. Time-series momentum goes long each stock with a positive 12-1 return and short each with a negative one, 1/N each, so its net exposure moves with the market. Short-term reversal is minus last month's return. Value is the valuation spread observed at the end of t-1. Low volatility is minus the volatility of the last 24 monthly returns. The blend is your weighted sum of the cross-sectional z-scores of the four cross-sectional signals.
  • Every signal for month t uses data through month t-1. A stock is eligible if it has 13 months of history and is still listed at the start of month t. Rank weighting holds every eligible stock, in proportion to its centred rank; quintile weighting holds the top and bottom fifths equally. Each raw book is one long and one short. Holding periods above one month average the last H monthly formations.
  • The combination fits weights across five strategies on each training window: momentum (the grid variant with the best training Sharpe), time-series momentum, reversal, value and low volatility. Mean-variance uses inverse covariance times mean, with the covariance shrunk halfway to its diagonal and negative weights dropped. The book is then scaled to the volatility target with a leverage cap, and each position is capped.
  • Costs are charged on turnover, measured against the weights the book drifted to over the month. A borrow fee is charged on the short side. Net returns are gross returns less both.
  • Validation is walk-forward: expanding training windows from 1999, refitted every five years, each tested on the following five years. Robustness adds the momentum grid, a circular block bootstrap of the out-of-sample Sharpe, the deflated Sharpe ratio for the number of trials, Holm and Bonferroni adjustments across the signals, placebo signals built on pure noise, a cost sweep, and a rerun on 20 seeds.

Biases the page guards against

  • Look-ahead bias is ruled out by construction: signals, regimes, volatility scaling and the walk-forward fits read only earlier months. A model test changes every return after month t and checks that the weights at t do not move, and the activity log repeats the check on the live data. The bias audit shows what a momentum signal that includes the current month would report.
  • Survivorship bias is avoided by keeping delisted stocks in the universe until they leave, with their delisting return. The bias audit reruns the research on only the stocks alive at the end, the dataset a careless download would give.
  • Data snooping is measured, since every trial run in sample is counted and the best Sharpe ratio is deflated for that number. Holm and Bonferroni adjust the signal tests, and the placebo signals show how good pure noise can look.
  • Overfitting is exposed by doing all selection and fitting on training windows and keeping the out-of-sample record apart. The in-sample figure and the gap are shown next to it.
  • Unrealistic costs are avoided by reporting every figure net of a stated trading cost and borrow fee unless it is labelled gross. The cost sweep shows the break-even cost.

Formulas

r_i,t = a_i,t + beta_i m_t + s_sector,t + sigma_i,t e_i,t
a_i,t = lam_mom,t z(MOM_i,t-1) - lam_rev z(r_i,t-1) + lam_val z(V*_i,t-1) - lam_lv z(sigma_i,t-1)
lam_mom,t = lam_mom (calm), 0.25 lam_mom (turbulent), lam_crash (turbulent month with market shock above +1 sd)
MOM(L)_i,t = sum over s = t-L .. t-2 of ln(1 + r_i,s)
rank weight w_i = (rank_i - (n+1)/2) / sum over rank_j > (n+1)/2 of (rank_j - (n+1)/2)
scale k_t = min(k_max, target / (sd(raw_t-36 .. raw_t-1) sqrt 12)),  |w_i,t| <= cap
turnover_t = sum_i |w_i,t - w_i,t-1 (1 + r_i,t-1)|
net_t = gross_t - c turnover_t - (b / 12) short_t
Sharpe = mean(net) / sd(net) sqrt 12,  Sortino = 12 mean / (sqrt(mean(min(r, 0)^2)) sqrt 12)
Calmar = annual return / |max drawdown|
r_t = alpha + beta b_t + e_t,  IR = 12 alpha / (sd(e) sqrt 12),  t = HAC t-stat of r_t - beta b_t
Fama-MacBeth: r_i,t = g_0,t + sum_k g_k,t z_k,i,t + u_i,t,  premium_k = mean_t g_k,t
PSR(SR*) = Phi((SR - SR*) sqrt(n - 1) / sqrt(1 - g3 SR + (g4 - 1) / 4 SR^2)),  SR monthly
SR0 = sqrt(V[SR_trials]) ((1 - gamma) Phi^-1(1 - 1/N) + gamma Phi^-1(1 - 1/(N e))),  DSR = PSR(SR0)
Holm: p_(k) adjusted = max over j <= k of min(1, (m - j + 1) p_(j));  Bonferroni: min(1, m p)
high-vol regime at t: sd(b_t-6 .. b_t-1) sqrt 12 > expanding q-quantile of its own past values

Assumptions

Data and limits

  • The stocks, sectors and returns are made up and the dates are labels. One seed is one history. The seed rerun on the Robustness tab shows how much the results move from one history to the next.
  • Sixty names is a small universe, so the books are concentrated and idiosyncratic risk is large. With more names the same premia would be measured with less noise.
  • Costs are linear in turnover. Real impact grows with trade size, so the cost drag at larger capital would be higher than shown.
  • Part of the measured momentum premium can come from the delisting returns of distressed losers, a channel the stated momentum premium does not include. The low-volatility signal uses trailing total volatility, which mixes in beta, so it recovers less of its premium.
  • The deflated Sharpe ratio uses the raw count of trials. The momentum variants are correlated, so the effective number of independent trials is smaller and the deflation is on the conservative side.
