How it works

Method, formulas, assumptions and limits
  • Sleeve and factor returns are simulated over ten years with calm and stress regimes. In stress months crowded trades fall together.
  • Equal risk contribution sets weights so each sleeve adds the same share of variance. Volatility targeting scales each sleeve to its mandate's target. A drawdown stop halves a sleeve for six months after a 10% fall.
  • Style drift is a change in a sleeve's factor loadings between the first seven years and the last 30 months. Correlation shifts are tested on calm months only.
  • Fees are charged monthly and performance fees crystallise once a year above the high-water mark. Netting cost is what sleeves pay in performance fees minus what one fee on the whole book would cost.
  • The portable alpha tab runs the original simulator's engine unchanged.

Formulas

ERC: w_i (S w)_i = w_j (S w)_j for all i, j vol target: scale_t = min(cap, target / vol_12m) drift z = (b_recent - b_base) / sqrt(se_r^2 + se_b^2) Fisher z = (atanh r1 - atanh r0) / sqrt(1/(n0-3) + 1/(n1-3)) overlap = b_A' S_f b_B / (vol_A vol_B) netting cost = sum_i w_i perf_i max(g_i, 0) - perf max(sum_i w_i g_i, 0)

Assumptions

Data and limits

  • The managers are made up. Five factors stand in for a full risk model.
  • Weights use the full ten-year covariance, so the backtest is in-sample. The risk controls do not look ahead.
