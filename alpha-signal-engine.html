Guide — Alpha Signal Discovery Engine

1 · What is simulated

A synthetic cross-sectional panel is generated once from a fixed seed: 400 names, 180 monthly dates, eight standardised factor exposures per name. Each exposure follows its own AR(1) process (its persistence is what drives turnover) and the eight series are mixed through the Cholesky root of a target correlation matrix, so the heat map reproduces the intended structure. Monthly stock returns are market + Σk λk,t · zk,t-1 + idiosyncratic, where λk is a random factor payoff whose mean is calibrated so each factor's univariate rank IC matches its target. Nothing in the UI is read back from that calibration table — every number shown is re-estimated from the simulated panel.

2 · Walk-forward protocol

The first 60 months are training-only warm-up. From month 61 the engine walks forward over 119 out-of-sample months: the model is refitted every 12 months on the trailing lookback window, scores every name at each month end, and holds a decile long/short book (top 40 long, bottom 40 short, equal weighted, dollar neutral, 50% gross) rebalanced at the chosen frequency. Only information available before the return month enters a fit, so there is no look-ahead.

3 · The four learners (all fitted in the browser)

ModelHow it is actually fittedImportance
Ridge (L2)Closed form w = (X'X/n + λI)-1 X'y/n on standardised exposures, λ = 0.15, from cached monthly Gram matrices|w|, normalised
Lasso (L1)Cyclic coordinate descent with soft thresholding on the same Gram matrices, penalty 0.012|w|, exact zeros allowed
Random Forest100 depth-1 trees; each bags the training months (block bootstrap) and searches 3 sampled features over 16 bins, target = cross-sectional rank of the forward returnshare of impurity (variance) decrease
Gradient Boost40 depth-1 stumps fitted sequentially to the residual rank on an 8,000-row draw, learning rate 0.15, 4 features sampled per stumpshare of squared-error reduction

These are deliberately small learners: honest implementations of the named algorithm restricted to stumps and eight features, not library-grade estimators. Because the data-generating process is linear, the linear models usually win — the same result most cross-sectional equity researchers report.

4 · Metrics

Mean IC
Average monthly Spearman rank correlation between the composite model score at month end and the cross-sectionally demeaned return of the following month. 0.03–0.06 is realistic for a composite.
IC IR
mean(IC) / std(IC) over the 119 monthly observations; the t-statistic is IC-IR × √T.
Gross / Net Sharpe
Sharpe of the funded strategy (benchmark + overlay): (12·mean − 3.8% cash) / (σ·√12). Net deducts transaction costs, so it always sits below gross.
Ann. Alpha
Annualised mean of the overlay return after costs. Its tracking error and information ratio are in the summary table.
Ann. Turnover
One-way turnover ½Σ|wt − wt-1| summed over a year, expressed as a share of gross exposure. Costs are charged on the full traded notional, i.e. twice the one-way turnover.
Max DD
Largest peak-to-trough fall of the net cumulative curve.
Hit Rate
Share of months with positive net alpha. 52–58% is normal even for good books.

5 · Reading the charts

Feature importance
Taken from the most recent fit of the selected model. Lasso can zero a factor out entirely; the forest spreads weight because each stump only sees a sampled subset of features.
Mean IC by factor
Univariate rank IC of each raw exposure. Whiskers are the 95% confidence interval (1.96 × std(IC) / √T); the t-statistic above the bar is the same quantity as a test against zero.
IC decay
IC of today's exposure against the return 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 months ahead. Decay is driven by the persistence of the exposure: momentum and sentiment fade fast, value, quality and size barely decay. The shaded band is the 95% interval around the equal-weighted mean of the selected factors.
Correlation matrix
Realised cross-sectional correlation of the exposures averaged over the evaluation window. Symmetric with a unit diagonal; value vs momentum is about −0.3, quality vs low vol about +0.45.
Cumulative growth
Benchmark, benchmark + gross overlay and benchmark + net overlay on the same seeded path. Red shading marks months below the running peak.
Turnover vs net Sharpe
The whole backtest re-priced at each cost level with the identical net-Sharpe formula, so the highlighted dot always equals the Net Sharpe tile.

6 · Controls

Factor universe
Restricts the feature set the model may use. Panel statistics (IC, decay, correlations) are properties of the data and do not move; everything downstream of the fit does.
Lookback
Length of the trailing training window, 12–60 months. Short windows track changing premia but are noisy; long windows are stable but stale.
Rebalance
Monthly, quarterly or semi-annual. Longer holding periods cut turnover and cost but let the signal go stale.
Transaction cost
One-way cost in basis points. 5–20 bp is typical for liquid large caps.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

A cross-sectional factor-research loop on a synthetic panel of 400 names over 180 months, generated once from seed 20260131. Eight standardised exposures per name follow their own AR(1) processes and are mixed through the Cholesky root of a target correlation matrix; returns are the equal-weighted market plus random monthly factor payoffs on the lagged exposures plus idiosyncratic noise. After a 60-month warm-up the engine walks forward over 119 out-of-sample months, refitting the selected learner every 12 months on the trailing lookback window, scoring the universe, and holding a decile long/short book rebalanced monthly, quarterly or semi-annually. Rank IC, IC decay, realised correlations, feature importance, turnover, costs and drawdowns are all measured on the simulated data.

Key assumptions

  • Universe 400 names, 180 months, 60 warm-up, 119 evaluation months; idiosyncratic stock vol 8.5%/month (≈29% p.a.).
  • Benchmark: equal-weighted market moment-matched to 7.0% return / 14.5% volatility over the evaluation window; cash 3.8% p.a.
  • Target univariate rank IC: momentum 0.070, sentiment 0.057, value 0.045, quality 0.035, low vol 0.030, macro 0.016, growth 0.015, size 0.012; monthly IC volatility 0.11–0.22, so per-factor IC-IR lands at 0.1–0.3.
  • Exposure persistence sets turnover: sentiment ≈250% and momentum ≈210% annual one-way, low vol and growth ≈120%, value ≈80%, quality ≈75%, size ≈40%.
  • Book: top and bottom decile, equal weighted, dollar neutral, 50% gross. Costs are charged on the full traded notional (2 × one-way turnover × cost in bp).
  • The executed book carries a residual common shock of 1.6% per month at the default gross (sector, crowding and single-name risk not spanned by the eight styles); without it a 400-name book would show an implausible information ratio.
  • Defaults: Random Forest, 36-month lookback, monthly rebalance, 15 bp one-way cost, all eight factors enabled.

Formulas & references

  • Rank ICt = Spearman(scoret, rt+1); IC-IR = mean(IC)/std(IC); t = IC-IR · √T; SE of the mean IC = std(IC)/√T, drawn as ±1.96 SE.
  • Ridge: w = (X'X/n + λI)^-1 X'y/n. Lasso: coordinate descent with soft threshold. Stumps: exhaustive 16-bin search maximising S²L/nL + S²R/nR − S²/n; importance is that gain, normalised.
  • Turnovert = ½Σi|wi,t − wi,t-1|; costt = 2 · turnovert · bp; Sharpe = (12·mean − rf)/(σ√12); IR = annualised alpha / tracking error; max drawdown from the cumulative curve.
  • Grinold & Kahn (2000), Active Portfolio Management (IC, breadth, the fundamental law); Fama & French (2015), A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model; Gu, Kelly & Xiu (2020), Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning.

Limitations

  • Synthetic Gaussian data, not history: no fat tails, no factor crashes, no crowding dynamics, no regime timing.
  • The learners are stump- and eight-feature-restricted browser implementations, so the model comparison is indicative rather than a benchmark of the real algorithms. The true process is linear, which structurally favours ridge and lasso.
  • No borrow cost or short constraint, no capacity or market impact, no risk model, no sector or beta neutralisation, one blended cost per name.
  • Costs are linear in turnover; in practice they are concave in participation rate and much higher for small caps.
  • Backtest Sharpe ratios of this kind routinely halve out of sample; the residual-risk term is a calibration device, not an estimated quantity.
