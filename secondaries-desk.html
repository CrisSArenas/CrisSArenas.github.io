06 · Secondaries and GP-leds

Secondaries Desk

Two decisions from the same desk. A fictional LP is selling six fund interests on NAVs dated 31 March; what should a buyer bid, as a share of NAV? And a fictional GP is moving two companies from its fourth fund into a continuation vehicle; should an LP in that fund roll or sell? The desk projects the cash flows, solves for the price, and reads the CV documents for the conflicts. The bid and the election are the team's call.

Method
Takahashi-Alexander projection by strategy, present value at the buyer's target IRR, beta roll-forward of stale NAVs, seeded growth shocks for the outcome range, continuation vehicle fees and tiered carry, break-even exit multiple.
Data
Synthetic LP portfolio and CV documents, fixed seed, fictional funds and companies. Or your own fund interests, processed locally.
Where AI is used
A terms extractor, a conflicts checklist builder and a memo drafter, with every checklist item approved by a person before it reaches the memo.
Limitations
Cash flows come from a model, not from the underlying companies. The CV is a single exit at a gross multiple. The pipeline is a simulation; no model is called.

          Methodology, assumptions and limitations

          Method

          • Projection. Each interest is projected quarterly from its NAV, unfunded commitment and age with Takahashi-Alexander rules: calls at a rate of the unfunded during the investment period, NAV growth, and a distribution rate that rises with age until the fund's life ends.
          • Stale NAVs. The 31 March NAV is moved to 30 June by the strategy's beta times the public proxy's return over the quarter, plus calls and less distributions paid since.
          • Price. The price is the present value of the projected net cash flows at the buyer's target IRR, so buying at that price earns exactly the target if the projection holds. Discount = 1 - price / NAV.
          • Outcome range. Each path draws one growth shock per fund and reprojects the stakes; the chart shows the buyer's IRR at the bid across paths.
          • Roll vs sell. Rolling invests the sale price in the CV, which grows to a gross multiple over the hold, less the management fee and tiered carry over the preferred return. Selling reinvests the cash at the LP's opportunity cost. The break-even multiple sets the two equal.

          Formulas

          call_q = (1 - (1 - rc)^(1/4)) x unfunded NAV_q = (NAV + call)(1 + g)^(1/4) RD(age) = (age / life)^bow price = sum (D_t - C_t) / (1 + r)^t NAV_today = NAV_ref (1 + beta x R_pub) + calls - dists roll = m - fee x hold - carry sell = (1 + opp)^hold

          Limitations

          • Every fund, GP, company and number is fictional. Cash flows come from a fund-level model; a real bid would underwrite the largest companies one by one.
          • The public proxy return for the quarter is an assumption on this page, not market data.
          • The CV is modelled as a single exit after the hold, with fees paid at exit; extensions are shown as longer holds at the same multiple.
