Investment memo 01 · Commitment pacing

Private markets commitment plan

Date
30 Sep 2026
Program
Fictional endowment, $1bn of total assets, 20 private funds
Strategy
Buyout, growth, venture, private credit, secondaries and real assets
Commitment budget under review
$168m over three vintages, $59m of it in 2027 (pacing rule, base case)
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Commit $168m across the 2027 to 2029 vintages, starting with $59m in 2027. Keep at least $160m in cash and short Treasuries.

Conditions

  • No re-up with Brisolt until the GP explains the $0.70m roll-forward break on the Brisolt Credit Opportunities III statement.
  • No re-up with Hasturn until we know whether the key person clause in Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout II has triggered.
  • The plan is rerun on the Q3 2026 marks when they arrive.

Summary

The endowment has $542m committed to 20 funds, $148m unfunded. GP marks dated 30 June 2026 or earlier put private NAV at $265m, 26.5% of total assets against a 25% target and a 20% to 30% band. Public markets have fallen since, so that share is overstated. I support the pacing rule's budget for four reasons.

  • Rolled forward to today, private NAV is $232m and the allocation is 24.0%, a little under target.
  • $59m in 2027, then $55.7m and $53.1m, keeps private NAV near 25% while older funds distribute. The plan path stays between 24.2% and 25.6% for ten years.
  • After a further 25% public drawdown, liquid public assets cover the stressed 12-month net cash need of $79m 4.9 times.
  • The main weakness, band breaches in a sell-off, is a denominator effect the program can ride out or trim with a secondary sale.

The program

What we own

Twenty funds from the 2017 to 2025 vintages. Called capital is $394.4m and $209.2m has come back, a 0.53x DPI. The 2021 vintage is the largest at 27%, then 2024 at 22%. Ostrel is the largest GP exposure at 15.0% of private NAV, through Ostrel Capital Partners VI and VII.

Re-up calendar

Six GPs expect to be in market over the next 24 months with prior commitments of $175m. The 2027 and 2028 budget is $114.9m, so the plan allows about two thirds of each prior ticket (Appendix D). I treat these as ceilings, since Brisolt and Hasturn carry open issues. For 2027 I would make two or three commitments of $20m to $25m: re-ups from the calendar and one secondaries fund to shorten the J-curve.

Performance and the pacing model

Program performance

On nowcast NAV the program shows a 1.12x TVPI and a 4.5% IRR. It is ahead of the public proxy, with a KS-PME of 1.07 and Direct Alpha of +2.2%. The program has earned its illiquidity, though modestly. Dispersion is wide. Caldrin Infrastructure II (1.73x) and Ivrena Healthcare Partners I (1.58x) carry the result, while Ostrel Capital Partners VI sits at 0.94x and a 0.6% IRR.

Reported returns are smoothed. For Quorvane Buyout III, autocorrelation of 0.42 means desmoothing lifts volatility from 11.1% to 17.2%. Allocation work should use the higher figure.

The NAV nowcast

The nowcast regresses each fund's reported returns on a lagged public index and rolls the last mark forward with the cash flows since. Program NAV falls 12.2%, from $264.8m to $232.4m, with a 90% band of $214m to $250m. Even the top of that band sits below reported NAV. Five funds, Ostrel Capital Partners VII among them, use a strategy prior for beta, so their estimates are the softest. Room below the 30% band grows from $35m to $58m.

The pacing model and its fan

Each fund follows the Takahashi-Alexander model, and each year's commitment is set so projected NAV sits on target four years out. One fund under the defaults returns 1.49x net and a 7.0% net IRR, with a 66% peak outlay and break-even in year 8. Steady state needs 3.98% of total assets a year. The near-term budget runs above that because existing funds distribute more than they call, $56.4m against $37.2m in 2027.

The Monte Carlo holds the plan fixed over 600 paths and 40 quarters. The median allocation in three years is 24.7%, with a 10th to 90th range of 19% to 31%. In 62% of paths the allocation crosses 30% in some quarter, almost always after a public drawdown. In 17% liquid public assets drop below unfunded commitments. The first number is about the denominator. The second is the one that needs a buffer.

Portfolio fit

Liquidity under stress

The stress case cuts public assets 25%, marks private NAV down 10% on a lag, calls 35% of unfunded in 12 months and cuts distributions 60%. The 12-month net cash need, calls less distributions plus spending, is $79m. Liquid public assets cover it 4.9 times, and the $160m buffer is about twice it.

The allocation is the weaker side. On lagged marks it jumps to 30.2% and settles at 27.5% once the markdown comes through. Selling 10% of the private book at a 12% discount raises $21m, costs $3m against NAV and brings it to 27.3%. I would pay that only if the breach persisted after the Q3 and Q4 marks.

The evergreen option

A semi-liquid evergreen fund would fill the allocation without a J-curve. The lab stresses one with requests of 12.0% of NAV a quarter against a 5.0% limit. It gated in 9 of 12 quarters, the queue peaked at $198m, and $182m of assets were sold at a 15% discount, costing holders who stayed $27.3m. It offers little liquidity in the scenario where the endowment would want it, so I would not count one toward the cash buffer.

Steady vintages of $52m to $64m a year should dilute the 2021 and 2024 concentration. Holding Ostrel's 2028 re-up at $29.6m keeps the largest GP exposure from growing.

Diligence findings

Six GP documents for five funds were read: four capital account statements, a quarterly report and a call notice. 42 fields were extracted, each tied to its page and line, and 49 checks were run. Against 43 hand-labeled fields, accuracy and citation coverage were both 97.7%, the one planted issue was caught and there were no false flags.

Breaks and flags

  • Brisolt Credit Opportunities III does not roll forward. Opening $22.15m plus contributions $0.90m less distributions $1.35m plus net income after fees $0.65m gives $22.35m, against a stated closing NAV of $23.05m. The fund is held out of the ledger until the GP explains the $0.70m. I would also ask about the PIK interest the statement notes.
  • The Mereth Ventures V fee of $56,250 sits on the line below its label and was left blank. Note 3 puts it inside net income, so the roll-forward ties. It will be recorded by hand.
  • Quorvane Buyout IV's $32,519,000 ending balance matches its quarterly report.

Inconsistencies to resolve

Quorvane Buyout IV reports a fund-level DPI of 0.71x. Our position shows 0.43x, a gap the nowcast cannot explain, so I would ask for our cash flow history. The program table carries Brisolt Credit Opportunities III at $15.3m NAV and $7.3m unfunded, against $23.05m and $5.40m on the statement. That must be reconciled before a Brisolt re-up is sized.

On the watchlist, three funds are marked two quarters stale, a named key person has left Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout II, and Ostrel Capital Partners VI charges a fee above the LPA rate after the step-down.

What would change the view

  • Q3 marks that put the allocation back near 26.5%, with room under the band near $35m. I would cut the 2027 budget.
  • A rerun with more than 17% of paths short of liquidity, or stressed coverage well below 4.9x. I would raise the cash floor first.
  • 2027 distributions well below the planned $56.4m.
  • The Hasturn clause triggering, or no answer on the Brisolt break. That allowance would move to another name.

Next steps

  1. Approve the $59m 2027 budget and the $160m cash floor.
  2. Send the Brisolt roll-forward query and hold the fund out of the ledger until it is answered.
  3. Confirm with Hasturn whether the key person clause has triggered.
  4. Record the Mereth Ventures V fee by hand from the cited line.
  5. Ask Quorvane for our cash flow history and follow up the Ostrel VI fee query.
  6. Source one secondaries fund and two re-ups of $20m to $25m for 2027.
  7. Rerun the nowcast and the pacing plan when the Q3 marks arrive.
