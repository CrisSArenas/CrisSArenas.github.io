Investment memo 01 · Commitment pacing

Private markets commitment plan

Date
30 Sep 2026
Program
Fictional endowment, $1bn of total assets, 20 private funds
Strategy
Buyout, growth, venture, private credit, secondaries and real assets
Commitment budget under review
$174m over three vintages, $63m of it in 2027 (pacing rule, base case)
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Commit $174m across the 2027 to 2029 vintages, starting with $63m in 2027. Keep at least $130m in cash and short Treasuries.

Conditions

  • No re-up with Brisolt until the GP explains the $0.70m roll-forward break on the Brisolt Credit Opportunities III statement.
  • No re-up with Hasturn until we know whether the key person clause in Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout II has triggered.
  • The plan is rerun on the Q3 2026 marks when they arrive.

Summary

The endowment has $502m committed to 20 funds, $114m unfunded. GP marks dated 30 June 2026 or earlier put private NAV at $276m, 27.6% of total assets against a 25% target and a 20% to 30% band. Public markets have fallen since, so that share is overstated. I support the pacing rule's budget for four reasons.

  • Rolled forward to today, private NAV is $256m and the allocation is 26.1%, a little over target.
  • $63m in 2027, then $57.5m and $53.7m, keeps private NAV near 25% while older funds distribute. The plan path stays between 23.9% and 25.7% for ten years.
  • After a further 25% public drawdown, liquid public assets cover the stressed 12-month net cash need of $64m 6.0 times.
  • The main weakness, band breaches in a sell-off, is a denominator effect the program can ride out or trim with a secondary sale.

The program

What we own

Twenty funds from the 2017 to 2025 vintages. Called capital is $388.4m and $240.3m has come back, a 0.62x DPI. The 2021 vintage is the largest at 26% of private NAV, then 2024 at 17%. Ostrel is the largest GP exposure at 15.7% of private NAV, through Ostrel Capital Partners VI and VII.

Re-up calendar

Six GPs expect to be in market over the next 24 months with prior commitments of $155m. The 2027 and 2028 budget is $120.3m, so the plan allows about three quarters of each prior ticket (Appendix D). I treat these as ceilings, since Brisolt and Hasturn carry open issues. For 2027 I would make two or three commitments of $20m to $25m: re-ups from the calendar and one secondaries fund to shorten the J-curve.

Performance and the pacing model

Program performance

On nowcast NAV the program shows a 1.28x TVPI and a 9.2% net IRR, with nearly a quarter of its NAV in funds still in their first three years. It is ahead of the public proxy, with a KS-PME of 1.11 and Direct Alpha of +3.5%, so the program has earned its illiquidity. The mature buyout and venture funds carry the result: Mereth Ventures IV at 1.65x, Quorvane Buyout III at 1.61x and Ostrel Capital Partners VI at 1.58x. The 2020 to 2022 growth and venture funds lag, with Vandrel Growth Equity III at 0.95x and Mereth Ventures V at 0.73x.

Reported returns are smoothed. For Quorvane Buyout III, autocorrelation of 0.44 means desmoothing lifts volatility from 8.9% to 14.2%. Allocation work should use the higher figure.

The NAV nowcast

The nowcast regresses each fund's reported returns on a lagged public index, shrinks the betas toward a strategy prior, and rolls the last mark forward with the cash flows since. Program NAV falls 7.4%, from $276.0m to $255.6m, with a 90% band of $237m to $275m. Even the top of that band sits just below reported NAV. Five funds, Ostrel Capital Partners VII among them, have too little history and use the strategy prior alone, so their estimates are the softest. Room below the 30% band grows from $24m to $38m.

The pacing model and its fan

Each fund follows the Takahashi-Alexander model, and each year's commitment is set so projected NAV sits on target four years out. One fund under the defaults returns 1.94x on called capital and an 11.0% IRR, the NAV growth the plan uses, which I treat as net of fees. If 11% were the gross return instead, a 2% fee and 20% carry would bring the fund to 1.49x and 7.0%. Before fees the peak outlay is 66% of commitment and the fund breaks even in year 8. Steady state needs 3.98% of total assets a year. The near-term budget runs above that because existing funds distribute more than they call, $71.5m against $36.3m in 2027.

The Monte Carlo holds the plan fixed over 600 paths and 40 quarters. The median allocation in three years is 24.2%, with a 10th to 90th range of 18% to 31%. In 63% of paths the allocation crosses 30% in some quarter, almost always after a public drawdown. In 12% liquid public assets drop below unfunded commitments. The first number is about the denominator. The second is the one that needs a buffer.

Portfolio fit

Liquidity under stress

The stress case cuts public assets 25%, marks private NAV down 10% on a lag, calls 35% of unfunded in 12 months and cuts distributions 60%. The 12-month net cash need, calls less distributions plus spending, is $64m. Liquid public assets cover it 6.0 times, and the $130m buffer is about twice it.

The allocation is the weaker side. On lagged marks it jumps to 31.4% and settles at 29.8% once the markdown comes through. Secondary discounts widen in a sell-off, so I assume a 20% discount, wider than the 5% to 15% of normal markets. Selling 10% of the private book at that discount raises $20m, costs $5m against NAV and brings the allocation to 28.4%. I would pay that only if the breach persisted after the Q3 and Q4 marks.

The evergreen option

A semi-liquid evergreen fund would fill the allocation without a J-curve. The lab stresses one with requests of 12.0% of NAV a quarter against a 5.0% limit. It gated in 9 of 12 quarters, the queue peaked at $198m, and $182m of assets were sold at a 15% discount, costing holders who stayed $27.3m. It offers little liquidity in the scenario where the endowment would want it, so I would not count one toward the cash buffer.

Steady vintages of $52m to $65m a year should dilute the 2021 and 2024 concentration. Holding Ostrel's 2028 re-up at $31.0m keeps the largest GP exposure from growing.

Diligence findings

Six GP documents for five funds were read: four capital account statements, a quarterly report and a call notice. 42 fields were extracted, each tied to its page and line, and 49 checks were run. Against 43 verified fields, accuracy and citation coverage were both 97.7%, the one planted issue was caught and there were no false flags.

Breaks and flags

  • Brisolt Credit Opportunities III does not roll forward. Opening $22.15m plus contributions $0.90m less distributions $1.35m plus net income after fees $0.65m gives $22.35m, against a stated closing NAV of $23.05m. The fund is held out of the ledger until the GP explains the $0.70m. I would also ask about the PIK interest the statement notes.
  • The Mereth Ventures V fee of $56,250 sits on the line below its label and was left blank. Note 3 puts it inside net income, so the roll-forward ties. It will be recorded by hand.
  • Quorvane Buyout IV's $32,519,000 ending balance matches its quarterly report.

Inconsistencies to resolve

Quorvane Buyout IV reports a fund-level DPI of 0.38x. Our records show 0.56x on the same paid-in capital, more than two partial realisations would return, so I would ask for our cash flow history. The program table carries Brisolt Credit Opportunities III at $14.1m NAV and $7.0m unfunded, against $23.05m and $5.40m on the statement. That must be reconciled before a Brisolt re-up is sized.

On the watchlist, three funds are marked two quarters stale, a named key person has left Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout II, and Ostrel Capital Partners VI charges a fee above the LPA rate after the step-down.

What would change the view

  • Q3 marks that put the allocation back near 27.6%, with room under the band near $24m. I would cut the 2027 budget.
  • A rerun with more than 12% of paths short of liquidity, or stressed coverage well below 6.0x. I would raise the cash floor first.
  • 2027 distributions well below the planned $71.5m, which is already 43% above the trailing $50m.
  • The Hasturn clause triggering, or no answer on the Brisolt break. That allowance would move to another name.

Next steps

  1. Approve the $63m 2027 budget and the $130m cash floor.
  2. Send the Brisolt roll-forward query and hold the fund out of the ledger until it is answered.
  3. Confirm with Hasturn whether the key person clause has triggered.
  4. Record the Mereth Ventures V fee by hand from the cited line.
  5. Ask Quorvane for our cash flow history and follow up the Ostrel VI fee query.
  6. Source one secondaries fund and two re-ups of $20m to $25m for 2027.
  7. Rerun the nowcast and the pacing plan when the Q3 marks arrive.
