Investment memo 01 · Commitment pacing

2027 to 2029 private markets commitment plan

Date
30 Sep 2026
Program
Fictional endowment, $1bn of total assets, 20 private funds
Strategy
Buyout, growth, venture, private credit, secondaries and real assets
Commitment budget under review
$168m over three vintages, $59m of it in 2027 (pacing rule, base case)
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Commit $168m across the 2027 to 2029 vintages, starting with $59m in 2027. Keep at least $160m in cash and short Treasuries.

Conditions

  • No re-up with Brisolt until the GP explains the $0.70m roll-forward break on the Brisolt Credit Opportunities III statement.
  • No re-up with Hasturn until we know whether the key person clause in Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout II has triggered.
  • The plan is rerun on the Q3 2026 marks when they arrive.

Summary

The endowment has $542m committed to 20 funds, $148m of it unfunded. The latest GP marks put private NAV at $265m, or 26.5% of total assets against a 25% target and a 20% to 30% band. Those marks are dated 30 June 2026 or earlier. Public markets have fallen since, so the reported share overstates where the program really sits.

This memo sets the commitment budget for the next three vintages. I support the pacing rule's output for four reasons.

  • Rolled forward to today, private NAV is $232m and the allocation is 24.0%, a little under target. The reported 26.5% would have argued for a smaller budget than the program needs.
  • $59m in 2027, then $55.7m and $53.1m, keeps projected private NAV near 25% four years out while the older funds distribute. The deterministic plan stays between 24.2% and 25.6% for the next ten years.
  • Liquidity holds up. After a further 25% public drawdown, liquid public assets cover the stressed 12-month net cash need of $79m 4.9 times.
  • The plan's main weakness is band breaches in a sell-off. Those are a denominator effect that the program can ride out or trim with a secondary sale.

The program

What we own

Twenty funds across buyout, growth, venture, private credit, secondaries and real assets, from the 2017 to 2025 vintages. Called capital is $394.4m and $209.2m has come back. The 2021 vintage is the largest at 27% of the program, followed by 2024 at 22% and 2022 at 17%. Ostrel is the largest GP exposure at 15.0% of private NAV, through Ostrel Capital Partners VI and VII.

The book is young in cash terms. Program DPI is 0.53x, so roughly half of what has been called is still sitting in NAV. That matters for pacing, because the size of next year's distributions drives how much room there is to commit.

Re-up calendar

Six GPs expect to be in market over the next 24 months, with prior commitments totalling $175m. The 2027 and 2028 budget is $114.9m, so the plan allows about two thirds of each prior ticket: $19.7m for Brisolt, Hasturn and Solvane, $9.9m for Mereth, $29.6m for Ostrel and $16.4m for Kestrine. I would treat these as ceilings. Two of the six names carry open issues today (Brisolt and Hasturn), and Ostrel already holds the largest share of NAV.

For 2027 I would make two or three commitments of $20m to $25m: re-ups from the calendar and one secondaries fund to shorten the J-curve.

Performance and the pacing model

Program performance

On nowcast NAV the program shows a 1.12x TVPI, 0.53x DPI and a 4.5% IRR. Against the public proxy it is ahead, with a KS-PME of 1.07 and Direct Alpha of +2.2%. The low absolute IRR reflects the young book and a weak public tape since the last marks. The relative numbers say the program has earned its illiquidity, though only modestly.

Dispersion across funds is wide. Caldrin Infrastructure II (1.73x, 16.4%), Ivrena Healthcare Partners I (1.58x, 15.9%) and Quorvane Buyout IV (1.28x, 13.8%) carry the result. Ostrel Capital Partners VI sits at 0.94x and 0.6% after a 2019 vintage, and Mereth Ventures V is at 0.70x. Both are on the watchlist.

Reported returns are smoothed. For Quorvane Buyout III the first-order autocorrelation is 0.42, and desmoothing raises annual volatility from 11.1% to 17.2%. I would use the desmoothed figure in any allocation work.

The NAV nowcast

The nowcast regresses each fund's reported returns on a lagged public index and rolls the last mark forward with the cash flows since. Program NAV falls 12.2%, from $264.8m to $232.4m, with a 90% band of $214m to $250m. Even the top of that band leaves the allocation below the reported figure. Five funds use a strategy prior because they lack enough history for their own beta, Ostrel Capital Partners VII among them, so their estimates are the softest.

The effect on headroom is large. Room below the 30% upper band is $35m on reported marks and $58m on the nowcast.

The pacing model and its fan

Each fund follows the Takahashi-Alexander model, and each year's commitment is set so projected private NAV sits on target four years out. A single fund under the default parameters returns 1.94x gross and 1.49x net, a 7.0% net IRR, peaks at a 66% net outlay and breaks even in year 8. In steady state the program needs 3.98% of total assets a year. The near-term budget sits above that because existing funds are distributing more than they call: $56.4m against $37.2m in 2027.

The Monte Carlo holds the plan fixed and draws public returns, private returns, calls and distributions together over 600 paths and 40 quarters. The median allocation in three years is 24.7%, with a 10th to 90th range of 19% to 31%. In 62% of paths the allocation crosses the 30% upper band in at least one quarter, almost always right after a public drawdown. In 17% of paths liquid public assets drop below unfunded commitments at some point. I read the first number as a statement about the denominator and the second as the one that needs a buffer.

Portfolio fit

Liquidity under stress

The stress case takes public assets down 25%, marks private NAV down 10% on a lag, calls 35% of unfunded over 12 months and cuts distributions by 60%. Public assets fall from $735m to $551m and distributions drop from $40m to $16m. The stressed 12-month net cash need, calls less distributions plus spending, is $79m. Liquid public assets cover it 4.9 times, and liquid assets cover the remaining unfunded 4.02x. The $160m buffer is about twice the stressed need.

The allocation is the weaker side. On lagged marks the private share jumps to 30.2%, above the band, and settles at 27.5% once the markdown comes through. If we wanted to act before marks catch up, selling 10% of the private book at a 12% discount raises $21m, costs $3m against NAV and brings the allocation to 27.3%. I would only pay that price if the breach persisted after the Q3 and Q4 marks.

The evergreen option

A semi-liquid evergreen fund is one way to fill the allocation without a J-curve. I looked at how one made-up fund behaves when requests jump to 12.0% of NAV a quarter against a 5.0% limit, with a 10% liquidity sleeve and a credit facility of 5% of NAV. It gated in 9 of 12 quarters. The queue peaked at $198m, or 19.8% of starting NAV. It sold $182m of private assets at a 15% discount, and the cost to holders who stayed was $27.3m, or 2.73% of starting NAV. Those terms give no real liquidity in the scenario where the endowment would want it. I would not count an evergreen fund toward the cash buffer or use one to replace drawdown commitments in this plan.

Exposures

The plan keeps vintage spread even at roughly $52m to $64m a year for a decade, which should dilute the 2021 and 2024 concentration over time. Capping Ostrel's 2028 re-up at $29.6m keeps the largest GP exposure from growing.

Diligence findings

I read six Q2 2026 GP documents for five funds: four capital account statements in different formats, one quarterly report and one capital call notice. 42 fields were extracted and every figure was tied to its page and line. 49 checks were run. Measured against a hand-labeled set of 43 fields, extraction accuracy was 97.7%, citation coverage was 97.7%, the one planted issue was caught and there were no false flags.

Breaks and flags

  • Brisolt Credit Opportunities III does not roll forward. Opening $22.15m plus contributions $0.90m less distributions $1.35m plus net income after fees $0.65m gives $22.35m. The statement shows a closing NAV of $23.05m, a $0.70m break. The fund is held out of the ledger until the GP answers. The statement also notes PIK interest inside income, which I would ask about in the same query.
  • The Mereth Ventures V management fee of $56,250 was left blank because the value sits on the line below its label. Note 3 says the fee is already inside net income, so the roll-forward ties. It needs to be recorded by hand from the cited line.
  • Quorvane Buyout IV ties. The statement's $32,519,000 ending balance matches the capital account in the quarterly report.
  • The Caldrin Infrastructure II call notice No. 14 asks for $2,975,000 due September 16, 2026, leaving $9,450,000 unfunded.

Inconsistencies to resolve

Quorvane Buyout IV reports a fund-level net TVPI of 1.62x and DPI of 0.71x. Our own position shows 1.28x and 0.43x. Part of the gap is the nowcast markdown, but the DPI gap is not explained by marks and I would ask the GP for our cash flow history. The program table still carries Brisolt Credit Opportunities III at $15.3m reported NAV and $7.3m unfunded, against $23.05m and $5.40m on the statement. That needs to be reconciled before any Brisolt re-up is sized.

Watchlist

Ostrel Capital Partners VI, Pellidor Real Estate Value II and Lorvenne Ventures III are marked two quarters stale. A named key person has left Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout II. Ostrel Capital Partners VI is charging a management fee above the LPA rate after the step-down, and a query has gone to the GP.

What would change the view

  • Q3 marks that put the allocation back near the reported 26.5%. Room under the band would shrink toward $35m and I would cut the 2027 budget.
  • A rerun in which more than 17% of paths show liquid assets below unfunded, or stressed coverage falling well below 4.9x. I would raise the cash floor before cutting commitments.
  • Distributions running well below the $56.4m plan for 2027. The plan depends on older funds returning cash faster than new funds call it.
  • The Hasturn key person clause triggering, or no satisfactory answer on the Brisolt break. The allowance would go to the secondaries fund or another calendar name.
  • A stressed allocation that stays above 30% after the markdown flows through. I would then use the LP stake sale.
