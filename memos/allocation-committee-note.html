Investment memo 09 · Allocation note

Allocation committee note

Date
30 September 2026
Portfolio
Kestrel Ridge College Endowment
Strategy
Policy portfolio of 10 sleeves, with tactical tilts and quarterly rebalancing
Under review
$1,182.9m of assets; tilts sized to a 0.50% tracking error budget; 1 drafted trade of $38.1m and a $15.2m futures overlay
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Adopt the sized tactical targets for Q4 2026, with every view scaled by 0.76 to fit the 0.50% tracking error budget. Approve the trade list as drafted: sell $38.1m of US equity to restore cash, and sell $15.2m of US equity index futures as a beta-adjusted overlay against the private equity overweight.

Conditions

  • Staff confirm the quarter's spending, calls and distributions before execution.
  • The overlay is resized on the Q4 private statements and removed once private equity is back under its 18% limit.
  • Overlay margin is paid from cash, and staff report if cash nears its 1% floor.

Summary

The Kestrel Ridge College Endowment held $1,182.9m at 30 September 2026 across 10 sleeves. Two sit outside their ranges: private equity at 18.3% against an 18% ceiling, and cash at 0.9% against a 1% floor after the third-quarter capital calls. I support the tilts and the trades for four reasons.

  • Cash is the binding problem. Spending and net calls take $25.0m against $10.3m on hand. The US equity sale restores cash to its 2.0% target.
  • The trade list is small and cures both breaches. One sale of $38.1m, 3.3% of assets, costs $7.6k, and the list passes 4 of 4 checks.
  • Ex-ante tracking error of current weights against policy is 1.11%. Private equity contributes 0.71% of it and US equity 0.26%, so the active risk is an equity overweight held mostly in private funds. Private equity cannot be sold, so the overlay is the only lever on it this quarter.
  • The tilts fit the budget. Scaled from 0.66% to 0.50% of tracking error, they add 8 bps a year.

The portfolio

The endowment is a fund of about $1.2bn. It holds seven liquid sleeves through single managers or directly, private equity through 14 buyout and growth funds and real assets through 6 real asset funds. At quarter end 74.3% of assets were liquid. Public sleeves trade daily; Halvergate Absolute Return deals quarterly.

Each sleeve has a policy target and range, listed in the appendix. Cash is the balancing account, with a 2% target and a 1% to 5% range. Liquid sleeves outside their range trade to the nearest edge. Private sleeves are never traded to rebalance; when private equity sits above its range, US equity index futures are sold against the excess. Trades under $2.0m are dropped unless they cure a breach.

The fund rebalances only at the range edges, so weights inside their ranges carry the drift of several quarters. Public equity sits above target after a rally, bonds below after a sell-off, and private equity above after strong marks. Those marks lag public markets, so private weights are estimates until the next statements arrive.

Analysis

Drift and breaches

Private equity is the largest gap, 0.3 pp above its upper limit and 3.3 pp above target. Cash is 0.1 pp under its floor. Inside their ranges, US equity is 1.9 pp above its 22% target, while Core bonds are 2.0 pp under, US Treasuries 1.8 pp under and Hedge funds 1.4 pp under.

Active risk against policy

Current weights carry 1.11% a year of ex-ante tracking error against the policy targets. Private equity contributes 0.71%, about two thirds, and US equity 0.26%. Emerging markets adds 0.08% and the bond underweights 0.06% together, while the Hedge funds underweight offsets 0.05%. The figures use desmoothed volatility: 22.5% for private equity against a reported 13%, and 16.7% for real assets against 9%. On reported volatility the total would read 0.80%. I read the drift as one position, about 5.7 pp of extra equity across private equity, US equity and emerging markets, held mostly in private funds and funded by bonds, hedge funds and cash.

Tactical views and sizing

My views are underweight US equity and Core bonds, and overweight International, Emerging markets and US Treasuries. They sum to zero. Private equity and real assets take no tilt, and I leave Hedge funds at policy because redemptions need notice. As entered the views carry 0.66% of tracking error, so each is scaled by 0.76, which keeps their shape. Every tactical target is inside its range, though Emerging markets at 8.29% sits near its 9% ceiling and Core bonds at 7.71% near the 7% floor. Emerging markets and International equity carry most of the tilt risk, at 0.42% and 0.27%.

Expected return and risk

The tilts add 8 bps a year, 0.15 per unit of tracking error. Expected return moves from 6.46% to 6.54% and volatility from 10.92% to 11.22%. That pickup is modest. Their main use this quarter is to steer the trades: the rebalance sells the sleeve furthest above its tactical target, which is why the cash comes from US equity.

Portfolio fit

The quarter's cash flows

In Q4 2026 the college draws $15.0m. Private equity calls $20.0m and distributes $12.0m; real assets call $6.0m and distribute $4.0m. Net calls are $10.0m. Cash would fall from $10.3m to -$14.7m, or -1.3%, before any trade, and the calls lift private equity to 19.3%.

Cash after the quarter

The US equity sale raises $38.1m. Cash takes it, pays the costs, and ends at $23.4m, 2.0% of assets. Assets after spending and costs are $1,167.9m. US equity ends at 21.0%, still above its 18.9% tactical target.

The overlay

Private equity's excess over 18% is 19.3% less 18% on $1,167.9m of assets, or $14.8m. The futures are sized at that excess times private equity's beta to US equity, 1.03 from a 0.73 correlation and desmoothed volatility of 22.5% against 16%, for $15.2m of notional. That holds the fund's equity exposure where it would be with private equity at its limit. It does not change the private holding, and the sizing ignores basis, roll and margin. With cash 1 pp over its floor, margin in an equity rally is the main liquidity draw.

Tilts after the trades

The list cures the breaches and leaves most tilts unfilled. International equity ends at 14.2% against a 16.3% tactical target, Emerging markets at 6.6% against 8.3% and US Treasuries at 6.2% against 8.8%. I accept that while private weights are estimates.

Diligence findings

Holdings were read for all 10 sleeves and tied to the $1,182.9m total. The model passed 25 of 25 tests and the audit log is intact. The trade list passed 4 of 4 checks.

  • Trades net to zero: $1,182.9m less $15.0m of spending and $7.6k of costs gives $1,167.9m.
  • All 10 sleeves sit inside their ranges after the trades, counting the overlay.
  • Private equity and real assets each trade $0.0m.
  • Every trade meets the $2.0m minimum or cures a breach.

Two items await approval, and two points need attention.

  • The range check books the overlay against private equity, shown at 18.0%. Booked against US equity, the futures would leave that sleeve at 19.7% (21.0% less 1.3 pp of notional), still inside its 17% to 27% range. I support the convention, and staff should report US equity with and without the futures.
  • Hedge funds are treated as tradable at quarter end, assuming the redemption notice was given. No hedge fund trade is proposed.

What would change the view

  • The Q4 statements move private equity well away from 19.3%. Above, the overlay grows; under 18%, it goes.
  • Calls well above $20.0m or $6.0m push cash toward 1%. I would sell more US equity before cutting any purchase that cures a breach.
  • The committee changes the 0.50% budget, which changes the 0.76 scale and every tactical target.
  • Private equity still contributes most of the 1.11% tracking error next quarter. That calls for a pacing review of new commitments.

Next steps

  1. Confirm the Q4 2026 flows with the college and the private fund managers.
  2. Approve the two items and the overlay convention.
  3. Sell through Tallowmere US Index Fund.
  4. Sell the futures, with margin from the custodian sweep account.
  5. Resize the overlay on the Q4 private statements.
  6. Report weights, cash, active risk against policy and the gap to tactical targets next quarter.
