Investment memo 09 · Allocation note

Allocation committee note

Date
30 September 2026
Portfolio
Kestrel Ridge College Endowment
Strategy
Policy portfolio of 10 sleeves, with tactical tilts and quarterly rebalancing
Under review
$1,182.6m of assets; tilts sized to a 0.50% tracking error budget; 3 drafted trades with $30.2m of turnover and a $48.8m futures overlay
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Adopt the sized tactical targets for Q4 2026, with every view scaled by 0.62 to fit the 0.50% tracking error budget. Approve the trade list as drafted: sell $51.2m of US equity, buy $7.9m of International equity and $1.4m of US Treasuries, and sell $48.8m of US equity index futures as an overlay against the private equity overweight.

Conditions

  • Staff confirm the quarter's spending, calls and distributions before execution.
  • The overlay is resized on the Q4 private statements and removed once private equity is back under its 18% limit.
  • Overlay margin is paid from cash, and staff report if cash nears its 1% floor.

Summary

The Kestrel Ridge College Endowment held $1,182.6m at 30 September 2026 across 10 sleeves. Three sit outside their ranges: International equity at 9.2% against a 10% floor, US Treasuries at 4.8% against 5%, and private equity at 20.7% against an 18% ceiling. I support the tilts and the trades for four reasons.

  • Cash is the binding problem. Spending and net calls take $34.0m against $15.4m on hand. The US equity sale restores cash to its 2.0% target.
  • The trades are small and cure every breach. Turnover is $30.2m, 2.6% of assets, for $13.5k of costs, and the list passes 4 of 4 checks.
  • Private equity contributes 1.16% of the 1.18% drift tracking error. It cannot be sold, so the overlay is the only lever this quarter.
  • The tilts fit the budget. Scaled from 0.81% to 0.50% of tracking error, they add 7 bps a year.

The portfolio

The endowment is a fund of about $1.2bn. It holds seven liquid sleeves through single managers or directly, private equity through 14 buyout and growth funds and real assets through 6 real asset funds. At quarter end 71.0% of assets were liquid. Public sleeves trade daily; Halvergate Absolute Return deals quarterly.

Each sleeve has a policy target and range, listed in the appendix. Cash is the balancing account, with a 2% target and a 1% to 5% range. Liquid sleeves outside their range trade to the nearest edge. Private sleeves are never traded to rebalance; when private equity sits above its range, US equity index futures are sold for the excess. Trades under $2.0m are dropped unless they cure a breach.

The private equity overweight follows a year of strong private marks. Those marks lag public markets, so private weights are estimates until the next statements arrive.

Analysis

Drift and breaches

Private equity is the largest gap, 2.7 pp above its upper limit. International equity is 0.8 pp under its floor and US Treasuries 0.2 pp under. US equity, at 24.3%, is inside its range but above its 22% target.

Drift tracking error

Current weights carry 1.18% a year of tracking error against policy, and private equity contributes 1.16%. That uses desmoothed volatility: 22.5% for private equity against a reported 13%, and 16.7% for real assets against 9%. On appraisal volatility the drift would look much smaller. The International equity underweight offsets 0.33%, since that sleeve has a 0.71 correlation with private equity. I read the drift as one position: too much equity risk held in private funds.

Tactical views and sizing

My views are underweight US equity, Core bonds and Hedge funds, and overweight International, Emerging markets, High yield and US Treasuries. They sum to zero. Private equity and real assets take no tilt. As entered they carry 0.81% of tracking error, so each is scaled by 0.62, which keeps their shape. Every tactical target is inside its range, though High yield at 5.86% sits near its 6% ceiling and Hedge funds at 8.90% near the 8% floor. Emerging markets and International equity carry most of the tilt risk, at 0.32% and 0.31%.

Expected return and risk

The tilts add 7 bps a year, 0.14 per unit of tracking error. Expected return moves from 6.46% to 6.53% and volatility from 10.92% to 11.24%. That pickup is modest. Their main use this quarter is to steer the trades: the rebalance sells the sleeve furthest above its tactical target, which is why the cash comes from US equity.

Portfolio fit

The quarter's cash flows

In Q4 2026 the college draws $15.0m. Private equity calls $24.0m and distributes $10.0m; real assets call $9.0m and distribute $4.0m. Net calls are $19.0m. Cash would fall from $15.4m to -$18.6m, or -1.6%, before any trade, and the calls lift private equity to 22.2%.

Cash after the quarter

Sales raise $51.2m and purchases use $9.2m. Cash takes the $42.0m difference, pays the costs, and ends at $23.3m, 2.0% of assets. Assets after spending and costs are $1,167.6m. US equity ends at 20.3%, close to its 19.5% tactical target.

The overlay

The overlay is private equity's excess over 18%, 22.2% less 18%, on $1,167.6m of assets: $48.8m of futures. It brings equity exposure to where it would be with private equity at its limit. It does not change the private holding, and its sizing ignores basis, roll and margin. With cash 1 pp over its floor, margin in an equity rally is the main liquidity draw.

Tilts after the trades

The list cures breaches and leaves most tilts unfilled. International equity ends at 10.0% against a 16.5% tactical target and Hedge funds at 12.6% against 8.9%. I accept that while private weights are estimates.

Diligence findings

Holdings were read for all 10 sleeves and tied to the $1,182.6m total. The model passed 24 of 24 tests and the audit log is intact. The trade list passed 4 of 4 checks.

  • Trades net to zero: $1,182.6m less $15.0m of spending and $13.5k of costs gives $1,167.6m.
  • All 10 sleeves sit inside their ranges after the trades, counting the overlay.
  • Private equity and real assets each trade $0.0m.
  • Every trade meets the $2.0m minimum or cures a breach.

Four items await approval, and three points need attention.

  • The $1.4m Treasuries purchase is under the minimum. Policy allows it because it cures a breach.
  • The range check books the overlay against private equity, shown at 18.0%. Booked against US equity, the futures would leave that sleeve near 16.1% (20.3% less the 4.2 pp excess), under its 17% floor. I support the convention, but the committee should approve it.
  • Hedge funds are treated as tradable at quarter end, assuming the redemption notice was given. No hedge fund trade is proposed.

What would change the view

  • The Q4 statements move private equity well away from 22.2%. Above, the overlay grows; under 18%, it goes.
  • Calls well above $24.0m or $9.0m push cash toward 1%. I would sell more US equity before cutting any purchase that cures a breach.
  • The committee changes the 0.50% budget, which changes the 0.62 scale and every tactical target.
  • Drift tracking error stays near 1.18% next quarter, still led by private equity. That calls for a pacing review.

Next steps

  1. Confirm the Q4 2026 flows with the college and the private fund managers.
  2. Approve each of the four items and the overlay convention.
  3. Sell through Tallowmere US Index Fund; buy through Brennock International Equity and the Treasury ladder.
  4. Sell the futures, with margin from the custodian sweep account.
  5. Resize the overlay on the Q4 private statements.
  6. Report drift, cash and the gap to tactical targets next quarter.
