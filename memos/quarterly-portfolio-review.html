Recommendation

Keep the policy benchmark and the 3.0% tracking error budget, and run the 1 October rebalance, which takes private equity from 19.1% of the portfolio back to its 16.5% start weight. Approve the three monitoring flags. Granite Shoal Real Assets is asked for a new valuation, Corvane Emerging Markets is asked to bring its 6.9% tracking error back inside the 5.0% mandate limit, and Ashcombe Fixed Income goes on the watch list for the rise in its credit beta.

Conditions

  • From this quarter, the tracking error budget and private asset sizing are reported on desmoothed marks.
  • If Corvane's tracking error is still above 5.0% at the next quarterly review, emerging markets equity comes back from 6.9% to its 6.0% policy weight.
  • The real assets top-up at the rebalance waits for Granite Shoal's new valuation.

Summary

This is the quarterly review of a $2,000m multi-asset portfolio run through 15 external managers, measured against its policy benchmark over 60 months to 30 Sep 2026. Returns are ahead of the benchmark and risk is inside budget. The weak point is how much of the risk sits in private equity, and how much of it the reported marks hide. I recommend small changes for four reasons.

  • Net of fees the portfolio returned 19.16% in the year against 18.20% for the benchmark. It is also ahead over three years (+2.28 pp a year) and five years (+0.63 pp a year).
  • Tracking error is 2.30% on desmoothed marks against a 3.0% budget, so the budget itself needs no trade.
  • Private equity carries 30.4% of total volatility and 1.92% of the 2.30% tracking error at a 19.1% weight. On reported marks the private sleeves look like 23% of total risk. Desmoothed, they are 36%.
  • Three managers need follow-up. Every reconciliation ties, so the figures in this review can go out.

The portfolio

The fund holds 15 managers across nine asset classes. Two are index funds, Harrowgate US Index Fund and Merrow Core Bond Index. Nine run active public or hedge fund mandates, each with a tracking error limit against its own index, from 0.5% for the index funds to 10.0% for Bellwyn Small Cap. The four private funds have no tracking error limit and report appraisal marks. They are Larchmont Buyout Fund IV and Westerly Growth Partners III in private equity, Brookhaven Direct Lending II in private credit and Granite Shoal Real Assets.

The policy benchmark is a fixed mix of nine indices. Private equity is measured against global equity plus 3.0% a year and private credit against senior loans plus 2%. Portfolio and benchmark both rebalance on 1 October and are held through the year. The portfolio starts each year 2.5 pp over policy in private equity (16.5% against 14.0%) and 1 pp over in emerging markets equity and hedge funds. It is under policy by 1 pp in US and international equity, by 2 pp in core fixed income and by 0.5 pp in real assets.

A strong year for private equity marks has moved the weights. At 30 Sep 2026 private equity is 19.1% of the portfolio, core fixed income 14.2%, and real assets 5.5% on Granite Shoal's June mark.

Performance and risk

Returns against the policy benchmark

The portfolio returned 19.16% net of fees in the year to 30 Sep 2026 against 18.20% for the policy benchmark, an active return of +0.96 pp. Over three years it is ahead by +2.28 pp a year with an information ratio of 1.18. Over five years the lead is +0.63 pp a year (7.98% against 7.35%) and the information ratio 0.34. The gap between the two comes from the first year, Oct 2021 to Sep 2022, which lost 2.47 pp to the benchmark.

Attribution

Brinson effects add up to the active return in every year, with a largest gap of 5.0e-17. Private equity drives the result. It was the largest positive class in each of the last four years and the largest negative in the first. In the latest year its 230 bps is more than the whole 96 bps of active return, so the other eight classes together took value away. These returns use reported marks, which lag, and I treat the private equity selection effect as the least certain number in the attribution.

Emerging markets selection was negative in four of the five years, and -82 bps in the latest. Real assets selection cost 84 bps this year, almost all of it Granite Shoal. Core fixed income selection added 48 bps.

Manager contributions

Larchmont Buyout Fund IV added 129 bps of active return on a 41.1% return. Ashcombe Fixed Income added 43 bps and Quillmere Equity Partners 40 bps. The largest detractors were Corvane Emerging Markets (-95 bps, 16.7% against 30.3% for its index), Granite Shoal Real Assets (-78 bps, 1.3% against 13.3%) and Bellwyn Small Cap (-68 bps, 1.6% against 24.2%). Bellwyn's three-year return is 21.8% a year and its tracking error of 7.2% sits inside its 10.0% limit, so I read this as one bad year. Corvane's five-year return of 2.7% a year is the lowest of the public equity managers. Ashcombe's gain came while its credit beta rose to 0.84 against 0.25 for its benchmark, so part of it looks like credit exposure the Core bond index does not carry.

Risk

Ex ante, with private marks desmoothed, total volatility is 13.15% against 11.4% for the benchmark. Equity explains 84% of it. Factors add 13.01% and manager-specific risk 0.14%. Tracking error is 2.30% against the 3.0% budget. Realised tracking error was 2.2% over one year and 1.9% over five.

Private equity is the concentration. At a 19.1% weight it carries 4.00% of total volatility, 30.4% of the total, and 1.92% of the 2.30% tracking error. Larchmont Buyout Fund IV is the largest single contributor at 2.35% of volatility (17.9%) on an 11.8% weight, and Westerly Growth Partners III adds 12.5% on 7.2%. Manager-specific risk is 36% of tracking error. The other 64% comes from factor tilts against the policy weights, led by equity, so the private equity overweight works mostly as an equity beta position.

The desmoothed view of private assets

The four private series have first-order autocorrelation of 0.44 to 0.50. Desmoothing lifts private sleeve volatility from 9.7% to 15.8%, 1.6x the reported figure. Larchmont's equity beta goes from 0.64 to 1.21 and Westerly's from 0.79 to 1.52. On reported marks total volatility would read 10.96% and tracking error 1.53%. Desmoothed they are 13.15% and 2.30%, and the private share of total volatility rises from 22.85% to 35.68%. I size and budget risk on the desmoothed figures. Geltner desmoothing assumes one lag of smoothing, so marks that lag by more than a quarter still understate risk.

Drawdowns and stress tests

The maximum drawdown over five years was -14.9% against -12.9% for the benchmark, and -15.8% with desmoothed marks. A repeat of the 2008 factor moves would cost the portfolio 39.7%, or $793m, against 33.4% for the benchmark. On the betas of the reported marks the loss would look like 32.1%, which is the gap smoothing hides. The 2020 and 2022 episodes cost 27.3% and 23.6%. The portfolio loses more than its benchmark in all three, by 2.1 to 6.2 pp, which is what the equity-heavy overweights cost in a sell-off.

Portfolio fit

The 1 October rebalance resets the start weights. Private equity goes from 19.1% to 16.5%, core fixed income from 14.2% to 16.0% and real assets from 5.5% to 6.5%. I have not rerun the risk model at those weights, so I give no new volatility or tracking error figure. The direction is clear. Private equity carries 1.92% of tracking error at 19.1%, and a lower weight cuts both total volatility and tracking error. For private funds the reset runs through commitment pacing and distributions, and until it is done I report the overweight against the budget.

I would not spend the room left under the 3.0% budget. Read on reported marks, tracking error is 1.53% and the budget looks half used. Desmoothed it is 2.30%, and the 2008 stress already costs 6.2 pp more than the benchmark.

Emerging markets equity is 6.9% against a 6.0% policy weight, all of it with Corvane. At class level it adds 0.34% to tracking error. If Corvane cannot bring its risk back inside the mandate, going back to policy weight removes the overweight and most of that exposure. Topping real assets back up to 6.5% at the rebalance would mean buying Granite Shoal on a mark three months old, which is why that part waits for the valuation.

Diligence findings

Every figure in the review has to add up before it goes out. All 7 reconciliations tie within 1e-9. Risk contributions sum to total volatility on both bases (13.1539% desmoothed and 10.9631% reported), and factor plus specific risk gives the same totals. Active contributions sum to the 2.3039% tracking error. Brinson effects tie in each of the 5 years, and the monthly series compounds to the 19.1563% holdings return for the year. All 4 private series were desmoothed, 25 of 25 model tests passed and the audit log chain is intact across its 11 entries.

Three items were raised for review, and the watch list takes approved flags only.

  • Granite Shoal Real Assets has shown the same mark for its last 3 months, unchanged since Jun 2026. Those months are left out of its autocorrelation and beta estimates, while its 5.5% weight still uses the old mark.
  • Corvane Emerging Markets has ex-ante tracking error of 6.94% against the Emerging markets index, over its 5.0% limit. Specific risk alone is 6.0% a year. It is the only mandate breach; 10 managers are inside their limits.
  • Ashcombe Fixed Income's credit beta was 0.24 up to Sep 2024 and 0.84 in the last 24 months, with a z of 9.6, against 0.25 for the Core bond index. It is the only shift among 75 manager and factor pairs.

One point on reading the manager table. Manager contributions to tracking error are shown gross of the benchmark legs, so they do not add to 2.30% on their own. The Corvane flag's 1.31% contribution is one of these gross figures. Net, at class level, emerging markets adds 0.34%.

What would change the view

  • Desmoothed tracking error rises toward the 3.0% budget. I would cut the private equity overweight faster than pacing allows.
  • Corvane's tracking error stays above 5.0% at the next quarterly review, or a fifth year of negative emerging markets selection follows. I would move emerging markets to policy weight and review the mandate.
  • Granite Shoal's new valuation comes in well below the June mark. The 1.3% return and the 5.5% weight would both be restated, and I would revisit the real assets allocation.
  • Ashcombe's credit beta stays near 0.84 without a change to its guidelines. I would shift core fixed income toward Merrow Core Bond Index.
  • The five-year active return turns negative, which would put the private equity overweight itself up for review.

Next steps

  1. Approve the three flags and put the three managers on the watch list.
  2. Request an updated valuation from Granite Shoal Real Assets for the months since Jun 2026.
  3. Ask Corvane Emerging Markets to explain its positions and how it will bring tracking error inside 5.0%.
  4. Ask Ashcombe Fixed Income to explain the rise in its credit beta.
  5. Run the 1 October rebalance, holding the real assets top-up until the new Granite Shoal mark is in.
  6. Rerun the risk model at the post-rebalance weights and report volatility, tracking error and stress losses on desmoothed marks in the next quarterly review.
