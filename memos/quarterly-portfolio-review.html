Investment memo 08 · Quarterly review

Quarterly portfolio review

Date
30 September 2026
Portfolio
Multi-asset fund with 15 external managers
Strategy
Nine asset classes, private assets included, managed against a policy benchmark
Portfolio size
$2,000m of total assets (custodian, 30 Sep 2026)
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Keep the policy benchmark and the 3.0% tracking error budget, and run the 1 October rebalance of the liquid sleeves. Private equity stands at 15.8% against its 14.0% policy weight and cannot be sold back to weight, so I would bring it down through distributions and a smaller 2027 commitment budget. Approve the three monitoring flags, which ask Granite Shoal Real Assets for a new valuation, ask Corvane Emerging Markets to bring its 6.9% tracking error inside the 5.0% limit and put Ashcombe Fixed Income on the watch list.

Conditions

  • The risk budget and private asset sizing are reported on desmoothed marks from this quarter.
  • If Corvane is still above 5.0% at the next review, emerging markets equity goes from 6.4% to its 6.0% policy weight.
  • The real assets top-up waits for Granite Shoal's new valuation.

Summary

This reviews a $2,000m multi-asset portfolio of 15 external managers against its policy benchmark, over 60 months to 30 Sep 2026. Active risk is moderate and spread over several positions. The weak points are three managers and a stale private mark. I recommend small changes for four reasons.

  • Net of fees the portfolio returned 17.77% against 18.20% for the year, 0.43 pp behind. It is ahead over three years (+0.95 pp a year) and level over five (-0.02 pp).
  • Desmoothed ex-ante tracking error is 1.39% against a 3.0% budget, so the budget needs no trade.
  • Private equity is the largest source of tracking error, 0.68% of the 1.39%, from a 1.8 pp overweight after strong marks and the specific risk of two programs. Emerging markets adds 0.26% and international equity 0.21%.
  • Three managers need follow-up, and every reconciliation ties.

The portfolio

The fund holds 15 managers across nine asset classes. Harrowgate US Index Fund and Merrow Core Bond Index are index funds. Nine active public and hedge fund mandates each carry a tracking error limit against their own index. The four private lines are separate accounts, each spread across many funds or deals. They report appraisal marks and have no limit: Larchmont Buyout Partners and Westerly Growth Partners in private equity, Brookhaven Direct Lending in private credit and Granite Shoal Real Assets.

The policy benchmark is a fixed mix of nine indices, with private equity measured against global equity plus 3.0% a year. Each year starts on 1 October from the same weights. The deliberate tilts are small. US equity sits 2.0 pp under policy, core fixed income 1.5 pp under and real assets 0.5 pp under. High yield, hedge funds and private credit are each 1.0 pp over, and international and emerging markets equity 0.5 pp over. Private equity starts each year at its 14.0% policy weight. This year its marks rose faster than the rest of the fund, 35.3% for Larchmont and 28.9% for Westerly against 17.8% for the fund, so it stood at 15.8% on 30 Sep 2026.

Performance and risk

Returns and attribution

The portfolio returned 17.77% net of fees in the year against 18.20% for the benchmark, an active return of -0.43 pp. Over three years it is ahead by +0.95 pp a year. Over five years it is level with the benchmark at -0.02 pp a year, with an information ratio of -0.01, because the first year lost 2.10 pp.

Allocation is small in every year, as the tilts are, so manager selection drives the result. Private equity was the largest positive class from 2023 to 2025 and the largest negative in 2022. This year core fixed income (66 bps) and private equity (62 bps) added the most, while emerging markets (-83 bps), real assets (-76 bps) and US equity (-43 bps) cost the most. Private returns use reported marks, so I treat the private equity selection effect as the least certain number here. Emerging markets selection was negative in four of five years.

By manager, Larchmont Buyout Partners added 57 bps on a 35.3% return. Ashcombe Fixed Income added 43 bps as its credit beta rose to 0.84 against 0.25 for its benchmark, so part of that gain is credit exposure. The largest detractors were Corvane Emerging Markets (-89 bps), Granite Shoal Real Assets (-78 bps) and Bellwyn Small Cap (-68 bps). Bellwyn returned 21.8% a year over three years, so I read this as one bad year. Corvane's 2.7% a year over five years is the weakest of the public equity managers.

Risk

Ex ante, with private marks desmoothed, total volatility is 12.22% against 11.4% for the benchmark, and equity explains 81% of it. US equity is the largest contributor, with 27% of total volatility at a 21% weight. Ex-ante tracking error is 1.39% against the 3.0% budget. Realised tracking error on reported marks was 1.8% over five years, higher because smoothed private returns move out of step with their public benchmarks.

Private equity carries 0.68% of the 1.39% tracking error, about half. That comes from the 1.8 pp overweight and from the specific risk of two programs measured against a public index. Emerging markets adds 0.26%, nearly all of it Corvane, and international equity 0.21%. Manager-specific risk is 58% of tracking error and factor exposures that differ from the benchmark's are the other 42%, led by equity.

Desmoothing lifts private sleeve volatility from 8.0% to 12.8%. It also lifts the equity beta of Larchmont from 0.54 to 1.00 and of Westerly from 0.65 to 1.22. On reported marks total volatility would read 10.58%. Tracking error would read about the same at 1.40%, but 93% of it would appear to sit in private assets, because smoothed marks make private equity look like a low-beta asset against a benchmark with a beta of 0.97. Desmoothed, the private share is 54%. I size and budget risk on the desmoothed figures.

The maximum drawdown over five years was -14.6% against -12.9% for the benchmark, and also -14.6% on desmoothed marks. A repeat of the 2008 factor moves would cost 36.8%, or $737m, against 33.4% for the benchmark; on reported betas it would look like 31.1%.

Portfolio fit

The 1 October rebalance takes the liquid sleeves back to their start weights, including core fixed income from 14.8% to 16.5% and international equity from 13.4% to 12.5%. Private equity cannot be traded back from 15.8% to 14.0%. I would size the 2027 commitments so distributions bring it down over the year. I have not rerun the risk model at the new weights, so I give no new figures.

I would not spend the room under the budget. Tracking error of 1.39% uses less than half of the 3.0% budget, but about two thirds of it sits in two places I want smaller: the private equity overweight (0.68%) and emerging markets (0.26%), which is mostly Corvane. Topping real assets up from 5.6% would mean buying Granite Shoal on a three-month-old mark.

Diligence findings

All 7 reconciliations tie within 1e-9. Risk contributions sum to total volatility on both bases, active contributions sum to the 1.3903% tracking error, Brinson effects tie in each of the 5 years and the monthly series compounds to the 17.7668% holdings return. All 4 private series were desmoothed, 26 of 26 model tests passed and the audit log is intact. Three flags went for review.

  • Granite Shoal Real Assets has held the same mark since Jun 2026. The 3 stale months are left out of its risk estimate, but its 5.6% weight still uses the old mark.
  • Corvane Emerging Markets runs 6.94% tracking error against a 5.0% limit, with specific risk of 6.0% a year. It is the only breach; 10 managers are inside their limits.
  • Ashcombe Fixed Income's credit beta moved from 0.24 before Oct 2024 to 0.84 since (z 9.6), the only shift among 75 manager and factor pairs.

In the manager table each manager's contribution to tracking error is net of its share of the class benchmark, so the two index funds show close to zero.

What would change the view

  • Desmoothed tracking error moves toward the 3.0% budget. I would sell part of the private equity overweight on the secondary market instead of waiting for distributions.
  • Corvane is still above 5.0% at the next review. I would move emerging markets to policy weight and review the mandate.
  • Granite Shoal's new valuation comes in well below the June mark. I would restate its 1.3% return and revisit real assets.
  • Ashcombe's credit beta stays near 0.84 without a change to its mandate. I would shift core fixed income toward Merrow.
  • The three-year lead of 0.95 pp a year disappears. I would review the active public managers, starting with Corvane and Bellwyn.

Next steps

  1. Approve the three flags for the watch list.
  2. Request an updated valuation from Granite Shoal Real Assets.
  3. Ask Corvane Emerging Markets for its positions and a plan to get inside 5.0%.
  4. Ask Ashcombe Fixed Income to explain its credit beta.
  5. Run the 1 October rebalance of the liquid sleeves, holding the real assets top-up.
  6. Set the 2027 private equity commitment budget so distributions bring the sleeve back toward 14.0%.
  7. Rerun the risk model at the new weights for the next quarterly review.
