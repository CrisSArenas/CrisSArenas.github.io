Investment memo 08 · Quarterly review

Quarterly portfolio review

Date
30 September 2026
Portfolio
Multi-asset fund with 15 external managers
Strategy
Nine asset classes, private assets included, managed against a policy benchmark
Portfolio size
$2,000m of total assets (custodian, 30 Sep 2026)
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Keep the policy benchmark and the 3.0% tracking error budget, and run the 1 October rebalance, which takes private equity from 19.1% back to its 16.5% start weight. Approve the three monitoring flags, which ask Granite Shoal Real Assets for a new valuation, ask Corvane Emerging Markets to bring its 6.9% tracking error inside the 5.0% limit and put Ashcombe Fixed Income on the watch list.

Conditions

  • The risk budget and private asset sizing are reported on desmoothed marks from this quarter.
  • If Corvane is still above 5.0% at the next review, emerging markets equity goes from 6.9% to its 6.0% policy weight.
  • The real assets top-up waits for Granite Shoal's new valuation.

Summary

This reviews a $2,000m multi-asset portfolio of 15 external managers against its policy benchmark, over 60 months to 30 Sep 2026. The weak point is how much risk sits in private equity, and how much of it reported marks hide. I recommend small changes for four reasons.

  • Net of fees the portfolio returned 19.16% against 18.20% for the year, and is ahead over three years (+2.28 pp a year) and five (+0.63 pp).
  • Desmoothed tracking error is 2.30% against a 3.0% budget, so the budget needs no trade.
  • Private equity carries 30.4% of total volatility at a 19.1% weight. On reported marks the private sleeves look like 23% of risk; desmoothed, 36%.
  • Three managers need follow-up, and every reconciliation ties.

The portfolio

The fund holds 15 managers across nine asset classes. Harrowgate US Index Fund and Merrow Core Bond Index are index funds. Nine active public and hedge fund mandates each carry a tracking error limit against their own index. The four private funds report appraisal marks and have no limit: Larchmont Buyout Fund IV and Westerly Growth Partners III in private equity, Brookhaven Direct Lending II in private credit and Granite Shoal Real Assets.

The policy benchmark is a fixed mix of nine indices, with private equity measured against global equity plus 3.0% a year. Portfolio and benchmark both rebalance on 1 October. The portfolio starts each year over policy in private equity (16.5% against 14.0%), emerging markets and hedge funds, and under in core fixed income (16.0% against 18.0%). After a strong year for private marks, private equity stood at 19.1% on 30 Sep 2026.

Performance and risk

Returns and attribution

The portfolio returned 19.16% net of fees in the year against 18.20% for the benchmark, an active return of +0.96 pp. Over three years it is ahead by +2.28 pp a year. Over five years the lead is +0.63 pp a year and the information ratio 0.34, held down by the first year, which lost 2.47 pp.

Private equity drives the result. It was the largest positive class in each of the last four years and the largest negative in the first. This year its 230 bps exceeds the whole 96 bps of active return, so the other eight classes together took value away. Those returns use lagged reported marks, so I treat that selection effect as the least certain number here. Emerging markets selection was negative in four of five years.

By manager, Larchmont Buyout Fund IV added 129 bps on a 41.1% return. Ashcombe Fixed Income added 43 bps as its credit beta rose to 0.84 against 0.25 for its benchmark, so part of that gain is credit exposure. The largest detractors were Corvane Emerging Markets (-95 bps), Granite Shoal Real Assets (-78 bps) and Bellwyn Small Cap (-68 bps). Bellwyn returned 21.8% a year over three years, so I read this as one bad year. Corvane's 2.7% a year over five years is the weakest of the public equity managers.

Risk

Ex ante, with private marks desmoothed, total volatility is 13.15% against 11.4% for the benchmark, and equity explains 84% of it. Tracking error is 2.30% against the 3.0% budget; realised, it was 1.9% over five years.

Private equity carries 4.00% of total volatility and 1.92% of the 2.30% tracking error. Factor tilts against policy, led by equity, make up 64% of tracking error, so the private equity overweight works as an equity beta position.

Desmoothing lifts private sleeve volatility from 9.7% to 15.8%, and Larchmont's equity beta from 0.64 to 1.21. On reported marks total volatility would read 10.96% and tracking error 1.53%. I size and budget risk on the desmoothed figures.

The maximum drawdown over five years was -14.9% against -12.9% for the benchmark, and -15.8% desmoothed. A repeat of the 2008 factor moves would cost 39.7%, or $793m, against 33.4% for the benchmark; on reported betas it would look like 32.1%.

Portfolio fit

The 1 October rebalance resets private equity from 19.1% to 16.5% and core fixed income from 14.2% to 16.0%. I have not rerun the risk model at those weights, so I give no new figures, but less private equity lowers both volatility and tracking error. For private funds the reset runs through pacing and distributions.

I would not spend the room under the budget. On reported marks tracking error reads 1.53% and the budget looks half used; desmoothed, it is 2.30%. Emerging markets, all of it Corvane, adds 0.34% to tracking error at class level. Topping real assets up from 5.5% would mean buying Granite Shoal on a three-month-old mark.

Diligence findings

All 7 reconciliations tie within 1e-9. Risk contributions sum to total volatility on both bases, active contributions sum to the 2.3039% tracking error, Brinson effects tie in each of the 5 years and the monthly series compounds to the 19.1563% holdings return. All 4 private series were desmoothed, 25 of 25 model tests passed and the audit log is intact. Three flags went for review.

  • Granite Shoal Real Assets has held the same mark since Jun 2026. The 3 stale months are left out of its risk estimate, but its 5.5% weight still uses the old mark.
  • Corvane Emerging Markets runs 6.94% tracking error against a 5.0% limit, with specific risk of 6.0% a year. It is the only breach; 10 managers are inside their limits.
  • Ashcombe Fixed Income's credit beta moved from 0.24 before Oct 2024 to 0.84 since (z 9.6), the only shift among 75 manager and factor pairs.

In the manager table, tracking error contributions are gross of the benchmark legs. Net of them, emerging markets adds 0.34% to the fund's tracking error.

What would change the view

  • Desmoothed tracking error moves toward the 3.0% budget. I would cut the private equity overweight faster than pacing allows.
  • Corvane is still above 5.0% at the next review. I would move emerging markets to policy weight and review the mandate.
  • Granite Shoal's new valuation comes in well below the June mark. I would restate its 1.3% return and revisit real assets.
  • Ashcombe's credit beta stays near 0.84 without a change to its mandate. I would shift core fixed income toward Merrow.
  • The five-year active return turns negative. I would review the private equity overweight.

Next steps

  1. Approve the three flags for the watch list.
  2. Request an updated valuation from Granite Shoal Real Assets.
  3. Ask Corvane Emerging Markets for its positions and a plan to get inside 5.0%.
  4. Ask Ashcombe Fixed Income to explain its credit beta.
  5. Run the 1 October rebalance, holding the real assets top-up.
  6. Rerun the risk model at the new weights for the next quarterly review.
