Investment memo 04 · Secondary purchase

Six-fund LP portfolio

Date
30 June 2026
Deal
Six LP fund interests, and the Fenmoor Capital Partners IV continuation vehicle election
Strategy
LP secondaries in buyout, growth, venture, infrastructure and credit funds
Size under consideration
Six interests with $171.0m of reported NAV and $29.0m of unfunded; a $12.4m stake in the Fenmoor CV
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Bid 88% of 31 March NAV, $150.3m, for the six interests, priced fund by fund at a 15% target IRR. In the Fenmoor Capital Partners IV continuation vehicle, elect to sell our $12.4m stake for $11.66m of cash.

Conditions

  • The bid holds only if Q2 NAVs come in within 5% of the roll-forward to 30 June.
  • We return the Fenmoor election form as a sale and ask the advisory committee for the 20 business days the LPA requires.

Summary

Six LP interests are offered with $171.0m of NAV at 31 March 2026 and $29.0m of unfunded. Separately, Fenmoor Capital Partners IV has asked its LPs to sell or roll into a GP-led continuation vehicle. We hold $12.4m of NAV there.

I support the bid for two reasons.

  • The reported NAVs are a quarter old. Rolled forward on a -4% public equity quarter, they are worth $162.8m, 4.8% below reported. The bid of 87.9% of reported NAV is 92% of the rolled figure.
  • At the bid the projected cash flows return 15%. Across 1,000 simulated paths the buyer IRR runs from 12.6% at the 10th percentile to 17.8% at the 90th, and no path earns less than 10%.

On Fenmoor, rolling needs 1.57x gross over four years to match selling and reinvesting at 10%. My house case of 1.70x clears that by $1.32m on our stake. The margin turns negative with one extension, and the process has four open conflicts. I prefer the cash.

The Fenmoor continuation vehicle

The GP proposes to move Larkspur Veterinary Group and Orrin Fluid Systems into Fenmoor Continuation Vehicle I, led by Halbrook Secondaries. The price is 94.0% of the two companies' 31 December 2025 NAV of $612 million. Our share of that NAV is $12.4m, and selling pays $11.66m.

Rolling LPs pay a 1.0% annual fee on NAV and carry of 12.5% over an 8% preferred return, rising to 20% on gains above a 2.5x net multiple, with full catch-up. The expected hold is four years, with two one-year extensions at the GP's discretion.

Pricing

NAV roll-forward and the bid

I moved each 31 March NAV to 30 June with its strategy's beta to public equity, then added calls and took off distributions since. Arvane Buyout VII falls most, from 42.0 to 38.1, because it paid out $2.6m in the quarter. Tamsin Buyout IX rises slightly to 35.1 on $1.4m of calls.

Each interest is then priced as the present value of its projected distributions less calls at the 15% target. The total is $150.3m, a 12.1% discount to reported NAV. Draycott Infrastructure II prices lowest at 84.3% because its 8% NAV growth sits well below the target. Sorrel Ventures IV prices highest at 93.1%.

Range of buyer outcomes

With each fund's growth shocked, the median path earns 15.3% at the bid. The 10th to 90th percentile range is 12.6% to 17.8%.

Sensitivity

Each extra 5% of NAV haircut takes four to five points off the price. At a 15% target, a 5% haircut takes the portfolio to 84% and a 10% haircut to 79%. Dropping the hurdle to 12% would justify 93%. I kept the desk's 15% hurdle. Unfunded matters less; on a three-year-old buyout interest the price moves from 83.6% of NAV with none to 81.5% at 40%.

Roll or sell in the Fenmoor CV

Selling turns $1 into 1.46x over four years at a 10% opportunity cost. Rolling beats that only if the companies return more than 1.57x gross, 11.9% a year, after the fee and the carry that restarts at the transaction price.

My house case of 1.70x gross nets 1.58x, 12.1% a year, and beats selling by 0.11x, or $1.32m on our stake at the end of the hold. If the GP uses both extensions at the same multiple, rolling nets 1.59x against 1.77x from selling. A single extension already turns the house case negative, at -0.04 per dollar.

Portfolio fit

The purchase brings $162.8m of current NAV with a weighted distribution duration of 2.6 years. Hollin Credit Opportunities III and Arvane Buyout VII return cash fastest; Tamsin Buyout IX and Sorrel Ventures IV take four years or more.

The $29.0m of unfunded, 17% of NAV, is cash we must plan to fund. Almost half of it, $14m, sits in Tamsin Buyout IX, the youngest fund. I did not measure the purchase against our wider pacing plan or existing manager exposures; this analysis prices the portfolio on its own.

Selling the Fenmoor stake returns $11.66m now. It removes exposure to two companies whose terms would reset under the GP's control.

Diligence findings

The review covered the three Fenmoor documents: the CV election notice, the Ellery Brook Advisors fairness opinion summary, and the LPA amendment with the LPAC consent. Every figure was tied to its page and line. Extraction accuracy was 100% on 20 hand-labeled fields, and all 4 planted issues were caught with no false flags. I did not review fund-level reports for the six interests; their NAVs are taken as reported at 31 March.

What would change the view

  • Q2 NAVs more than 5% below the roll-forward. I would reprice from the new marks.
  • A higher hurdle. At an 18% target the portfolio is worth 83% of NAV, and the bid would come down to match.
  • A house case for Fenmoor at 2.0x gross. Rolling then beats selling at every hold from three to six years, with +0.05 per dollar even after both extensions.
  • A status quo option, or a hold capped at four years. Either would remove the extension risk that drives the sale.

Next steps

  1. Submit prices fund by fund at the levels in the pricing table, conditional on Q2 NAVs within 5% of the roll-forward.
  2. Return the Fenmoor election form as a sale ahead of the 15-business-day deadline.
  3. Write to the advisory committee asking for the 20 business days under Section 6.3 and for the full fairness opinion.
  4. Add the $29.0m of unfunded to the liquidity plan, with Tamsin Buyout IX's $14m first.
