Recommendation

I recommend we pass on the $25m commitment to Hasturn Mid-Market Buyout III and move the $25m to the secondaries commitment in the pacing plan. I would look again at Fund IV if Fund II matures well outside healthcare and the GP gives credible answers on Fund I net TVPI, Fund II losses and the key person departure.

Summary

Hasturn Partners is raising its third fund with a $900m target and a $1,100m hard cap. The GP targets a first close in the fourth quarter of 2026. The firm's case rests on Fund I (2016), which shows a 2.03x gross MOIC. Fund II (2022) is at 1.24x gross and mostly unrealized.

Four reasons drive my view.

  • Fund I leans on one deal. Brightmere Dental Group made 42.5% of Fund I's gains. Without it, Fund I is 1.65x gross.
  • The manager's own numbers do not hold up. The 2025 letter claims 2.1x net TVPI for Fund I. Rebuilt from the deals and the stated terms, it is 1.62x. The DDQ says Fund II has no realized losses, yet Pell Industrial Coatings sold at 0.55x.
  • Fund II is weak so far. Net TVPI is 1.10x and net IRR is 4.4% as reported. Without the subscription line the net IRR is 3.4%.
  • The team and the ranking are both moving the wrong way. Tomas Brennick, one of three named key persons, stepped down in March 2026. Fund I was top quartile when Fund II was raised and is second quartile today.

The manager

Firm and team

Hasturn Partners was founded in 2014. The February 2026 DDQ lists 31 employees, of whom 14 are investment professionals. It names three key persons: Elena Varga (Managing Partner), Tomas Brennick (Partner) and Daniel Osei (Partner). The investment period suspends if any two of the three stop devoting substantially all of their business time to the fund.

Brennick stepped down in March 2026 and stays on as a senior advisor (team changes are in appendix A3). Varga and Osei now lead the investment committee. Fund III would start with one departure of headroom left on the clause.

Strategy

Control buyouts of North American businesses with EBITDA of $10m to $50m, in healthcare, industrials, technology-enabled services and consumer. In practice the best outcomes have come from healthcare. Brightmere Dental Group (5.79x) and Rowan Veterinary (2.10x), the best deal in each fund, are both healthcare. I do not yet see a repeatable edge outside that sector.

Track record

Gross returns and concentration

Fund I made $844m on $416m across 13 deals. The headline 2.03x depends on Brightmere. The top three deals made 64% of gains. Fund II made $550m on $445m across 10 deals, and its gains are even more concentrated: the top three made 89%, and Rowan Veterinary alone made 43.8%. Without Rowan, Fund II is 1.15x gross.

Value creation

In Fund I, EBITDA growth added $385m and multiple expansion $158m, while net debt rose by $115m. EBITDA growth explains 90% of the $428m equity gain and multiple change 37%. In Fund II, a $134m rise in net debt from add-ons and recapitalisations absorbed most of the $234m from EBITDA growth, leaving a $105m equity gain. Fund II is buying growth with debt, and so far the equity has kept little of it.

Subscription line

Fund II calls capital about nine months after each investment. That lifts the reported net IRR from 3.4% to 4.4%, a gain of 1.0 pp, while TVPI stays at 1.10x. I underwrite Fund II on 3.4%.

Quartile then and now

Fund I was top quartile in 2021, when Fund II was raised, on a net TVPI of 1.78x that was mostly unrealized. On the rebuilt 1.62x it is second quartile today. Among 2016 buyout peers, 55% of funds that were top quartile at fundraising were still top quartile once their portfolios matured. Fund III would be a re-up on Fund II's interim numbers. I did not measure PME.

Portfolio fit

The demo does not model our pacing or exposures, so this section stays short. Fund III would add North American mid-market buyout exposure with a healthcare tilt. Its term runs 10 years plus two one-year extensions. Fund I is ten years old and still holds three unrealized deals, so I would plan on the extensions being used. The subscription line would push our capital calls about nine months past each investment.

The fund also steps up in size. Fund II is $700m (a demo assumption) and has invested $445m. Fund III targets $900m. A larger fund raised on a weak Fund II needs more deals of Brightmere's quality, and the record does not show the GP finds them outside healthcare.

Diligence findings

I read the Hasturn DDQ excerpt (February 2026) and the three LP letters (2024, 2025 and mid-year 2026). Every extracted figure was tied to its page and line. Extraction matched all 20 verified fields and team events.

Flags raised

  • The 2025 letter says Fund I net TVPI is 2.1x. My rebuild from the 13 Fund I deals gives 1.62x. The gap is larger than fee timing can explain.
  • The DDQ says Fund II has no realized losses to date. Pell Industrial Coatings was exited in June 2025 for $22m on $40m invested, and the GP's own 2025 letter says it was sold at a loss. That is a reporting control issue.
  • The DDQ names Brennick as a key person. He stepped down a month after it was dated.

Checks that held

  • The DDQ says no deal exceeds 15% of a fund's invested capital. That holds: the largest is 11.7%.
  • The 2.5% GP commitment is above 1%, and fees and carry match across the DDQ and the term summary.
  • The DDQ's gross figures (2.0x for Fund I, 1.2x for Fund II) match the deal data, and its statement that Fund I has one realized loss matches Eskel Home Brands.

Other items to reconcile

  • The 2025 letter says Fund I has returned 1.6x of paid-in capital. That is almost the whole of the rebuilt 1.62x TVPI, though three Fund I deals remain unrealized. Either the DPI or the TVPI claim needs support.
  • The 2024 letter says Fund II completed its sixth investment. The deal data show nine Fund II entries dated in 2024 or earlier.

What would change the view

  • Capital account history that supports a Fund I net TVPI close to the 2.1x in the letter.
  • Fund II exits outside healthcare that lift its gross MOIC without Rowan Veterinary well above 1.15x.
  • Fund II net IRR without the subscription line rising well above 3.4% as the portfolio matures.
  • A corrected DDQ on Fund II losses, with Varga and Osei both committed through the investment period.

Next steps

  1. Write to Hasturn Partners before the first close targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026 with three questions: Fund I net TVPI, Fund II realized losses and the key person departure.
  2. Request the Fund I capital account history and the Fund I and Fund II fee and carry terms.
  3. Ask the GP to reconcile the Fund II investment count in the 2024 letter and the Fund I DPI in the 2025 letter.
  4. Move the $25m to the secondaries commitment in the pacing plan.
  5. Track Fund II exits in each Hasturn letter ahead of a Fund IV review.
