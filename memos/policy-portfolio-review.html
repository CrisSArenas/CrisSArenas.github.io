Investment memo 05 · Policy portfolio

Endowment policy review

Date
30 September 2026
Portfolio
Fictional Endowment Fund
Strategy
Policy portfolio across eight asset classes, private assets included
Under review
$880.1m of assets; drafted trade lists T-1 ($7.0m) and T-2 (15.8% of the portfolio)
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Rebalance now. Release trade list T-1, which buys $7.0m of US equity with $4.7m of US Treasuries and $2.3m of cash, and keep T-2 blocked. Move the hedge fund overweight into international equity over the next two redemption windows.

Conditions

  • The policy register is updated first with the committee's 17 September changes, so T-1 is checked against the targets in force from 1 October 2026.
  • Staff query the custodian on the $10.0m gap before the next trade list.

Summary

This is the annual review of the endowment's policy portfolio, $880.1m by the sum of the custodian's lines. After the fall in public equity, US equity sits at 14.2%, below its 15% floor, while private equity and hedge funds sit above target at their latest marks. I support a small rebalance for four reasons.

  • US equity is outside its range, and IPS section 6 tells staff to rebalance to the nearest edge. T-1 does that with 0.8% turnover.
  • Active risk against the 70/30 reference portfolio is 4.31%, over the 4.0% budget. Most of it comes from the public equity underweight.
  • A full reoptimization adds little. The best Sharpe ratio is 0.26 against 0.25 today, reached by filling classes that cannot be traded to rebalance.
  • After a GFC-sized stress the portfolio still holds $416m of liquid assets against the $254m the IPS requires.

The portfolio

The Fictional Endowment Fund's Investment Policy Statement, adopted in March 2025, sets a target and range for each class. Liquid assets after a severe stress must cover 1.5 times the next 24 months of calls and spending. Breaches are rebalanced to the nearest range edge. Private equity, hedge funds and real estate are not traded to rebalance, and no trade list may move more than 10% of the portfolio.

On 17 September 2026 the committee cut the hedge funds target from 12% to 10% (range 5% to 14%) and raised US Treasuries from 20% to 22% (range 14% to 32%), effective 1 October 2026. Staff will report quarterly against a reference of 70% global equity and 30% bonds, with an active risk budget of 4% a year.

The custodian carries private equity and real estate at the latest general partner values and hedge funds at administrator estimates, so private weights after a fall are estimates. Against the updated targets, hedge funds at 13.9% sit just under the new 14% ceiling and private equity is at 19.2% against 16%.

Analysis

Optimal, current and policy

I ran a maximum Sharpe optimization inside the policy ranges, with liquidity as a hard constraint.

Policy6.56%11.79%0.24
Optimal6.34%10.01%0.26

The optimal mix has a simulated monthly VaR 95 of 4.1% and CVaR 95 of 5.2%, over 120 paths of 120 months. It puts private equity at 22.0% and hedge funds at 14.0%, both ceilings in classes staff cannot trade, and real estate at its 4% floor. The resampled frontier at the same risk holds 18.4% in US equity and 14.1% in private equity. A gain of 0.01 in Sharpe is inside the error of the return assumptions, so I use the optimizer only as a check on direction.

Black-Litterman views

I blended three views into the equilibrium returns. Emerging markets equity outperforms US equity by 2% at 50% confidence, private equity returns 7.5% after fees at 60%, and US Treasuries return 4.7% at 80%. The posterior puts private equity at 8.42% against 9.40% in the capital market assumptions, and international equity at 8.50% against 7.20%. That weakens the case for private equity at its ceiling and strengthens the case for adding international equity.

Private asset risk

Geltner desmoothing lifts private equity volatility from a reported 14.5% to 23.5%, real estate from 11.5% to 25.0% and hedge funds from 5.5% to 7.5%. The correlation of private equity with US equity rises from 0.45 to 0.72. On desmoothed numbers, private equity contributes 4.01% of the portfolio's 10.58% volatility, the largest share of any class.

Total portfolio approach

The 70/30 reference portfolio expects 6.24% at 11.5% volatility. The current portfolio expects 6.36% at 10.6%, so it earns 0.12 pp more with 4.31% of active risk, or 0.03 per unit. US equity contributes 1.69% of active risk, international equity 0.85% and emerging markets 0.27%; private equity adds 1.20% and real estate 0.44%.

Moving 3% from US Treasuries to US equity cuts active risk by 0.16 pp but adds 0.40 pp of volatility for 0.07 pp of return, below the portfolio's own 0.25 per unit of risk. Moving 2% from US equity into private equity adds 0.25 pp of active risk. T-1 is a smaller move in the first direction, and I support it as a range repair.

Attribution

Over the last 12 months the portfolio returned -2.00% against -2.94% for policy, an active return of +0.94 pp. Allocation added +1.12 pp, mostly the US equity underweight (56 bps) in a year when US equity fell 9.5%. Selection cost 0.20 pp. The same underweight that helped the return is what now breaks the active risk budget.

Portfolio fit

Stress tests

In a GFC-sized stress the current portfolio loses 24.5%, or $216m, with private assets shocked at economic values. The policy mix would lose 30.9% and the optimal mix 20.8%. The current portfolio loses less than policy in every scenario because it holds less public equity and more Treasuries.

Liquidity

The requirement is 1.5 times $169m, the $90m of projected calls over 24 months plus two years of spending at 4.5%, or $254m. After a GFC-sized shock the portfolio would hold $416m of liquid assets, 2.5 times calls and spending. The optimal mix keeps 51.0% of assets liquid after the same stress against 28.8% required. T-1 swaps Treasuries and cash for US equity, which trims the post-stress total, but at $7.0m the margin stays wide.

Regime view

The regime probabilities in this review are synthetic, drawn from a seeded process. Stagflation is the most likely regime at 84%. At a conviction of 0.50 the tilt trims US equity by 0.8 pp, to a tactical 23.2%, still far above the current 14.2%. The tilt does not change the direction of the rebalance, and I give it no weight in sizing.

Diligence findings

Staff read the IPS, the minutes and the custodian summary and tied every figure to its page and line. The review ran 58 checks and raised 4 breaks or flags, 3 sent for human review. Against verified fields, extraction accuracy was 100% on all 44, citation coverage was 100%, and 3 of 3 seeded issues were caught with no false flags.

  • The IPS register is out of date. The minutes change hedge funds and US Treasuries. The updated targets sum to 100.0% with every target inside its range.
  • The custodian total does not add up. The eight lines sum to $880.1m against a printed total of $890.1m. Weights here use the sum of the lines.
  • US equity at 14.2% is below its 15% floor, which is why T-1 was drafted.

Two trade lists were checked against the IPS. T-1 keeps post-trade weights inside every range and liquidity above the requirement, so it passed. T-2 rebalances every class to target and was blocked. It trades private equity, hedge funds and real estate, which section 6 excludes, and it moves 15.8% of the portfolio against the 10% limit. The audit log is intact.

What would change the view

  • The custodian confirms $890.1m as the true total. I would recompute the weights and resize T-1 before release.
  • The committee rejects the register update. Against a 12% hedge funds target the case for redeeming is weaker.
  • Liquid assets after a GFC-sized stress fall toward the $254m requirement, or projected calls rise well above $90m. I would slow the move out of Treasuries and cash.
  • Private equity marks come in above the 22% ceiling. That would need a pacing review.
  • Active risk remains above 4.0% in the first quarterly report after the hedge fund redemptions.

Next steps

  1. Approve the policy register update with the 17 September changes, effective 1 October 2026.
  2. Release T-1 today: buy $7.0m of US equity, sell $4.7m of US Treasuries and $2.3m of cash.
  3. Query the custodian on the $10.0m gap.
  4. Keep T-2 blocked. Staff to size hedge fund redemptions into international equity for the next two windows and measure active risk before each.
  5. Report against the 70/30 reference portfolio and the 4% budget from Q4.
  6. Bring the review of the reference portfolio approach to the next meeting.
