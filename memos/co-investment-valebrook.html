Investment memo 03 · Co-investment

Valebrook Lab Services

Date
May 2026
Sponsor
Quorvane Partners, alongside Quorvane Buyout IV
Strategy
Buyout co-investment, clinical lab services
Size offered
Up to $40m, no management fee and no carried interest
Prepared by
Cristian Arenas

Recommendation

Commit $15m of the $40m offered, alongside the endowment's $40m commitment to Quorvane Buyout IV. On my house case the deal returns 1.84x and 13.0% with no fee or carry, and the size keeps the downside loss to about $3.6m.

Conditions

  • A quality of earnings report that backs at least half of the $8.2m of 2025 add-backs.
  • Our commitment reaches Quorvane by the 14 July 2026 deadline in the CIM.

Summary

Quorvane Partners is buying Valebrook Lab Services, a clinical lab business, for $840m, 12.1x 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $69.4m. It offers up to $40m of fee-free co-investment beside Quorvane Buyout IV, where the endowment has $40m committed. Commitments are due 14 July 2026, ahead of a 28 July close.

The GP plan returns 3.75x. I do not believe it. My house case returns 1.84x and the downside 0.76x. The reasons for the recommendation:

  • The house case strips out what the company's history does not support: 7% growth against the CIM's 12%, one point of margin against 4.8, an exit one turn below entry and half the add-backs.
  • At 13.0% net it beats the 11.7% I expect from the fund, so it lifts the blend to 12.1%.
  • It passes all four triage checks, including 5.8x leverage on my view of EBITDA against a 6.0x limit.
  • The main risk is earnings quality, and the size and condition are set around it.

The asset

The company

Valebrook grew revenue from $241.0m in 2021 to $312.8m in 2025, 6.7% a year. Adjusted EBITDA went from $51.6m to $69.4m over the same years, and the margin moved by 0.8 points to 22.2%. Capex has run at 3.9% to 4.0% of revenue. The largest customer is 9% of 2025 revenue and the top ten are 38%. It is a steady business with no customer large enough to break it.

The sponsor

Quorvane Partners manages Quorvane Buyout IV, already in the portfolio through our $40m commitment. The fund takes 75% of the deal, so the GP's own vehicle carries most of the risk. The fund terms are a 1.5% fee and 20% carry over an 8% hurdle.

Deal terms

Net debt at close is $382m, 5.5x adjusted EBITDA. The price and leverage both depend on the $8.2m of add-backs. On my view, which accepts half of them, the price is 12.9x and leverage 5.8x. With none of them, the price is 13.7x and leverage 6.2x. The value creation plan lists three AI initiatives with a combined run-rate EBITDA uplift of about $10.5m.

Underwriting

The GP case takes the CIM projections at face value and holds the entry multiple. I give it little weight. The house case grows revenue at 7%, close to the 6.7% history, and adds one point of margin. Its exit margin of 21.9% sits below the reported 22.2% because it starts from EBITDA with only half the add-backs. It exits at 11.0x. Exit EBITDA is $102.2m and net debt falls to $249m.

Value creation bridge

In the house case equity goes from $475m to $875m. EBITDA growth adds $395m, the AI initiatives $80m and debt paydown $133m. The lower exit multiple takes $190m and costs take $17m. Most of the return comes from organic growth and debt paydown, which the history supports.

I weight the AI initiatives at 50% for sample routing, 70% for billing review and 60% for predictive maintenance. On those odds they are worth $29.2m, $33.6m and $10.4m of equity at the house entry multiple. At full odds they would be worth $124m. The GP case takes them at full odds and gets $127m from them in the bridge. In the downside they still add $45m, more than half of what EBITDA growth adds in that case, so the downside depends on them more than I would like.

Range of outcomes

I ran 2,000 paths around the house case, shocking growth by 2.5 points, exit margin by 1 point and the exit multiple by 1.2 turns. MOIC runs from 1.34x at the 10th percentile to 2.39x at the 90th. 1% of paths lose money. The shocks do not move the add-backs, so the simulation understates the downside that matters most here.

Portfolio fit

Fund and co-investment blend

The fund alone, at 2.00x gross, nets 1.74x and 11.7% after 0.26x of fees and carry. The co-investment at 1.84x gross is also 1.84x net and 13.0%. The deal is weaker than the fund before fees and better after them. At $15m beside $40m, a ratio of 0.375x, the blended net IRR is 12.1%, 0.4 points above the fund alone.

The sizing is about the downside. Taking the full $40m, a 1.00x ratio, lifts the house case blend only to 12.4%. In the downside the blend falls to 8.1% at $15m and to 4.6% at $40m. At $15m the downside costs about $3.6m on the check.

Exposure

Healthcare is 14% of private NAV today and 18% after a check of the full $40m offered, under the 22% cap. A $15m check adds less. The exposure is doubled in one name, since the endowment also owns Valebrook through Quorvane Buyout IV.

The other two deals in the queue, Wenlock Packaging and Tessaly Software, fail at least two triage checks each and are declined, so Valebrook does not compete with them for capital.

Diligence findings

I read the two-page CIM excerpt dated May 2026 and tied every one of the 21 extracted fields to its page and line. Extraction matched my hand labels on 21 of 21 fields, citation coverage was 100%, and all 3 planted issues were caught with no false flags. Six consistency checks ran. Three tie and three are flagged.

  • The projections grow revenue at 12.0% a year to 2030 and say the growth is organic, with no acquisitions. The company grew 6.7% a year from 2021 to 2025. Nothing in the excerpt explains the difference.
  • Adjusted EBITDA margin rises from 22.2% in 2025 to 27.0% in 2030, a 4.8 point gain against 0.8 points over the history, six times the pace. The AI initiatives add about $10.5m at run rate, roughly 1.9 points on 2030 revenue. That explains less than half the gain.
  • The $69.4m of 2025 adjusted EBITDA includes $8.2m of add-backs for one-time costs and pro forma savings. On $61.2m, net debt of $382m is 6.2x and the price 13.7x. That leverage would fail the 6.0x triage limit.

The entry multiple, the leverage arithmetic and customer concentration all tie. All three flags are reflected in the house case.

What would change the view

  • The quality of earnings report backs less than half of the add-backs. Leverage moves from 5.8x toward 6.2x and the price toward 13.7x. With none of them the deal fails the 6.0x triage limit. I would decline.
  • The quality of earnings work supports most of the add-backs and the GP explains the growth step-up with customer or site data. I would consider a larger check, keeping in mind that the blend gains only 0.3 points more at the full $40m.
  • House case MOIC falls below the fund's 1.74x net multiple. Below that level the co-investment lowers the blend.
  • The fund's share of the deal falls well below 75%, or the terms add a fee or carry. Either would fail the alignment check.

Next steps

  1. Send Quorvane the three CIM flags: the growth step-up, the margin path and the add-backs.
  2. Order the quality of earnings report, scoped to the $8.2m of add-backs.
  3. Ask for the debt terms and the evidence behind each AI initiative's run-rate uplift.
  4. Rerun the three cases with the confirmed add-backs and debt terms.
  5. If the condition is met, send the commitment to Quorvane before 14 July 2026.
