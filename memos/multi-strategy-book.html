Recommendation

Cut quant (Veyl Systematic) from 15% to 5% of the book and move the ten points to credit (Ostvale Credit). Hold relative value (Brennoch Relative Value) flat, with no additions, until its duration is back inside the mandate. Turn on volatility targeting and drawdown stops for every sleeve.

Conditions

  • Rerun the book on the new weights before the credit subscription goes in, and check the worst stress episode against today's 17.0%.
  • Brennoch gets a written request to bring its rates loading back under the 0.1 cap in its mandate.

Summary

This is the periodic review of our five-sleeve hedge fund book. The sleeves are weighted so each adds a fifth of the variance. The review covers ten years of monthly returns, July 2016 to June 2026, and the five sleeve mandates. The book returned 3.4% a year net, with 4.8% volatility, a Sharpe ratio of 0.31 and a maximum drawdown of 17.0%.

I am recommending a reallocation for these reasons.

  • Too much of the book sits on one momentum bet. Quant and equity long/short have shared it from the start. Macro has added a momentum overlay its mandate rules out, and macro and quant now correlate 0.69 in calm months, up from 0.13.
  • Quant is the weakest sleeve. It made 3.1% gross and 0.6% net a year on average, with a Sharpe ratio of −0.15 and a 36.7% drawdown, and it was the largest loser in the worst stress episode.
  • The diversification the book was sized on disappears in stress. Average correlation between sleeves goes from 0.10 in calm months to 0.47 in stress months.
  • Relative value has drifted outside its mandate on duration, and its pass-through expenses take most of its gross return. Adding to it now would reward both.

Track record

Book and sleeves

Over the ten years the book compounded at 3.4% net with 4.8% volatility. Macro carried the book with 6.6% net and a Sharpe ratio of 0.60, and the only small drawdown, 9.8%. Equity long/short (5.1%) and credit (3.9%) earned their risk share. Relative value made 1.2% and quant 0.1% net, both with negative Sharpe ratios. Together they hold 44% of the capital. Quant's 36.7% drawdown is the deepest in the book, ahead of equity long/short at 33.1%.

Calm and stress

In calm months the sleeves look close to independent, with an average correlation of 0.10. In the 18 stress months that rises to 0.47. With the same weights, book volatility is 3.3% on the calm covariance and 8.3% on the stress covariance. The diversification ratio falls from 1.88 to 1.32. Two stress pairs stand out. Equity long/short and quant correlate 0.67, and relative value and credit 0.76.

The worst stress episode ran 11 months from June 2017. Replayed on today's weights the book loses 17.0%. Quant contributed −5.5%, equity long/short −4.7%, credit −4.6% and relative value −3.9%. Macro made 0.4%. The episode matches the book's maximum drawdown.

Factor overlap and style drift

Equity long/short and quant load on momentum at 0.32 and 0.62 over the first seven years. Shared factors explain a correlation of 0.44 between them. Quillmere's mandate says it runs no systematic style tilts. Veyl's mandate names momentum. The other nine sleeve pairs show no shared factor bet.

Comparing the first seven years with the last 30 months, two sleeves changed style. Macro's momentum loading went from 0.10 to 0.79 (z 5.3). Relative value's rates loading went from 0.08 to 0.78 (z 5.8), against a mandate cap of 0.1. The other 23 sleeve and factor pairs were stable. Because macro now runs momentum too, its calm-month correlation with quant rose from 0.13 to 0.69 (Fisher z 3.0). That is a change in ordinary months, outside any crowding episode.

Fees

The sleeves made 6.4% gross and 3.5% net a year on average. Total fee drag is 295 bps a year, 46% of the gross return. Pass-through expenses cost 60 bps a year more than the standard 1.5% and 20% would on the same sleeves. Relative value goes from 5.2% gross to 1.4% net after a 3.50% pass-through. Quant goes from 3.1% to 0.6% after 1.00% in management fee and 1.50% in pass-through. Netting cost, the performance fees paid to winning sleeves in years when others lose, is only 6 bps a year.

Portfolio fit

Risk controls

Volatility targeting on its own lifts the book to 4.8% a year at the same 4.8% volatility, a Sharpe ratio of 0.59, and cuts the drawdown to 12.2%. Drawdown stops on their own give 3.9% and a 12.6% drawdown, with 7 stops fired. With both on, the book returns 4.8% with 4.7% volatility, a Sharpe ratio of 0.60 and a 10.2% drawdown, with 9 stops fired. I want both. The limits are real. Stops act after the loss and sell near the low when a crowded unwind snaps back. Volatility targeting uses a 12-month lookback, so it reacts late. The weights use the full ten-year covariance, so the base book is in-sample, though the controls do not look ahead.

Exposures and liquidity

The cut takes the book's largest momentum position down and removes the worst contributor to the stress episode. Credit is the cleanest sleeve to add to. It has calm-month correlations of 0.17 or less with every other sleeve, no drift flag and no shared factor bet. The trade-off is that credit and relative value correlate 0.76 in stress, and credit lost 4.6% in the worst episode. After the move those two sleeves will hold more than half the book. Liquidity also gets slower, since quant redeems monthly on 30 days notice and credit quarterly on 90. The demo does not show the book on the new weights, which is why the rerun is a condition.

Portable alpha overlay

The overlay tab runs at its own defaults over 180 months, with a generic alpha sleeve in place of this book. On that path portable alpha compounded at 3.97% against 5.67% for the 60/40 benchmark, with a 34.78% drawdown against 25.44% and an information ratio of −0.45. Across 96 seeds the median active return is +0.3%, p10 −2.0% and p90 +1.5%, and 60% of paths beat the benchmark. Collateral held up, with no margin-call months and a minimum cushion of 35.3% of NAV. I would not add an overlay on this evidence. This book was not tested as the alpha sleeve.

Diligence findings

I read the five sleeve mandates and extracted 26 fields: target volatility, fees, hurdles, pass-through and limits. Each one is tied to its page and line, and all 26 citations resolve. Against a hand-labeled answer key, extraction accuracy is 100% on 26 fields with 100% citation coverage. The review ran 71 checks and raised 4 flags, all sent for human review. It caught 4 of 4 planted issues with no false flags on 41 clean checks.

  • Global macro's momentum loading rose from 0.10 to 0.79. Line 5 of the Tarsen mandate rules out systematic trend following or a momentum overlay.
  • Relative value's rates loading rose from 0.08 to 0.78. Line 4 of the Brennoch mandate holds rates beta between −0.1 and 0.1.
  • Global macro and quant now correlate 0.69 in calm months, up from 0.13.
  • Equity long/short and quant run the same momentum bet. Line 5 of the Quillmere mandate says the sleeve has no systematic style tilts.

I approve all four. Two points need care when reading the outputs. The overlap table uses first-seven-year loadings, so it shows macro and quant with no shared bet (0.16 explained) while the correlation flag shows they now move together. Also, every sleeve shows a 20% risk share by construction, so the "largest share of risk" figure on equity long/short is a tie. The audit log has 7 entries and its hash chain is intact.

What would change the view

  • Brennoch's rates loading back under 0.1. Relative value could then return to its equal-risk weight.
  • Brennoch's rates loading still near 0.78 at the next review. I would redeem relative value at its quarterly window.
  • Tarsen's momentum loading back near its earlier 0.10. Macro is the best sleeve, with a 0.60 Sharpe ratio, and I would add to it ahead of credit.
  • The rerun on new weights showing a worst stress episode deeper than 17.0%, or credit and relative value together driving most of it. I would revisit the size of the credit add.
  • Average stress correlation moving above 0.47, or the calm-month average rising well above 0.10. Both would mean the book's diversification is thinner than sized.
  • A test of this book as the portable alpha sleeve showing a positive information ratio. I would then reopen the overlay.

Next steps

  3. Subscribe the ten points to Ostvale Credit once the rerun is reviewed.
  4. Write to Brennoch about the 0.78 rates loading against its 0.1 cap, and to Tarsen about the momentum overlay against line 5 of its mandate.
  5. Ask Quillmere to explain its 0.32 momentum loading against its mandate's no-tilt language.
  6. Turn on volatility targeting (12-month lookback, 2x cap) and drawdown stops (10% trigger, 50% allocation for six months).
