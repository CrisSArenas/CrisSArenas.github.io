Recommendation

I recommend we raise all three breaks with the GP and ask for a corrected Q2 2026 report. The two fee items come to $108,281 for our account, and the $1,236,187 carry accrual in Q2 2026 should be reversed. We should make no new commitment to Ostrel until the corrections are in.

Conditions for lifting the hold on new commitments

  • The GP credits the fee and offset differences against our next capital call.
  • The Q2 2026 report is reissued with accrued carry at $0 under the section 9.1 waterfall and restated net IRR and TVPI.

Summary

Ostrel Capital Partners VI is a $1,200m fund with an initial close on 15 October 2019. We hold a $45m commitment, which is almost fully called. I read the LPA excerpt and the four quarterly reports from Q3 2025 to Q2 2026, and recomputed every fee, offset and carry line against the LPA. Three of the 14 checks broke. All three favour the GP.

  • The Q3 2025 management fee was charged at 1.5% of total commitments when the LPA sets 1.5% of net invested capital after the investment period. The report shows $168,750 against $107,578, an overcharge of $61,172.
  • The Q1 2026 fee offset was about half of what section 8.2 requires: $47,109 against $94,219.
  • The Q2 2026 report accrues $1,236,187 of carry with no preferred return. Under the whole-fund waterfall nothing is due, and $7.05m of the 8% preferred return is still owed. Restated net IRR is 4.1% against the 3.3% reported.
  • Three errors in four reports, all in one direction, look like a review problem at the GP. The fund is also weak on its own merits, at 3.6% net once the subscription line comes out. That is why I would hold any new commitment to the manager until this is resolved.

The fund and our position

Terms in the LPA

The LPA excerpt is two pages. It sets a 2/20 structure with an 8% hurdle and a European waterfall. The fee basis steps down from commitments to net invested capital when the investment period ends on the fifth anniversary of the initial close, which was 15 October 2024. Net invested capital is the cost of portfolio investments not yet realized or written off, measured at the start of each quarter.

Our position

Per the Q2 2026 capital account we have contributed $44,631,563 and received $25,875,000 of distributions. NAV before accrued carry is $24,937,500. The GP reports a net TVPI of 1.11x and a DPI of 0.58x. Five deals are realized and seven remain. Fund net invested capital fell from $765m to $600m over the four quarters I reviewed. Recomputed net management fees since inception are $4.79m, or 10.7% of our commitment.

Team and edge

The documents do not describe the team or the investment strategy, so this review gives me no view on either. It answers a narrower question: whether the GP charges and reports what the LPA says.

Gross returns at deal level are 6.9% and 1.29x. That is a modest result for a fund in its seventh year. The GP's own net figures recompute exactly from its own inputs, so the arithmetic is consistent. The problem sits in one input, the carry accrual. Restating fees and carry to the LPA lifts net IRR from 3.3% to 4.1% and net TVPI from 1.11x to 1.14x.

Ostrel VI does not report IRR without its subscription line. I rebuilt it by moving each capital call back to the date of the investment it paid for, a lag of about 180 days in the ledger. Net IRR falls to 3.6%. The line adds 0.5 pp of IRR and leaves TVPI at 1.14x. With DPI at 0.58x, more than half of the value is still in NAV. I did not measure PME in this review.

European versus American carry

At reported NAV, LPs hold $50.81m of distributions and NAV against $44.52m contributed. The $44.52m is the report's $44.63m less the fee overcharge. The preferred return owed by now is $13.34m. A liquidation at NAV pays $6.29m of it and stops in tier (b) with $7.05m still owed. Nothing reaches the GP, so accrued carry under the LPA is $0.

The waterfall type matters a great deal here. If carry ran deal by deal, the GP would already have $1.42m on the five realized deals, with no netting against the weaker ones. I simulated the remaining exits over 2000 paths: the seven unrealized deals exit over the next four years at their marks grown at 6% a year with 30% volatility. Under the whole-fund waterfall the GP earns any carry in only 4% of paths. Median final carry is $0.00m under the LPA against $2.34m deal by deal, and the American figure is higher in 100% of paths. The Q2 2026 accrual ignores the term that protects us most.

Portfolio fit

The corrections are small against the position. The fee items are $108,281 against $24.94m of NAV. Reversing the carry accrual adds $1,236,187 back to our NAV and takes net TVPI from 1.11x to 1.14x. With $44.63m contributed on a $45m commitment, there is very little unfunded exposure left to Ostrel VI.

Fee drag is falling as deals are sold. The quarterly fee stepped down from $225,000 to $123,047 when the basis moved to net invested capital, and it was $84,375 by Q2 2026. Liquidity depends on the seven unrealized deals, which the simulation assumes exit over the next four years.

The demo covers this fund only. I have not measured the effect on pacing, exposures or manager concentration across our wider portfolio, and the hold on a new commitment to Ostrel should be read in that light.

Diligence findings

I read five documents and extracted 37 fields: 10 LPA terms and 27 report lines. Every figure is tied to its page and line, and all 37 citations resolve. Against hand-labeled ground truth, extraction accuracy is 100% and citation coverage is 100%. All 3 planted issues were caught with no false flags on the 11 clean checks, and 9 of 9 recomputed amounts match the hand-worked figures.

Break 1: Q3 2025 management fee

LPA 8.1 (p1 l6 and l9) against the Q3 2025 report (p1 l7). The investment period had ended, so the fee is 1.5% of net invested capital. The report gives $765m at the start of the quarter, which ties to the ledger, and our share is $107,578. The report charges $168,750, which is 1.5% of total commitments. The post-period rate was applied to the wrong base. Overcharge: $61,172.

Break 2: Q1 2026 fee offset

LPA 8.2 (p1 l12) against the Q1 2026 report (p1 l8 and l9). The fund received $2.8m of transaction and monitoring fees. Our share is $105,000, capped at the gross fee of $94,219. The report offsets $47,109, about 50% of what is owed. Under-offset: $47,110.

Break 3: Q2 2026 accrued carry

LPA 9.1(b) (p2 l4) and 9.4 (p2 l10) against the Q2 2026 report (p1 l21 and l22). The report accrues 20% of cumulative net gains, $6.18m of gains times 20%, with no preferred return. Section 9.4 requires the 9.1 waterfall at reported NAV, under which no carry is due. The accrual understates our NAV and the reported net TVPI.

What tied

Eleven checks tie. Net invested capital matches the ledger in all four quarters. The other fees and offsets recompute exactly. The GP's 1.11x TVPI and 0.58x DPI tie to its own capital account, which means the reported performance carries the wrong accrual through without a separate error.

Query letter

The letter is drafted from the approved breaks only, one point per break, each with the clause and report line. It asks the GP to confirm the Q3 2025 fee basis and credit $61,172, to explain the Q1 2026 offset and credit $47,110, and to reverse the Q2 2026 accrual and restate net performance. It asks that the fee items be credited against the next capital call. It has not been sent.

Scope limits

Side letters and MFN terms are out of scope because the LPA is an excerpt. Partnership expenses of $13,125 a quarter for our share flow through the cash flows but were not tested against a cap. I did not see reports before Q3 2025. The recomputed schedule charges the quarter ended 31 December 2024 at the full 2% rate, although the investment period ended on 15 October 2024. The excerpt does not say how that quarter should be split, so I would ask.

What would change the view

  • The GP credits $108,281 and reissues Q2 2026 with accrued carry at $0. The fee points would then be closed, and a new commitment could be judged on the merits of the fund.
  • The GP produces a reading of section 9.4 that supports the $1,236,187 accrual. I do not expect this, but it would need legal review before we push further.
  • The review of quarters since October 2024 turns up further breaks. A fourth error in the GP's favour would move me from a hold on new commitments to a wider review of the relationship.
  • Exits lift the whole fund past the preferred return. LPs are still owed $7.05m of pref at reported NAV, so a carry accrual only becomes legitimate once that is cleared.
  • Evidence that carry was paid on realized deals. That would turn the $1.42m deal-by-deal figure into a clawback claim.

Next steps

  1. Approve the three breaks and send the query letter, after fixing its fee total to $108,281 so it matches the fee table.
  2. Ask for a corrected Q2 2026 report with accrued carry under the section 9.1 waterfall and restated net IRR, TVPI and DPI.
  3. Ask for the fee basis on every quarter since October 2024, including how the quarter ended 31 December 2024 was charged.
  4. Ask the GP to confirm no carry has been distributed and to report IRR without the subscription line.
  5. Request the full LPA and side letters.
  6. Audit the Fund VII reports next quarter.
