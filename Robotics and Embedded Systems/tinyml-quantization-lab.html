Guide — TinyML Model Quantization Lab

1 · What this lab does

It takes four representative TinyML models, quantises their weights the way TensorFlow Lite Micro does, and then budgets the result against a real microcontroller: flash for the weights, SRAM for the tensor arena, and cycles for the inference. Weights are drawn from a seeded generator with layer-appropriate statistics — they are not trained weights — but every number derived from them (scales, quantisation error, SQNR, model size, arena size, latency) is computed live from the same arithmetic a real toolchain uses.

2 · The quantisation arithmetic

Weights — symmetric, per output channel. For each output channel c: s_c = max|w_c| / 127 (INT8) or / 7 (INT4), zero-point is exactly 0. Quantise with q = clamp(round(w / s_c), -127, +127), dequantise with ŵ = q · s_c, so dequantisation is the exact inverse of the rounding step. A symmetric zero-point is what lets CMSIS-NN skip the cross-term in the accumulator.

Activations — asymmetric, per tensor. From a calibration pass: s = (max − min) / 255, z = round(−min / s). Post-ReLU tensors have min = 0, so z = 0; tensors that can go negative (the input MFCC/image, the logits) get a non-zero zero-point. The lab shows the uint8 convention; int8 kernels use the same scale with the zero-point shifted by −128.

Clip percentile. The slider replaces max|w_c| with the p-th percentile of |w_c|: outliers then saturate at ±127 while every other weight gets a finer step. Drag it and watch SQNR fall — for weights that trade almost always loses, which is exactly why TensorFlow Lite calibrates weights with plain max-abs (p = 100%) and reserves percentile and KL calibration for activation ranges, where one outlier frame really can wreck the scale. The reported accuracy drop is clamped to the INT8/INT4 bands, so below roughly 99.4% it stops moving even though the SQNR keeps dropping.

3 · Size, arena and latency

Model size
params × bytes-per-weight + 8 × output-channels + 8 × tensors. FP32 is 4 B/weight, INT8 1 B, INT4 0.5 B (two nibbles per byte). The overhead is the int32 multiplier and shift stored per channel plus the scale/zero-point per tensor, which is why the size ratio is a little under 4× and 8×.
Tensor arena
max over operators of (input tensor + output tensor) + scratch, where scratch is 2 KB of interpreter bookkeeping plus 8% for the CMSIS-NN im2col buffer. Buffers are reused, so the peak activation pair — not the sum of all activations — sets the requirement. Under FP32 the arena is 4× larger, which is usually what actually blocks deployment.
Latency
An explicit MAC-throughput model: cycles = MACs / (r · e) with r = 0.32 MAC/cycle for FP32 (FPU present but load/store bound), 1.55 for INT8 (SMLAD dual-MAC), 0.50 for INT4 (no native kernel — nibbles are unpacked to int8 first). e is a kernel-efficiency factor: 1.0 for regular and 1×1 convolutions, 0.35 for depthwise (one MAC per weight load), 0.80 for fully connected (memory bound). 10 µs per operator is added for setup and requantisation.

4 · Accuracy degradation

Not a lookup table: the drop is derived from the quantisation noise actually present in the sampled weights. For each layer the relative noise power Σ(w − ŵ)² / Σw² is weighted by parameter count and by a sensitivity factor — 2.5× for the first convolution (few parameters, wide dynamic range, every later layer inherits its error), 2.0× for the final classifier (its errors land directly on the logits), 1.5× for depthwise layers (per-channel ranges vary by orders of magnitude), 1.0× elsewhere. The aggregate maps to a percentage-point drop and is clamped to the empirically observed bands: 0.3–1.5 pp for INT8, 3–10 pp for INT4.

6 · Reading the panels

  • Heatmap — one row per sampled output channel, one column per sampled weight in that channel. Because quantisation is per channel, each row has its own scale; hover any cell for the FP32 value, its dequantised value and the error.
  • Histogram — the FP32 distribution (outline) against the quantised one (filled). INT8 leaves 254 usable levels and the shape survives; INT4 leaves 14 and the distribution collapses into visible spikes.
  • Trade-off chart — accuracy bars against size and latency for all three precisions of the current model on the current target.
  • Target panel — flash and SRAM budgets with a pass/fail margin. Change the target to see the same model fit or fail.

How This Lab Works

STEP 1

Pick a model and a target

Choose one of four real TinyML workloads and an MCU. Parameter counts, MAC counts and activation shapes come from the actual layer geometry, so the budgets add up.

STEP 2

Quantise

Switch between FP32, INT8 and INT4. Weights use symmetric per-channel scales; the heatmap, histogram, MSE and SQNR all recompute from the same arithmetic.

STEP 3

Check the fit

Flash holds the weights, SRAM holds the tensor arena. The margin bars show pass or fail — try MobileNet in FP32 on the STM32L4 to see what actually blocks deployment.

STEP 4

Flash and run

Flash the model, then run inference. The MNIST CNN classifies a digit you draw; the other three report a seeded example. Latency comes from the stated MAC-throughput model.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

Post-training quantisation of four representative TinyML models and the deployment budget that follows from it. Weight tiles are sampled from a seeded generator with layer-appropriate statistics (depthwise kernels wider than pointwise, the first convolution carrying outliers), then quantised with symmetric per-output-channel INT8/INT4 scales and dequantised exactly. From those tiles the lab computes per-layer quantisation MSE and SQNR, an accuracy drop weighted by layer sensitivity, model size including per-channel scale overhead, the peak tensor arena, and an inference-latency estimate from an explicit MAC-throughput model for the selected microcontroller. Flash and SRAM budgets are shown as pass/fail margin bars.

Key assumptions

  • Weights: symmetric per channel, s_c = P·max|w_c| / 127 (INT8) or / 7 (INT4), zero-point 0, clamped to ±127 / ±7. P is the clip percentile (default 100%).
  • Activations: asymmetric per tensor, s = (max − min)/255, z = round(−min/s). Post-ReLU tensors give z = 0; input and logit tensors do not.
  • Size overhead: 8 bytes per output channel (int32 multiplier + shift) and 8 bytes per tensor (activation scale + zero-point), so the reduction is slightly under 4× / 8×.
  • Arena = max(input + output tensor) over operators + 2 KB interpreter bookkeeping + 8% CMSIS-NN scratch. Activations are int8 when quantised, float32 under FP32.
  • Latency at 0.32 MAC/cycle FP32, 1.55 INT8, 0.50 INT4, scaled by kernel efficiency (conv/pointwise 1.0, depthwise 0.35, fully connected 0.80) and 10 µs setup per operator. INT4 has no native CMSIS-NN kernel; nibbles are unpacked to int8 first, so it is slower than INT8 per MAC.
  • Default target STM32F407VG: Cortex-M4F at 168 MHz, 1 MB flash, 128 KB SRAM1/2 plus 64 KB CCM (CCM excluded from the arena budget because it is not DMA-accessible). Bootloader 16 KB, app + TFLite Micro + CMSIS-NN 96 KB, static RAM 24 KB.
  • Baselines: DS-CNN-S 94.4% (Speech Commands, 12 classes), MobileNetV1 0.25 96×96 84.9% (Visual Wake Words), autoencoder 0.889 AUC (MIMII-style), MNIST CNN 99.1%.
  • Everything is driven by a single seed (1337); every control re-runs the whole pipeline, so results are reproducible.

Formulas & references

  • Quantise q = clamp(round(w / s_c), −q_max, q_max); dequantise ŵ = q · s_c — the exact inverse of the rounding step.
  • MSE = mean((w − ŵ)²); SQNR = 10·log10(Σw² / Σ(w − ŵ)²) dB.
  • Accuracy drop = 21.5 · N^0.35 · k with N the parameter- and sensitivity-weighted relative noise power (first conv 2.5×, final dense 2.0×, depthwise 1.5×) and k a per-model robustness factor; clamped to 0.3–1.5 pp (INT8) and 3–10 pp (INT4).
  • bytes = params · bpw + 8·channels + 8·tensors; cycles = Σ MACs / (r · e) + 10µs·f.
  • Jacob et al. (2018), Quantization and Training of Neural Networks for Efficient Integer-Arithmetic-Only Inference; Krishnamoorthi (2018), Quantizing deep convolutional networks for efficient inference; Zhang et al. (2017), Hello Edge: Keyword Spotting on Microcontrollers; Lai, Suda & Chandra (2018), CMSIS-NN; Banbury et al. (2021), MLPerf Tiny Benchmark.

Limitations

  • Weights are generated, not trained. Real weight distributions are not Gaussian and per-channel ranges differ far more than here, so real SQNR spreads are wider.
  • The accuracy model is a calibrated mapping from noise power to percentage points, not an evaluation on a test set. It reproduces the right bands and the right ordering, nothing finer.
  • The latency model counts MACs only: no cache or flash-wait-state stalls, no ping-pong buffer copies, no operator fusion. Measured TFLite Micro figures typically run 1.5–3× slower than this estimate.
  • Only a tile of each layer is sampled for the heatmap and histogram, so tile statistics are not full-layer statistics.
  • Quantisation-aware training, mixed precision, weight clustering, pruning and sparse kernels are out of scope; so is int16 activation mode.
  • The clip-percentile slider is applied to weights to show why max-abs is the right default there. Real percentile and KL calibration operate on activation ranges, which this lab treats as fixed per layer.
  • The MNIST classifier is a nearest-template matcher over eight stroke templates plus a seeded confusion model, not a real forward pass through the listed layers.
Browser simulation for education. Timings, register values and sensor models are idealised approximations and are not hardware-validated.
