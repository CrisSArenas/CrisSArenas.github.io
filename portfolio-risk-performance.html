How it works

Method, formulas, assumptions and limits
  • Monthly returns come from a five-factor model (equity, rates, credit, inflation and currency). Each manager adds its own alpha and specific risk on top of its factor betas.
  • Private equity, private credit and real assets report smoothed marks. Before they enter the risk model I estimate each series' first-order autocorrelation and desmooth it with the Geltner filter.
  • Betas and specific risk come from a regression of each manager's monthly returns on the five factors. The covariance matrix is the factor covariance mapped through the betas, plus specific variance.
  • Risk contributions use the Euler split. Each manager's weight times its marginal risk gives its share, and the shares add up to total volatility. Ex-ante tracking error is the volatility of the active weights (portfolio minus policy benchmark) in the same model, and it splits the same way. In the manager table each manager carries its share of the class benchmark, pro rata to its weight in the class, so an index fund shows close to zero.
  • Stress tests apply the factor moves of three episodes to the current betas. Private assets are shocked at their desmoothed betas.
  • The portfolio and the policy benchmark both start each year on 1 October from fixed weights and are held between those dates. For the liquid sleeves that is the annual rebalance. For the private sleeves it stands in for a year of capital calls, distributions and commitment pacing, since private funds cannot be traded back to weight. Holding the weights through the year makes each year's attribution add up exactly to the year's active return.
  • Attribution is Brinson-Fachler by asset class. A class's allocation effect is its active weight times its benchmark return less the total benchmark return, so an overweight in a class that lagged the benchmark counts against the fund. Selection uses policy weights and interaction is shown on its own.

Assumptions

Data and limits

  • The fund, its managers and every return are made up. Each private line is a separate account spread across many funds or deals. The policy benchmark indices are modelled as exact combinations of the five factors, so all specific risk sits with the managers.
  • Private equity is measured against global equity plus a spread, with no lag. Many funds lag that benchmark by a quarter to match statement timing. The synthetic marks here are monthly and trail the true values by about a month, so a quarter's lag would overcorrect.
  • Sixty months is a short window for a five-factor regression. The betas of the private managers are the least certain numbers on the page.
  • Geltner desmoothing assumes one lag of smoothing. Marks that lag by more than a quarter still understate risk after the filter.
  • The stress tests are linear in the betas. They do not capture forced selling or the slow repricing of private marks after a fall.
