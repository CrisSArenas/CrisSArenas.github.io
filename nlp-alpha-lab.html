Guide — NLP Earnings Sentiment Alpha Lab

1 · What this lab does

It runs a complete, if miniature, text-to-portfolio pipeline. A finance sentiment lexicon in the Loughran–McDonald style scores every word of a synthetic earnings-call transcript; sentence tone is aggregated with section weights into a document tone in −1…+1; that tone is z-scored against a stylised cross-section and blended with three other signal z-scores into a composite; the composite’s information coefficient then drives a 60-month long/short overlay backtest net of transaction costs. Every transcript, name and number on this page is synthetic.

2 · How the words are scored (this is the actual code)

The transcript is tokenised on whitespace and each token is looked up in a hand-built finance lexicon of roughly 200 terms with polarities in −3…+3. Two modifiers are applied, exactly as shown in the heatmap:

  • Negation — if a negator (no, not, never, without, fails, despite…) appears within the three preceding tokens, the polarity is flipped. Loughran–McDonald negate positives only; this demo flips both directions, so “no sign of softening” scores positive.
  • Amplifiers / diminishers — significantly, dramatically, overwhelmingly multiply the next scored token by 1.3–1.6; slightly, modestly, somewhat by 0.5–0.7. Scores are clipped to ±3.

Sentence tone = tanh( Σ wi / (3·√n) ) over the n tokens of the sentence, so a long neutral sentence cannot be dominated by one word. Document tone = tanh( 1.5 · Σs ωs·raws / Σs ωs ) with section weights ω = 0.8 (CEO prepared remarks), 1.0 (CFO prepared remarks) and 1.4 (analyst Q&A) — unscripted answers carry more information than a scripted script. Hover any highlighted word to see its lexicon polarity, whether it was negated, the multiplier applied and the resulting score. There is no transformer here and none is claimed.

3 · From tone to a tradable signal

Document tone is standardised against a stylised cross-section (mean +0.10, s.d. 0.28 — earnings-call language is positively skewed) to give zNLP. The other three signal z-scores are fixed synthetic values stored with each transcript. The composite is zc = Σ wi zi / √(w’Cw), so it is itself unit-variance, and its information coefficient follows the same Grinold–Kahn combination rule ICc(h) = Σ wi ICi(h) / √(w’Cw). Weights are yours to set and are renormalised to sum to 1; C is the assumed signal correlation matrix (0.10–0.35). Because the signals are correlated the composite beats the best single signal only modestly — about 0.048 against 0.045 at the default settings.

4 · Signal decay and the holding period

Each signal’s IC decays exponentially in the forward holding period h: ICi(h) = ICi(1) · 2−(h−1)/HLi.

5 · The backtest

The overlay is sized to a constant 4.5% tracking error. Gross skill uses the fundamental law with a transfer coefficient: IRgross = TC · IC(h) · √(Neff · 252/h) with TC = 0.45 and Neff = 120 effectively independent bets per rebalance — far below the nominal universe size, because cross-sectional bets share common factor exposure. Costs are real: two-way annual turnover is 2κ·252/h and the cost per unit of turnover is 8 bp + 6 bp·√(f/12), a square-root market-impact term that penalises fast trading. Net alpha = gross alpha − cost, and net is always below gross. Trading faster raises IC but raises costs faster still, so net alpha peaks around a two-to-three-week horizon and turns negative below about a week.

Monthly active returns are drawn as shuffled normal quantiles standardised to exactly the modelled mean and volatility, so the realised annual alpha, tracking error, information ratio and hit rate in the table match the model rather than drifting with sampling noise, while the path — and therefore the max drawdown — stays a genuine random walk from the seed.

7 · Controls

Transcript
Four synthetic calls from fictional issuers. Changes the heatmap, sentence tone, document tone, composite z, sector map and the highlighted scatter point. It does not change the backtest: IC is a universe-level statistic and cannot be re-estimated from one call.
Signal toggles
Drop a signal from the composite. At least one must stay on.
Weights
Raw sliders renormalised to sum to 1. They move the composite IC, the composite turnover (and therefore the cost drag), the half-life and every backtest number.
Holding period
Forward horizon in trading days, 5–63. Drives IC decay, breadth, turnover and cost.

How This Lab Works

Select Transcript

Read the Heatmap

Combine Signals

Watch gross alpha, transaction costs and net alpha trade off as you change the holding period. Net is always below gross.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

A text-to-portfolio pipeline on synthetic earnings-call transcripts written for this demo, attributed to fictional issuers. A hand-built finance lexicon in the Loughran–McDonald style scores each token in −3…+3, applying a three-token negation window and amplifier/diminisher multipliers. Sentence tone is tanh(Σw/(3√n)); document tone is a section-weighted mean (CEO prepared 0.8, CFO prepared 1.0, analyst Q&A 1.4) squashed to −1…+1. That tone is z-scored and combined with analyst-revision, options-flow and supply-chain z-scores into a unit-variance composite, whose information coefficient drives a 60-month long/short overlay backtest sized to a constant tracking error and charged realistic transaction costs.

Key assumptions

  • Signal IC at a one-day horizon and half-life: NLP 0.052 / 28 d, analyst revisions 0.058 / 55 d, options flow 0.049 / 18 d, supply chain 0.031 / 70 d. At the default 21-day horizon these are 0.032, 0.045, 0.023 and 0.025.
  • Assumed signal correlations 0.10–0.35 (NLP–analyst 0.35 is the highest). At equal weights the composite IC is about 0.048 against 0.045 for the best single signal.
  • Overlay sized to 4.5% tracking error; transfer coefficient 0.45; Neff = 120 effectively independent bets per rebalance.
  • Two-way annual turnover 2κ·252/h with κ of 0.65 / 0.35 / 0.85 / 0.30 by signal (0.25 for the traditional-factor sleeve); cost per unit turnover 8 bp + 6 bp·√(f/12), i.e. about 14 bp at monthly rebalancing.
  • Benchmark 7.0% arithmetic return, 15.5% volatility; cross-sectional tone distribution mean +0.10, s.d. 0.28; cross-sectional return dispersion 0.35·√(h/252).
  • Defaults: equal 25% weights, 21-day horizon, all four signals on, seed 20260421.

Formulas & references

  • ICi(h) = ICi(1)·2^(−(h−1)/HLi); composite ICc = ΣwiICi / √(w’Cw), weights renormalised to Σw = 1.
  • Fundamental law with transfer coefficient: IRgross = TC·IC(h)·√(Neff·252/h); gross alpha = IR·TE; net = gross − turnover·cost rate; IRnet = net / TE.
  • Max drawdown on the cumulative total-return path; hit rate = share of months with positive active return; sample IC on the scatter is constructed exactly by orthogonalising the noise against the score.
  • Loughran & McDonald (2011), When Is a Liability Not a Liability? Textual Analysis, Dictionaries, and 10-Ks; Grinold & Kahn (2000), Active Portfolio Management, chs. 6 and 15; Clarke, de Silva & Thorley (2002), Portfolio Constraints and the Fundamental Law.

Limitations

  • The transcripts are invented and short. A bag-of-words lexicon has no syntax, no coreference and no numeric reasoning; a fine-tuned encoder would beat it on real filings.
  • The lexicon is hand-built (~200 terms) and inspired by Loughran–McDonald rather than being it; the published dictionary is far larger and is not redistributed here.
  • Signal ICs, correlations, transfer coefficient and breadth are assumed, not estimated. Nothing is fitted to data, so there is no out-of-sample claim to make.
  • The backtest is a single overlay path with Gaussian active returns and no factor model, no borrow cost, no capacity limit, no crowding and no decay of the signals themselves over calendar time.
  • Backtest results are deliberately independent of the selected transcript: one call cannot re-estimate a universe-level IC.
  • Sector read-across uses hand-set linkage betas and is illustrative only.
