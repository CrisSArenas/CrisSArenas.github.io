02 · Private capital program

Private Capital Lab

A fictional $1bn endowment runs a program of twenty private funds. Its NAVs arrive a quarter late, public markets have just fallen, and next year's commitment budget is due. This lab does the gathering, nowcasting, pacing and stress testing. The commitment is the committee's call.

Private marketsAI
Decision it supports
How much to commit this year, and to what.
The hard problem
Pacing a program across a portfolio of funds on stale NAVs and uncertain calls, without a liquidity squeeze.
What the system takes off the team
Reading GP statements into a reconciled, cited ledger; nowcasting stale NAVs; projecting calls, distributions and the allocation under thousands of paths.
What stays with the team
How much to commit this year, and to what.
Method
Takahashi-Alexander cash flow model, a pacing rule to target, quarterly Monte Carlo, NAV nowcasting on a lagged public proxy, IRR, TVPI, DPI, KS-PME, Direct Alpha, Geltner desmoothing.
Data
Synthetic data, fixed seed, fictional funds and GPs. Or your own file, processed locally.
Where AI is used
Document classification, statement extraction with citations, a citation validator and a reconciliation agent, with human approval of every break.
Limitations
Model parameters are assumptions, not estimates from real funds. The pipeline is a deterministic simulation; no model is called.

        Methodology, assumptions and limitations

        Method

        • Fund model. Takahashi and Alexander (2002): calls are a fixed share of unfunded commitment during the investment period, NAV compounds at the growth rate, distributions are a rising share of NAV with a bow factor and a yield floor. The extracted code reproduces the original pacing panel exactly (tests/legacy-proof.js).
        • Pacing rule. Every fund, existing and planned, follows the Takahashi-Alexander rules from its current state. Each year the plan finds, by bisection, the level commitment that puts the projected private share on target h years ahead, given the commitments already planned.
        • Monte Carlo. The same fund rules at quarterly steps, with lognormal shocks: public returns, private NAV returns (beta to the public shock plus idiosyncratic noise), call rates and distribution rates (higher after good markets) drawn jointly. With every volatility set to zero it tracks the deterministic plan (tested). Spending is paid from public assets.
        • Nowcast. Reported quarterly NAV returns are regressed on the public proxy at lags 0, 1 and 2. The lagged betas catch up the part of past market moves the mark has not yet absorbed; their sum, the economic beta, rolls the NAV through each stale quarter with the cash flows known since. Alpha is set to zero.
        • Performance. XIRR on dated quarterly flows, TVPI, DPI, RVPI, Kaplan-Schoar PME and Direct Alpha against the public proxy, Geltner desmoothing at the measured first-order autocorrelation.

        Formulas

        C_t = RC x unfunded (t <= IP) NAV_t = (NAV_t-1 + C_t)(1 + G) D_t = max(Y, (t/L)^B) x NAV_t nowcast_k = (nowcast_k-1 + calls_k - dists_k)(1 + B r_proxy,k), B = b0 + b1 + b2 KS-PME = (sum D I_T/I_t + NAV_T) / sum C I_T/I_t r_true = (r_obs - rho r_obs,t-1) / (1 - rho)

        Limitations

        • All funds, GPs, documents and the endowment are fictional, and every number is synthetic. Parameters are plausible, not estimated from real funds.
        • The fund model is deterministic per vintage; real programs have fund-specific pacing, recallable capital and extensions.
        • The nowcast assumes the fund's sensitivity to the public proxy is stable. Stale marks in a fast market are exactly when that assumption is weakest, which is why the band widens with every stale quarter.
        • The pipeline is a simulation over text documents. It shows the control flow, citations, validation, approvals and logging, not a language model's accuracy on real PDFs.

        Not investment advice. Educational demonstration with synthetic data and fictional entities.
