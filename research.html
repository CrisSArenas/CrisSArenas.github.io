Working paper · SSRN · 2026

Regime-Aware Transformer Architectures with Cross-Attention Regime Discovery for Multi-Asset Portfolio Construction

Cristian Miguel Sanchez Arenas · Independent Researcher

SSRN 6517438 26 pages 9 baselines + oracle 3 DGPs × 3 seeds 10,000-rep bootstrap

Traditional regime-aware allocation runs in two stages: classify the market regime, then optimize the portfolio conditional on that label. The discrete label between the stages throws away uncertainty and transition dynamics, and the classifier is optimizing the wrong objective. This paper proposes Cross-Attention Regime Discovery, which replaces that interface: learnable regime queries attend over a transformer's temporal representation to produce soft regime assignments and regime-specific allocation heads, trained end to end against a single portfolio objective. It is then evaluated honestly, across three data generating processes, three seeds, ten alternatives and a block bootstrap, and the result is not a clean win.

Neural > traditional
Every neural allocator beats every classical baseline on all three processes, mostly by taking less risk
No architecture wins
A 16k-parameter LSTM matches or beats the transformer; zero pairwise differences reach significance
The regime paradox
The mixture model identifies regimes far better (ARI 0.59 vs 0.03) and allocates far worse (0.44 vs 0.82)
Constraints pay
Position bounds cost raw Sharpe but halve turnover against the two-stage pipeline

Research areas

Where the work sits, and where it is going next.

End-to-end allocation

Training a single model against a portfolio objective instead of chaining a forecast, a classifier and an optimizer, each optimizing something different.

Regime representation

Soft, learned regime structure rather than discrete labels, and the open question of what a portfolio-relevant regime actually is.

Interpretability

Attention over the lookback window as an explanation of which observations drive a regime, so a risk committee can interrogate the model.

Honest evaluation

Multiple data generating processes, multiple seeds, strong baselines, bootstrap intervals, and reporting the null result when that is the result.

Cost-aware construction

Turnover and transaction costs inside the objective, not bolted on afterwards, so stability is rewarded during training.

Real-market validation

Taking the same protocol to liquid ETF universes with rolling out-of-sample windows. In progress.

Method & reproducibility

The standards every study on this page is held to.

Controlled seeds

Python, NumPy and PyTorch seeds are fixed, deterministic mode is enabled, and every configuration runs across multiple seeds. Results are reported as mean and standard deviation, never as a single favourable run.

Strong baselines

Equal weight, minimum variance with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, risk parity, hierarchical risk parity, a two-stage mixture-model pipeline, and neural alternatives. Plus an infeasible oracle that receives the true regime labels as an upper reference.

Statistical testing

Sharpe ratio differences are tested with a circular block bootstrap that respects serial dependence. Confidence intervals are reported in full, including the wide ones.

Costs and turnover

Every performance claim is repeated after proportional transaction costs, and turnover is reported so the reader can apply their own cost assumption.

Stated limitations

Synthetic data is labelled as synthetic. Where a study cannot support a claim, it says so. Negative and null results are published rather than dropped.

Reproducible pipeline

One pipeline regenerates every table and figure in the paper from the configuration files, so a reviewer can rerun the study rather than trust the write-up.

On synthetic data. The current paper is a controlled methodological study. Ground truth regimes are known, which allows evaluation that real market data does not permit, and it is the only way to ask whether a model recovers the structure it claims to. It also means the results are not evidence of real-world portfolio performance, and should not be read that way. Real-market validation is the next study, not a footnote to this one.
