How an endowment actually sizes private assets. The illiquidity budget is set first, from spending and liquidity needs, and the optimiser then works inside it. Private marks are de-smoothed before they enter the covariance matrix, and each asset class carries an investment-policy range, so the solver cannot park the fund in whatever has the best reported Sharpe. The adopted portfolio is the lowest-risk mix that still clears spending plus inflation.

Guide — Multi-Asset Portfolio Optimizer

1 · How to drive it

1. Set the seed, risk-free rate and constraints on the left. 2. Move the weight sliders or load a preset. 3. Press Optimize to solve the frontier and click any result to apply it. Weights always rescale to 100%; the L toggle locks an asset so rescaling leaves it alone.

2 · Capital market assumptions

Long-horizon, arithmetic, nominal USD assumptions in the spirit of published 2026 CMAs. Cash always returns the risk-free rate you set, so raising Rf lowers every Sharpe ratio and shifts the tangency portfolio.

3 · Covariance and the optimizer

Σ = D C D,  D = diag(σ),  C symmetric with unit diagonal (min eigenvalue 0.14 ⇒ Σ is positive semi-definite)
μp = w'μ  ·  σp = √(w'Σw)  ·  Sharpe = (μp − Rf) / σp

The frontier is not the textbook closed form w ∝ Σ−11: that ignores every constraint. Each frontier point solves

maxw  λ μ'w − w'Σw   s.t.   Σwi = 1,   0 ≤ wi ≤ cap,   policy bands

by projected gradient ascent, sweeping the risk-aversion parameter λ from 0 (minimum variance) to a level where the return term dominates (maximum return corner). The projection is the exact Euclidean projection onto the capped simplex, alternated with the group constraints. Sweeping λ traces exactly the set of portfolios that minimise variance for their own level of return, so the curve is monotone and no random portfolio can sit above it.

Min variance is λ = 0. Max Sharpe is refined with a direct ascent on the Sharpe gradient μ/σ − (μ'w−Rf)Σw/σ³. Target vol is the frontier point whose volatility is closest to your slider; if the target is below the constrained minimum-variance point the optimizer says so instead of pretending.

4 · Constraints

Always on: long-only, a per-asset ceiling, and the illiquidity budget, which weights private equity and real estate at 1.0 and hedge funds at 0.5 for gates and lock-ups. With policy bands ticked each class also carries its own IPS range and the portfolio must hold at least 30% listed equity and 50% growth assets. Floors are clipped automatically if a tight ceiling or a small budget would otherwise make the feasible set empty. All of these sets are convex, so the solver reaches the intersection by alternating projections: an exact projection onto the box with per-class minimums and maximums, and Euclidean projections onto each halfspace.

5 · Monte Carlo and risk metrics

200 seeded paths of 180 monthly returns. Each month draws correlated standard normals through the Cholesky factor of C and maps them to log-normal asset returns with r = exp(m − ½s² + s·z) − 1, calibrated so that E[r] = μ/12 exactly. Wealth compounds multiplicatively, so a −50% month cannot be undone by a +50% month.

  • VaR / CVaR — empirical 5% and 1% quantiles of the pooled 36,000 monthly portfolio returns, 1-month horizon, reported as positive loss numbers (VaR 95% = 4.8% means a 5% chance of losing at least 4.8% in a month). CVaR is the mean of the returns beyond the quantile.
  • Max drawdown — worst peak-to-trough fall of each simulated wealth path; the tile shows the median across the 200 paths and the tooltip the 95th percentile.
  • Worst 12m — 5th percentile across paths of that path's worst rolling 12-month return.
  • Sortino = (μp − Rf) / target semi-deviation below the monthly Rf, annualised. Calmar = geometric return / median max drawdown.
  • Tracking error, information ratio, beta, active share are all measured against the 60/40 (60% US equity / 40% Treasuries) benchmark on the displayed path; active share is ½Σ|wi − wb,i|.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

A constrained mean–variance optimizer over an eight-asset endowment universe, set up the way an investment committee actually runs the decision rather than as a textbook exercise. Three things follow from that. The illiquidity budget is an input, not an output: it is chosen from spending and liquidity needs and the optimiser solves inside it. Private marks are de-smoothed before they reach the covariance matrix, using the reported series' own autocorrelation, because appraisal-based volatility understates economic risk and would otherwise flatter every private allocation. And each class carries an investment-policy range, since no committee hands a solver a single global cap and accepts whatever corner it finds. The portfolio presented as adopted is the least-risk point on the frontier that still clears spending plus assumed inflation, which is the hurdle a perpetual pool has to beat to hold its purchasing power.

Key assumptions

  • Arithmetic nominal CMAs: US equity 6.8%/16.0%, intl equity 7.2%/17.5%, EM equity 7.9%/21.5%, private equity 9.4%/23.0%, hedge funds 5.6%/7.5%, real estate 6.6%/18.0%, US Treasuries 4.4%/5.5%, cash = Rf (default 3.75%) at 0.8% vol.
  • Equity–Treasury correlation +0.05; US–intl equity +0.85; private equity–US equity +0.72; hedge funds–US equity +0.72. Private-equity and real-estate vols are de-smoothed, not reported appraisal vols.
  • Policy bands: cash ≤ 5%, growth assets (public equity + PE + real estate) ≥ 50%, illiquid (PE + real estate) ≤ 50%, per-asset cap 15–60% (default 35%). Long-only throughout.
  • Monte Carlo: 200 paths × 180 months, Gaussian copula via the Cholesky factor of C, log-normal marginals calibrated so E[r] = μ/12; monthly rebalancing back to the target weights; no fees, taxes, transaction costs or capital calls.
  • Benchmark is 60% US equity / 40% US Treasuries. Charts show path 1 of the 200; risk statistics pool all 200.

Formulas & references

  • μp = w'μ, σp = √(w'Σw), Sharpe = (μp − Rf)/σp, risk contribution = w_i(Σw)_i / w'Σw.
  • Frontier: max λμ'w − w'Σw over the feasible set, λ swept from 0 upward; Euclidean projection onto {0 ≤ w ≤ cap, Σw = 1} by bisection on the shift, alternated with the group-constraint projections.
  • VaRα = −quantile1−α of pooled 1-month returns; CVaRα = −mean of returns below that quantile; both positive = loss.
  • Max drawdown from the compounded wealth path Wt = ∏(1+rt); Sortino uses the target semi-deviation below monthly Rf; active share = ½Σ|w − wb|.
  • Markowitz (1952), Portfolio Selection; Michaud (1989), The Markowitz Optimization Enigma; Swensen (2009), Pioneering Portfolio Management; Geltner (1991) on appraisal smoothing.

Limitations

  • Point-estimate CMAs with no estimation error: mean–variance optimizers are notoriously sensitive to μ, and no resampling, shrinkage or Black–Litterman prior is applied here.
  • Gaussian copula with log-normal marginals: no fat tails, no correlation breakdown in crises. The stress panel exists precisely because the Monte Carlo cannot produce those events.
  • Private equity and real estate are modelled as liquid, monthly-priced sleeves. Capital calls, J-curves, lock-ups, secondary discounts and the denominator effect are not modelled.
  • Capital-market assumptions are long-horizon point estimates entered by hand, not estimated from data, and mean–variance is highly sensitive to the return inputs. The policy ranges exist partly to contain that sensitivity.
  • No fees, carry, taxes or transaction costs. Rebalancing is assumed free, instantaneous and monthly, which flatters every allocation that drifts.
  • Capital calls and distributions are not modelled as cash flows. The illiquidity budget is a stock constraint; a real pacing model would also test unfunded commitments through a denominator-effect drawdown.
  • De-smoothing uses a first-order autocorrelation adjustment. It is the standard correction, not a precise one, and the true economic volatility of private assets is unobservable.
  • Stress shocks are fixed historical index moves applied instantaneously to today's weights, with no path, no rebalancing and no liquidity constraint.
