Featured paper · SSRN working paper · 2026

Regime-Aware Transformer Architectures with Cross-Attention Regime Discovery for Multi-Asset Portfolio Construction

Cristian Miguel Sanchez Arenas · Independent Researcher · MS Computer Science (AI), University of Colorado Boulder

SSRN 6517438 26 pages 10 baselines · 3 DGPs · 3 seeds Reproducible pipeline
We investigate end-to-end regime-aware portfolio optimization through a novel Cross-Attention Regime Discovery (CARD) mechanism that enables transformer architectures to jointly learn regime structure and allocation policies. Unlike traditional two-stage pipelines that classify market regimes and optimize allocations independently, CARD uses learnable regime query vectors that attend to temporal feature representations via cross-attention, producing interpretable soft regime assignments and regime-specific portfolio weights. We evaluate CARD within our proposed Regime-Aware Transformer (RAT) architecture across three synthetic data generating processes with varying regime persistence and signal-to-noise ratio, each tested over multiple random seeds with transaction cost analysis. Our controlled evaluation on known Markov-switching processes reveals that all neural methods substantially outperform traditional baselines across settings, but no single neural architecture dominates. On the base DGP, RAT-CARD achieves a Sharpe ratio of 0.82 ± 0.40 versus 0.29 ± 0.40 for equal weight and 0.44 ± 0.43 for GMM-MVO. However, a simpler LSTM baseline (1.13 ± 0.45) and RAT with a VAE-based regime module (0.99 ± 0.43) perform comparably or better, with no pairwise differences reaching statistical significance. CARD's attention-based regime discovery provides qualitatively different, interpretable regime representations, though explicit GMM models recover true regime labels more accurately. These findings demonstrate that architectural sophistication does not guarantee performance gains, and highlight the gap between regime identification and portfolio-relevant regime awareness.

Open research directions

The paper closes with the questions it could not answer on synthetic data. These are the threads I am pulling on next.

01

Real-market validation

Re-run the full protocol on liquid ETF universes with rolling out-of-sample windows and externally dated regimes.

02

Turnover-aware training

Put the transaction-cost penalty inside the loss so constrained models earn their stability advantage explicitly.

03

Non-Markov regimes

Time-varying transition matrices and duration-dependent regimes, to test whether soft assignments adapt where hard classifiers break.

04

Truly latent regimes

Settings where regime information is not encoded in observable features, the case the paper argues CARD is actually built for.

05

Larger universes

Scale from 10 to 50+ assets, where regime-specific allocation heads have room to specialise and diversification benefits compound.
