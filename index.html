About

I am a Quantitative Investment Professional and AI Engineer with 6+ years of experience across institutional investing, applied AI, quantitative research, and data science in the United States, Europe, and Latin America.

My work sits at the intersection of AI and portfolio management. I design and build agentic AI systems that operate inside investment workflows. Multi-agent systems monitor portfolios, read GP reports, DDQs, and manager letters, extract data with a citation back to the source, and draft research for review. Every system I build carries monitoring, guardrails, human-in-the-loop approval, and a full audit trail, because in investing an answer you cannot trace is worse than no answer.

Private markets are a particular focus, from commitment pacing and liquidity planning to fund selection and direct and co-investment analysis. My investment background spans public and private markets, including public equity, fixed income, hedge funds, private equity, private credit, and real assets. My experience covers asset allocation, manager research and due diligence, portfolio construction, multi-strategy hedge fund portfolios, and risk management for institutional portfolios.

On the quantitative side, I build models, analytical tools, data pipelines, and research infrastructure that turn messy, incomplete investment data into structured inputs for decisions. My independent research on regime-aware deep learning for multi-asset allocation is published as an SSRN working paper.

Ultimately, I combine institutional investment experience with hands-on AI and quantitative engineering. I translate investment problems into models, agents, and production-ready tools that an investment committee can trust and audit.

Regime-Aware Transformer Architectures with Cross-Attention Regime Discovery for Multi-Asset Portfolio Construction

I test whether a transformer that learns market regimes and allocations together beats simpler allocators. On synthetic regime-switching markets every neural model beats the traditional baselines, though a plain LSTM does as well and none of the differences are significant.
