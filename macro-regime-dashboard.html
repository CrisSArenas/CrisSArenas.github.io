Guide — Macro Regime & Tactical Allocation

1 · Data

104 quarters, Q1 2000 – Q4 2025. Growth and inflation are stored as stylised historical standardised scores: the inflation score is the CPI y/y deviation from the 2% target divided by a 3.6pp standard deviation (so the score maps back as CPI ≈ 2% + 7.2 × score), and the growth score is a composite growth-momentum reading (ISM new orders, payrolls, real GDP versus trend) clipped to ±2σ. VIX is the quarterly average close with the intra-quarter high shown separately. The numbers are hand-calibrated to the historical record, not a live data feed; asset returns inside the attribution panel are simulated from a seeded generator (mulberry32, seed shown in the status strip) so every figure is reproducible.

2 · Regime quadrant

The horizontal axis is the growth z-score, the vertical axis the inflation z-score, both plotted over ±2σ. The cut-off is 0σ on each axis — growth at trend and inflation at the 2% target:

RegimeConditionTypical quarters
Goldilocksgrowth > 0, inflation ≤ 02013–2016, 2019, H2 2020

Stagflationgrowth ≤ 0, inflation > 0H1 2001, H1 2008, all of 2022
Deflationgrowth ≤ 0, inflation ≤ 02002, Q4 2008–2009, H1 2020

The trail shows the previous 20 quarters and the corner percentages the regime shares of the trailing 13 quarters. 2023–25 sits just inside Reflation: CPI keeps falling but stays above the 2% target, so the point drifts towards the Goldilocks boundary without crossing it.

3 · VIX gauge

Bands are the standard practitioner cut-offs: below 15 low, 15–25 normal, 25–35 elevated, above 35 crisis. The needle and the large number are the quarterly average; the smaller reading is the intra-quarter high (Q4 2008: 58.0 average, 80.9 high; Q1 2020: 57.0 average, 82.7 high). The line chart plots both series to the as-of quarter.

4 · Momentum signals

Because the data set is quarterly, the four horizons are 1, 2, 4 and 8 quarters (3M / 6M / 12M / 24M). Each horizon score is a recency-weighted average of a two-factor reading, score = βg·growth + βπ·inflation, with weights 1, 2, 3… on successively more recent quarters. The composite is the weighted blend (1·m1Q + 1.5·m2Q + 2.5·m4Q + 3·m8Q) / 8. The trading rule is explicit: composite > +0.15 → BUY, composite < −0.15 → SELL, otherwise HOLD. This is a factor-implied score, not realised price momentum.

5 · Tactical allocation

The strategic policy portfolio is 25 / 15 / 10 / 25 / 5 / 10 / 8 / 2 across US equity, international equity, EM equity, fixed income, high yield, private equity, real assets and cash. Each regime has a fixed tactical vector; every vector sums to 100% and every tilt is bounded at ±10pp (most are ±8pp), so the tilts always sum to zero. This is an overlay, not a regime-timing bet.

6 · Attribution

Standard Brinson–Hood–Beebower arithmetic on the eight segments: Allocation = Σ(wp−wb)·rb, Selection = Σwb·(rp−rb), Interaction = Σ(wp−wb)·(rp−rb). The three terms sum exactly to the total active return Σwprp − Σwbrb, which is what the last bar shows. Segment returns are driven by the quarter's growth and inflation scores plus seeded noise; the information ratio annualises the trailing 12-quarter active-return mean and standard deviation by √4.

7 · Risk decomposition

Ex-ante, from the tactical weights, the long-run volatility assumptions and the correlation matrix: σp = √(w'Σw) and the Euler contribution of asset i is CTRi = wi·(Σw)i / σp. These contributions sum to σp exactly, so the percentages sum to 100% — the panel prints that sum as a check. Ex-ante tracking error uses the same covariance matrix on the active weights.

8 · Private capital pacing

The Takahashi–Alexander (2002) model, annual periods:

Ct
= RC × uncalled commitment (drawn at the start of year t)
NAVt
= (NAVt−1 + Ct) × (1 + G)
RDt
= max(Y, (t / L)B) — the bow factor B back-loads distributions, the yield Y is a floor
Dt
= RDt × NAVt; at t = L the rate reaches 1 and the fund fully winds down

Net IRR is solved with Newton–Raphson on the annual net cashflow stream (calls at the start of the year, distributions at the end). Peak net outlay is the deepest point of the cumulative-cashflow J-curve and break-even the first year cumulative distributions exceed cumulative calls.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

A quarterly macro-regime dashboard over 104 quarters (Q1 2000 – Q4 2025). Each quarter carries a growth z-score and an inflation z-score which place it in one of four regimes (Goldilocks, Reflation, Stagflation, Deflation) with the cut-off at 0σ on both axes. The regime drives a bounded tactical overlay on a strategic policy portfolio of eight asset classes; the dashboard then reports the VIX volatility regime, a factor-implied cross-asset momentum table, a Brinson–Hood–Beebower attribution of the quarter's active return, an ex-ante Euler risk decomposition, the long-run correlation matrix, and a Takahashi–Alexander private-capital pacing model with its J-curve.

Key assumptions

  • Inflation score = (CPI y/y − 2%) / 3.6pp; growth score = composite growth momentum vs trend, clipped to ±2σ. Both are stored at half scale and plotted as z = 2 × stored value.
  • VIX bands: <15 low, 15–25 normal, 25–35 elevated, >35 crisis. Crisis quarters carry both the average and the intra-quarter high (Q4 2008 58.0 / 80.9; Q1 2020 57.0 / 82.7).
  • Strategic weights 25/15/10/25/5/10/8/2 (%); tactical vectors sum to 100% with tilts bounded at ±10pp.
  • Capital-market assumptions (annual): expected return 6.8 / 7.2 / 7.8 / 4.5 / 6.2 / 9.5 / 6.2 / 3.6%; volatility 16 / 17 / 21 / 5.5 / 9 / 22 / 14 / 0.6%; equity–bond correlation +0.05.
  • Momentum rule: composite > +0.15 BUY, < −0.15 SELL, else HOLD, over 1/2/4/8-quarter lookbacks.
  • Pacing defaults: $100M commitment, RC 25% of uncalled per year, fund life 12 years, growth 15%/yr, bow factor 2.5, yield floor 5%.
  • Simulated segment returns use a seeded mulberry32 generator (seed 20260101), so every figure on the page is reproducible.

Formulas & references

  • Brinson: alloc = Σ(wp−wb)rb, sel = Σwb(rp−rb), inter = Σ(wp−wb)(rp−rb); the three sum exactly to Σwprp − Σwbrb.
  • Risk: σp = √(w'Σw), CTRi = wi(Σw)i/σp, Σi CTRi = σp; ex-ante TE = √(a'Σa) with a = wp − wb.
  • Information ratio = 4 · mean(active) / (√4 · sd(active)) over the trailing 12 quarters.
  • Pacing: Ct = RC · uncalled; NAVt = (NAVt−1+Ct)(1+G); Dt = max(Y,(t/L)B) · NAVt; IRR by Newton–Raphson on the net cashflow stream.
  • Brinson, Hood & Beebower (1986), Determinants of Portfolio Performance; Takahashi & Alexander (2002), Illiquid Alternative Asset Fund Modeling, JPM; Litterman (1996), Hot Spots and Hedges (Euler risk contributions); Whaley (2009), Understanding the VIX.

Limitations

  • The macro series is a hand-calibrated stylised reconstruction, not a vendor data feed; quarter-by-quarter values are approximations of the historical record and 2025 is partly forward-looking.
  • Regime classification uses hard 0σ cut-offs, so quarters near a boundary flip regime abruptly and the tactical vector jumps with them; no smoothing, hysteresis or transition probabilities are modelled.
  • Momentum is a factor-implied score from the same two macro series, not realised asset price momentum, so it cannot be back-tested as a trading signal.
  • Attribution segment returns are simulated, and the decomposition is single-period arithmetic Brinson — no multi-period linking, no currency or interaction-with-timing terms, no transaction costs or turnover drag.
  • The correlation matrix and volatilities are static long-run assumptions; correlations rise in crises, which this understates.
  • Private-capital pacing is deterministic: no manager dispersion, no secondary sales, no recycling, no fees or carry, and the fund always fully liquidates at year L.
