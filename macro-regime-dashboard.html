Guide — Macro Regime & Tactical Allocation

1 · Data — what it is and what it is not

104 quarters, Q1 2000 – Q4 2025. This is a stylised historical reconstruction, hand-calibrated to the published record. It is not a vendor data feed and no figure on this page should be quoted as an observation. Two series are stored per quarter, at half scale:

  • 7.2 × inf is headline CPI y/y minus the 2% target, in percentage points. Spot-check: Q2 2022 stores 0.92 → 8.6% CPI; Q3 2009 stores −0.50 → −1.6%; Q4 2021 stores 0.65 → 6.7%.
  • 2 × g is a composite growth-momentum reading (ISM new orders, payrolls, real GDP versus trend), clipped at the source to ±2 in its own units — Q2 2020 is the only quarter at the clip.

VIX is the mean of the daily closes inside the quarter, with the highest close in the quarter carried as a separate series. Asset returns inside the attribution panel are simulated from a seeded generator (mulberry32, seed in the status strip), so every figure is reproducible; they are not the returns those asset classes actually delivered.

2 · Standardisation — and why it changed

An axis labelled σ has to be measuring σ. Over these 104 quarters the growth composite has mean +0.44 and standard deviation 0.75, and the old inflation score (CPI deviation divided by a flat 3.6pp) has standard deviation 0.47. Neither was a z-score: growth sat above its own reference in two thirds of quarters, and 2022’s 8.6% print showed as 1.8σ when measured against the post-2000 record it is nearer 3.7σ.

Both series are now standardised, and standardised point-in-time: quarter i is scored using quarters 0…i only. A point on the trail is therefore the reading an allocator would have had that quarter, and it does not move when you drag the slider. Growth is demeaned — its reference is trend, estimated as the mean to date. Inflation is not demeaned — its reference is the 2% policy target, not the historical average — so its scale is the root-mean-square deviation from target to date, 1.79pp over the full sample. The first 20 quarters share the statistics of the first 20 quarters; that five-year warm-up is in-sample, for 2000–2004 only. Points beyond ±3σ are drawn at the plot edge; the status strip and the tooltip carry the unclipped value.

3 · Regime classification — dead band and persistence

A bare sign test on each axis re-labels the quarter whenever a z-score crosses zero by any amount. On this data that is 26 regime changes in 104 quarters, six of them one quarter long, and several of them triggered by inflation moving 0.02σ — each one moving a 10pp tactical book. That is the single most common way a regime dashboard becomes unusable. Per axis the classifier now applies:

RuleSettingEffect
Dead band (hysteresis)±0.25σInside the band the axis carries no information and the last confirmed sign is held, so a wobble around zero flips nothing.
Persistence2 consecutive quartersA new sign must print twice running before it is adopted.
Path constructionforward from Q1 2000Computed once; the label of any quarter never depends on where the slider sits.

Result: 15 changes, shortest regime 2 quarters, and the path lands where it should — Stagflation through 2001 and 2008, Deflation from Q1 2009 and from Q2 2020, Goldilocks Q3 2013 to Q1 2018, Stagflation from Q2 2022, Reflation from Q3 2023. The cost is a recognition lag of one to two quarters at turning points. That is not a defect being hidden: in Q1 2020 and Q1 2022 the confirmed label is still Reflation, the chart says in words that the point sits in a different quadrant and the label is being held, and conviction (below) is zero, so the book is at policy weights. A real committee incurs the same lag.

4 · VIX gauge

Bands are the usual practitioner cut-offs: below 15 low, 15–25 normal, 25–35 elevated, above 35 crisis. The needle and the large number are the quarterly average of daily closes throughout. The earlier build re-cut the crisis quarters to their panic months and still called the result an average: Q1 2020 was shown at 57.0, which is roughly the March average, when the quarter as a whole averaged about 32 because January and most of February were calm. That is corrected. The intra-quarter high (Q1 2020: 82.7; Q4 2008: 80.9) is printed under the needle rather than on the dial, because it is a different statistic. A quarterly average will always understate an intra-quarter panic — that is what the second series is for.

5 · Cross-asset macro factor scores

This is not price momentum and cannot be back-tested as a trading signal. Each horizon is a recency-weighted average (weights 1, 2, 3… on more recent quarters) of a two-factor reading βg·zg + βπ·zπ over 1, 2, 4 and 8 quarters; the composite is (1·m1Q + 1.5·m2Q + 2.5·m4Q + 3·m8Q) / 8.

The cut-off changed for a reason. The raw composite has a different mean and dispersion for every asset — the S&P score averages +0.15, the gold score +0.03 with half the dispersion — so the old fixed ±0.15 rule on the raw number was not a signal, it was a standing bias: it printed BUY on the S&P in 67% of all quarters and SELL on the 10-year in 69%. Each composite is now standardised against its own history to the as-of quarter (expanding window, point-in-time, minimum 12 quarters) and the same ±0.5σ cut-off is applied to every row. Across the sample that fires BUY 33% / SELL 25% / HOLD 42%. Two factors drive all eight rows, so the cross-section has rank 2: these are not eight independent signals. The panel is informational and does not feed the tilts.

6 · Tactical allocation

The strategic policy portfolio is 25 / 15 / 10 / 25 / 5 / 10 / 8 / 2 across US equity, international equity, EM equity, fixed income, high yield, private equity, real assets and cash. Each sleeve also has an investment-policy range, shown on hover, and each regime has a target vector that is the allocation at full conviction. The applied weight is

wi = policyi + conviction × (targeti − policyi)

with conviction = the weaker of the two axes’ agreement with the label, in σ, capped at 1σ. A regime only earns its full tilt when both conditions hold; if either axis has turned against the label, conviction is zero and the portfolio is the policy portfolio. Over this sample the book sits at policy in 38 of 104 quarters and at full tilt in 12. Scaling a zero-sum vector keeps it zero-sum, so the weights sum to 100% at every conviction level, no tilt can breach the 10pp bound, and every sleeve stays inside its IPS range — all three are checked on the panel, every quarter.

Private equity is pinned at its 10% policy weight in all four regimes. The earlier build tilted it by −2pp and −1pp, which is not implementable: a drawn private book cannot be traded quarterly on a macro read. Private weight is steered through the commitment pacing model, on a multi-year horizon. This also removes the earlier step change, where a regime flip moved 10pp of NAV overnight with no turnover cost anywhere in the model.

7 · Attribution

Standard Brinson–Hood–Beebower arithmetic on the eight segments: Allocation = Σ(wp−wb)·rb, Selection = Σwb·(rp−rb), Interaction = Σ(wp−wb)·(rp−rb). The total is computed independently, as Σwprp − Σwbrb, and the panel prints the residual against the three effects. The earlier build set the total equal to the sum of the three parts and then asserted that it equalled itself — a tick that could not fail and therefore proved nothing. The residual is now a real number, and it is below 4×10−17 in every one of the 104 quarters.

Segment returns come from a two-factor model, ri = μi/4 + βg,izg + βπ,izπ + εi, and the residual ε is not a free parameter: its covariance is set to Σtarget − Σfactor and factored by Cholesky, so the generator reproduces the same volatilities and correlations the risk panel uses. The earlier build drew an independent shock per sleeve at half the stated quarterly volatility, so the simulated benchmark carried about 6.8% annualised risk while the risk panel three inches away reported 11.0% ex-ante for the same weights. It now realises 11.6% against 11.0% ex-ante. Selection tracking error is sleeve-specific (2.0% a year on US equity, 6.0% on private equity, 0.05% on cash), rather than the flat 50bp a quarter on every sleeve including cash.

The information ratio annualises the trailing 12-quarter active-return mean and standard deviation. Twelve quarters is three years, and the standard error of an annualised IR over three years is about 0.58, so the panel prints the point estimate with its standard error: anything inside roughly ±1.2 is not distinguishable from zero. Two decimal places on their own would imply a precision the sample cannot support.

8 · Risk decomposition

Ex-ante, from the applied tactical weights, the long-run volatility assumptions and the correlation matrix: σp = √(w′Σw) and the Euler contribution of asset i is CTRi = wi·(Σw)i / σp. Because Σi wi(Σw)i = w′Σw = σp2, these sum to σp exactly — verified below 10−16 at every quarter — so the percentages sum to 100%, and the panel prints that sum as a live check. Ex-ante tracking error uses the same matrix on the active weights, and scales with conviction: 0bp when the book is at policy, 142–220bp at full conviction depending on the regime. The correlation matrix is positive semi-definite (smallest eigenvalue 0.126). Private-equity volatility is carried de-smoothed at 22%, not at the 12–14% an appraisal-based reported series would show, and the panel reports the locked illiquid weight alongside.

9 · Private capital pacing

The Takahashi–Alexander (2002) model, annual periods:

Ct
= RC × uncalled commitment, for t ≤ IP and zero after — capital is called during the investment period, not for the whole fund life
NAVt
= (NAVt−1 + Ct) × (1 + G)
RDt
= max(Y, (t / L)B) — the bow factor B back-loads distributions, the yield Y is a floor
Dt
= RDt × NAVt; at t = L the rate reaches 1 and the fund fully winds down

The investment period is the change that matters. A constant contribution rate applied across the whole fund life, as the earlier build did, still draws capital in year 12 and calls 97% of the commitment; no limited partnership works that way. At the defaults (RC 35%, IP 5 years) the pace is 35 / 22.8 / 14.8 / 9.6 / 6.2 per cent of commitment, 88% is called and the remaining 12% expires uncalled — which is precisely why allocators over-commit.

Read the IRR for what it is. In this model NAV compounds at exactly (1+G) and every dollar is eventually distributed out of that NAV, so the gross IRR of the stream is identically G, at every setting of every other slider (checked: G = 5 / 13 / 30% gives IRR = 5.0000 / 13.0000 / 30.0000%). It is an identity, not a forecast. The model earns its keep on liquidity: peak net outlay, break-even year, and the steady-state commitment.

Steady-state commitment is the number the panel exists to produce. Committing the same amount to one vintage a year means holding vintages aged 1…L simultaneously, so aggregate NAV per $1 of annual commitment is the sum of a single vintage’s year-end NAVs. Inverting gives the annual commitment, as a share of total plan assets, that holds the 10% private-equity policy weight: 1.35% a year at the defaults. That is what ties this panel to the 10% sleeve in the policy portfolio above. Net IRR is solved by Newton–Raphson and cross-checked against a bisection solve (agreement to four decimal places, |NPV| at the root of order 10−15) at the corners of every slider.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

A quarterly macro-regime dashboard over 104 quarters (Q1 2000 – Q4 2025). Each quarter carries a point-in-time growth z-score and inflation z-score which, after a ±0.25σ dead band and a two-quarter confirmation rule, place it in one of four regimes (Goldilocks, Reflation, Stagflation, Deflation). The regime drives a conviction-scaled tactical overlay on a strategic policy portfolio of eight asset classes, bounded by an investment-policy range per sleeve. The dashboard then reports the VIX volatility regime, a cross-asset macro factor score table, a Brinson–Hood–Beebower attribution of the quarter’s active return, an ex-ante Euler risk decomposition, the long-run correlation matrix, and a Takahashi–Alexander private-capital pacing model with its J-curve and steady-state commitment.

Key assumptions

  • Inflation score = (CPI y/y − 2%) standardised point-in-time by the r.m.s. deviation from target to date (1.79pp full sample); growth score = composite growth momentum, demeaned and standardised point-in-time (full-sample σ 0.75 in its own units). Five-year in-sample warm-up; plot clipped at ±3σ.
  • Regime rule: dead band ±0.25σ per axis, 2-quarter confirmation, path built forward from Q1 2000. 15 regime changes, shortest regime 2 quarters.
  • VIX is the mean of daily closes in the quarter throughout; the highest close in the quarter is a separate series. Bands <15 low, 15–25 normal, 25–35 elevated, >35 crisis.
  • Strategic weights 25/15/10/25/5/10/8/2 (%). IPS ranges 15–35 / 8–22 / 5–15 / 15–35 / 0–10 / 10 (pinned) / 4–18 / 0–10. Tilts bounded at ±10pp and scaled by conviction.
  • Capital-market assumptions (annual): expected return 6.8 / 7.2 / 7.8 / 4.5 / 6.2 / 9.5 / 6.2 / 3.6%; volatility 16 / 17 / 21 / 5.5 / 9 / 22 / 14 / 0.6% with private equity de-smoothed; equity–bond correlation +0.05; correlation matrix positive semi-definite (min eigenvalue 0.126).
  • Factor score rule: composite standardised against each proxy’s own history to date (min 12 quarters), > +0.5σ BUY, < −0.5σ SELL, else HOLD, over 1/2/4/8-quarter lookbacks.
  • Pacing defaults: $100M commitment, RC 35% of uncalled per year, investment period 5 years, fund life 12 years, gross NAV growth 13%/yr, bow factor 2.5, yield floor 0%.
  • Simulated segment returns use a seeded mulberry32 generator (seed 20260101) with a Cholesky-factored residual covariance, so every figure is reproducible and consistent with the volatilities and correlations used elsewhere on the page.

Formulas & references

  • Tilt map: wi = policyi + c · (targeti − policyi), c = clip(min(sgzg, sπzπ), 0, 1) with s the confirmed axis signs. Zero-sum in, zero-sum out.
  • Brinson: alloc = Σ(wp−wb)rb, sel = Σwb(rp−rb), inter = Σ(wp−wb)(rp−rb), checked against an independently computed Σwprp − Σwbrb; max residual over 104 quarters 3.6×10−17.
  • Return model: ri,t = μi/4 + βg,izg,t + βπ,izπ,t + (Lεt)i, LL′ = Σtarget − BΣfB′.
  • Risk: σp = √(w′Σw), CTRi = wi(Σw)i/σp, Σi CTRi = σp; ex-ante TE = √(a′Σa) with a = wp − wb.
  • Information ratio = 4 · mean(active) / (√4 · sd(active)) over 12 quarters, quoted with s.e. ≈ √((1 + IR²/2)/T) ≈ 0.58 at T = 3 years.
  • Pacing: Ct = RC · uncalled for t ≤ IP; NAVt = (NAVt−1+Ct)(1+G); Dt = max(Y,(t/L)B) · NAVt; IRR by Newton–Raphson, cross-checked by bisection; steady-state commitment = target NAV / ΣtNAVt per unit committed.
  • Brinson, Hood & Beebower (1986), Determinants of Portfolio Performance; Takahashi & Alexander (2002), Illiquid Alternative Asset Fund Modeling, JPM; Litterman (1996), Hot Spots and Hedges (Euler risk contributions); Whaley (2009), Understanding the VIX.

Limitations — read these before you read the numbers

  • The data is stylised. The macro and VIX series are a hand-calibrated reconstruction of the published record, not a vendor feed. Quarter-by-quarter values are approximations. Nothing here is an observation.
  • The classifier lags on purpose. The dead band and the two-quarter confirmation buy stability at the cost of one to two quarters at every turning point — Q1 2020 and Q1 2022 are both still labelled Reflation. The chart says so in words when the label and the raw quadrant disagree, and conviction is zero there, but the lag is real and a faster rule would trade stability for it.
  • Point-in-time standardisation is not free of in-sample content. The first five years share the statistics of the first five years, and the scaling denominator moves as history accumulates, so a given CPI print maps to a different z in 2005 than in 2025. That is deliberate — it is what a real-time system does — but it means the z-scores are not comparable across decades as levels.
  • There is no transaction cost, turnover budget, or implementation lag anywhere. Conviction scaling smooths the path, but the model rebalances to the target instantly and free of charge every quarter.
  • The factor score panel is a two-factor construct, not price momentum. Eight rows, two factors: the cross-section has rank 2, so it cannot be treated as eight independent signals, and it cannot be back-tested as a trading rule. It informs; it drives nothing.
  • The VIX panel drives nothing either. A real overlay would scale gross risk with realised and implied volatility. This one does not: tilts respond to the macro read alone.
  • Attribution is single-period arithmetic Brinson on simulated returns. No multi-period linking, no currency effect, no fees, no turnover drag. Quarterly attribution on the private-equity sleeve is particularly weak: private marks arrive with a lag of a quarter or more, and institutions normally lag the private benchmark to match.
  • Risk is a static long-run covariance. Volatilities and correlations do not vary with the regime, and correlations rise in crises — which this understates in exactly the quarters where it matters most. The de-smoothed 22% private-equity volatility is an assumption, not a measurement.
  • The gross IRR in the pacing panel is an identity. It equals the growth slider by construction and carries no information. Use the panel for liquidity, not for return.
  • Pacing is one vintage, deterministic and gross. No manager dispersion, no secondaries, no recycling, no management fee and no carried interest — a typical buyout fee load takes roughly 0.3–0.5x off TVPI. Contributions run at a constant rate of the uncalled balance within the investment period, where real funds ramp; and the fund always fully liquidates at year L, where real funds run tails and take extensions. The steady-state commitment figure assumes one identical vintage a year forever.
