NEON FURNACE · Briefing

Play
NEON FURNACE
KESTREL VS. THE WARDEN

An original third-person brawler in a neon city, written from nothing in one HTML page. No engine, no library, no image, model or audio files: every texture, character, radio portrait and beat of techno is made by the page while you play.

What you are getting into

You are Kestrel, a vigilante in a homemade winged suit. The Warden, the security AI of the city's biggest tower, has given itself final authority and bought the street gangs to keep the city quiet while it does. Mira, who built your suit, talks you through it on the radio. The Warden talks too, mostly to tell you how small your file is.

The city is a stack of islands of towers over a chasm full of fog, joined by bridges, cranes and an elevator, crossed by flying traffic and a sky train, with people out in the rain at the market stalls. You fight up through five districts, each with its own boss: Undercity Market (Chain), Data Vault (the twins Echo and Null), Neon Rooftops (the gunship Hammerhead), The Core (the heavy frame Atlas) and The Spire, where the Warden waits. Each district opens with a short scene in the game's own engine, and every scene can be skipped.

Run, vault, slide, roll, climb a ledge, double jump, glide on fold-out wings, grapple to a rooftop and launch off the end of the line, dive bomb out of a glide, and run along a wall. Each district has keycards that open gates and run its cranes, data shards, three graffiti tags, a gang radio with a piece of the story, and an uplink that opens only once you hold every key and its boss is down.

Fighting

Strike and flow

One button strikes. It picks the enemy nearest the direction you are pushing and closes the gap, flowing through twelve different strikes: jabs, hooks, elbows, knees, a spinning heel, a flying kick, a backfist for whoever is behind you and a stomp for whoever is on the floor. Every five hits raise the score multiplier, up to ×8. Every eight charge a special takedown that ends one enemy on the spot.

Read the warnings

Enemies take turns and tell you before they hit. Red chevrons mean counter it: Kestrel turns to meet the attack from the front, the side or behind, and can counter several at once. An amber ring means get out of the way. Shots about to land can be sent back. Knock someone into a wall or over a rail and the finish is a slam or a throw.

Unseen

Anyone who has not seen you can be taken down in one move from behind or from above, a silent takedown. Guards who see a friend drop come to look. Searchlights sweep the decks. Smoke blinds a group in the middle of a fight so you can do it again.

Who you are fighting

  • Shields block from the front: vault over them.
  • Brutes shrug off strikes until a counter opens them up.
  • Batons shock you from the front. Knives cannot be countered until you learn how.
  • Ninjas dodge until your combo is high. Snipers paint you from the far roofs.
  • Guards, hackers who blink away, drones and mechs, and a boss in every district with its own tells and phases.

Gadgets

  • A disc that seeks a target and comes back, later electric, ricocheting or three at once.
  • An EMP that drops every drone nearby, stalls the machines and can ground a boss.
  • Smoke that blinds everyone around you.

Scoring, medals and suits

Style pays on top of every knockout: takedowns, counters, air and dive knockouts, wall slams, deflections, close calls and variety. Each district has four medals: no damage, all secrets, par time and every enemy. Medals and challenge stars unlock eight suits in the Armory, from Chrome to Prism.

Ways to play

Campaign

Five districts and five bosses on one of five skills, from Tourist to Flatline. Progress saves at each district, and Continue picks up where you stopped. Radios you find are kept in the Story Log.

Challenges

Six maps with three stars each: two combat arenas, two stealth rooms where being seen costs you, and two glide courses through rings against the clock. Each keeps its own top-ten table.

XP and upgrades

Everything you score earns XP, and every level is a point for one of 18 upgrades across combat, gadgets and the suit: quicker specials, knife counters, combo keeper, critical strikes, the disc upgrades, armour, faster glides and a bigger dive.

Overdrive and the daily run

Endless waves on a rooftop that get harder as they come. The daily run is one map and one twist a day, the same for everyone on that UTC date. Share run copies a line like NEON FURNACE · DAILY 2026-11-20 · Wave 9 · 96,400 · 41 hit combo.

Weekly gauntlet

Ten fixed waves for the whole ISO week, with a boss at wave five and another at wave ten. Clear all ten for the big bonus.

High scores

Overdrive, the daily run, the weekly gauntlet and every challenge keep a top ten in this browser. Make the table and you sign it with three initials, the old way.

How it works

A renderer written from scratch

The city is drawn with hand-written WebGL2. A handful of meshes (a box, a bevelled head, tapered limbs, cylinders, cones) are drawn thousands of times with instancing, each copy with its own matrix, colour and flags. Tower windows are worked out per pixel in the shader from a hash, so a whole skyline of lit windows costs nothing.

Textures painted by code

At start-up the page paints a 1024×1024 texture atlas: concrete, brick, rust, hazard stripes, shutters, server racks, signs in made-up scripts, posters, graffiti, suits, faces and the radio portraits. Walls take it from three sides at once, so it never stretches, with darker corners and wet streaks on every deck when it rains.

Light, shadow and the frame

The moon casts real shadows from a depth map, filtered so they stay soft. Neon lights the surfaces near it, lamps and signs bloom, light shafts hang under the street lamps, and the frame goes through bloom, a colour grade, smoothing of jagged edges, motion blur on a dash, rain, lightning and grain. Quality presets, or AUTO, keep it smooth on a phone.

Characters posed by code

Every person is built from shaped parts on one skeleton and animated by blending key poses: run, vault, roll, climb, glide with a bank into turns, twelve strikes, three counters, finishers. Heads turn to look at you, Kestrel's scarf is a little chain of points that hangs and streams in the wind, and a knockout falls as a ragdoll.

Districts that are provably finishable

Each district is written as a few islands, links, cranes and lasers, and built into the city by code. Before it ships, a checker walks it with movement rules a little weaker than the real ones, rides the cranes and the elevator only once their keycard is held, and proves every key, shard, tag, radio, boss and the uplink can be reached.

How the music works

There is no audio file. The soundtrack is a 16-step techno sequencer written with Web Audio, scheduling notes a tenth of a second ahead so the timing holds steady. The kick is a sine wave swept down from 160 Hz; hats and claps are filtered noise; the bassline is a sawtooth through a resonant filter with an envelope on the cutoff, in the style of an acid line. Pads are detuned chords through a dotted-eighth echo, and everything except the kick ducks under it, the way a sidechained mix breathes.

Each district has its own key, tempo and bassline, generated from a seed, and every boss gets a faster, darker track. The music listens to the fight: exploring gets a stripped-back groove, being spotted brings in the claps, and a big fight adds the arpeggio. Combos of 10, 25 and 50 hits land a stinger.

Phone

  • Turn the phone sideways. The game takes the whole screen.
  • Left thumb anywhere on the left moves you.
  • Right thumb drags the camera, which also follows you as you run.
  • STRIKE, COUNTER, JUMP (hold to glide), DASH, GRAPPLE, SPECIAL, the three gadgets and MAP sit on the right.

Worth knowing

  • A cyan diamond marks the grapple point you would take. The compass at the top points to your next goal.
  • Updraft vents throw a glide high into the air. In the Spire, glide into the wind, not with it.
  • Shards, tags and radios hide on needle towers, floating billboards and under the decks.
  • There are cheat codes. The first is a synonym for invulnerable skin.
NEON FURNACE is an original game. Every district, character, sound and note of music is made by its own code in your browser. It draws on the genre of neon-noir city brawlers, none of their content, and it is not affiliated with any other game, studio or publisher.
