NEON FURNACE · Briefing

Play
NEON FURNACE
A ROGUE AI HAS TAKEN THE TOWER

An original third-person brawler in a neon city built from blocks, written from nothing in one HTML page. No engine, no library, no image, model or audio files. The techno you hear is composed by the page while you play.

What you are getting into

The Warden, a corporate AI, has sealed itself at the top of its own tower. The city below is a stack of islands of towers hanging over a chasm full of fog, joined by bridges and crossed by streams of flying traffic. You fight your way up through five districts: Undercity Market, Data Vault, Neon Rooftops, The Core and The Spire, where the Warden waits.

It plays like the street brawlers it is in the spirit of: gangs on every deck, fists first, gadgets when you need them, and the rooftops as your road. Run, double jump, glide on fold-out wings, grapple to a rooftop, dash through an attack and run along a wall. Each district has keycards on its rooftops, gates in their colour, hidden data shards, and an uplink that only opens once you hold every key.

Fighting

Strike and flow

One button strikes. It picks the enemy nearest the direction you are pushing and closes the gap for you, so a fight flows from one target to the next. Hits within a moment of each other build a combo, and every five hits raise the score multiplier, up to ×8. Get hit and the combo breaks.

Read the warnings

Enemies take turns, and they tell you before they hit. Red chevrons over a head mean counter it: the attack is reversed and they go down. An amber ring means it cannot be countered, so dash out of the way. Shots that are about to land can be countered too, and go back where they came from.

Unseen

Anyone who has not seen you can be taken down in one move from behind or from above, a silent takedown. Smoke blinds a group so you can do it again in the middle of a fight. An enemy on the floor can be finished.

Gadgets

  • A disc that seeks the nearest target and comes back. One brings a drone down.
  • An EMP that drops every drone nearby and stalls the heavy machines.
  • Smoke that blinds everyone around you for a few seconds.

Who you are fighting

Gang members who rush in, guards who line up a laser before they fire, hackers who glitch away when you hit them, drones you have to jump or throw at, a mech whose stomp you have to dodge, and the Warden, which only takes damage once it is on the ground. Their shots hit each other, too.

Scoring and medals

Style pays on top of every knockout: silent takedowns, counters, air knockouts, ground finishes, deflections and friendly fire. Each district has four medals: no damage, all shards, par time and every enemy. Twenty in all, and they unlock suits in the Armory: Chrome, Toxic, Gold and, for all twenty, Glitch.

Ways to play

Campaign

Five districts on one of five skills, from Tourist to Flatline. Falling into the chasm or going down puts you back at your last safe footing. Progress saves at each district, and Continue picks up where you stopped.

Overdrive

Opens once you finish the first district. A rooftop, waves that keep coming and keep getting harder, a mech every fifth wave, and stims between them. Your best score is kept in this browser.

Daily run

One rooftop and one twist a day, the same for everyone on that UTC date, so scores compare fairly. Share run copies a line like NEON FURNACE · DAILY 2026-11-20 · Wave 9 · 96,400 · 41 hit combo.

How it works

A renderer written from scratch

The city is drawn with hand-written WebGL2. Everything is a box: one cube, drawn thousands of times with instancing, each copy with its own matrix, colour and a few flags. Faces are lit flat, from screen-space derivatives, which gives the clean block look. Tower windows are not geometry: the shader works out which window a pixel is in from its world position and decides, from a hash, whether that window is lit and what colour.

Fog, neon and glow

Fog thickens with distance and much more down in the chasm, where the supports of every island vanish into it. Each district has its own palette. Neon signs, rails and lamps light the surfaces near them, and the frame goes through a bloom pass, a colour grade, rain streaks and a little grain. The skyline in the distance is real geometry, hundreds of towers of it, so it is never painted on a wall.

Characters from boxes

Every person in the game is built from boxes on one small skeleton, posed by code each frame: a run cycle, a jump, a glide, six strikes, a counter, a takedown, a stagger, a fall. Drones, the mech and the Warden have their own builds. Suits are just a different palette passed to the same body.

Districts that are provably finishable

Each district is written as a few islands and links, and built into boxes by code. Before it ships, a checker walks it with movement rules a little weaker than the real ones (step, jump, double jump, glide, grapple, updraft) and proves every keycard, enemy, data shard and the uplink can be reached. A hundred Overdrive rooftops are checked the same way.

How the music works

There is no audio file. The soundtrack is a 16-step techno sequencer written with Web Audio, scheduling notes a tenth of a second ahead so the timing holds steady. The kick is a sine wave swept down from 160 Hz; hats and claps are filtered noise; the bassline is a sawtooth through a resonant filter with an envelope on the cutoff, in the style of an acid line, with a sine an octave below for weight. Pads are detuned chords through a dotted-eighth echo, and everything except the kick ducks under it, the way a sidechained mix breathes.

Each district has its own key, tempo (128 to 140 BPM) and bassline, generated from a seed. The music listens to the fight: exploring gets a stripped-back groove, being spotted brings in the claps and opens the filter, and a big fight or a long combo adds the arpeggio and sixteenth hats. Changes only land on a bar line, a riser leads into the bigger sections, and the Warden gets a faster, darker track of its own.

Phone

  • Turn the phone sideways. The game takes the whole screen.
  • Left thumb anywhere on the left moves you.
  • Right thumb drags the camera, which also follows you as you run.
  • STRIKE, COUNTER, JUMP (hold to glide), DASH, GRAPPLE and the three gadgets sit on the right.

Worth knowing

  • A cyan diamond marks the grapple point you would take. Keycards always sit on a rooftop.
  • Updraft vents throw a glide high into the air. Some crossings have no bridge.
  • Shards hide on needle towers, on floating billboards and under the decks.
  • There are cheat codes. The first is a synonym for invulnerable skin.
NEON FURNACE is an original game. Every district, character, sound and note of music is made by its own code in your browser. It draws on the genre of neon-noir city brawlers, none of their content, and it is not affiliated with any other game, studio or publisher.
