NEON FURNACE · Briefing

Play
NEON FURNACE
A ROGUE AI HAS TAKEN THE TOWER

An original first-person shooter, written from nothing in one HTML page. No engine, no game library, no image or audio files. The techno you hear is composed by the page while you play.

What you are getting into

The Warden, a corporate AI, has locked itself inside its own tower. You start in the rain at street level and fight upward through five levels: Undercity Market, Data Vault, Neon Rooftops, The Core and The Spire, where the Warden is waiting. Keycard doors, secret rooms, charged runoff that bites if you stand in it, and five weapons from a smart pistol to a rail cannon that goes through everything in a line.

The machines come in five kinds: drones that weave and spit bolts, guards with rifles, hackers who glitch out of the way when you hit them, a mech that fires rockets in threes, and the Warden itself. Their shots hit each other too. Get a guard to shoot a hacker and they will settle it between themselves.

Ways to play

Campaign

Five levels on one of five skills, from Tourist to Flatline. Progress saves at the start of each level, so Continue picks up where you stopped, and R restarts a level in an instant.

Overdrive

Opens once you finish level one. A sealed arena, waves that keep coming and keep getting harder, a mech every fifth wave, and supplies dropped between them. Your best score is kept in this browser.

Daily run

One arena, one loadout and one twist per day, the same for everyone on that UTC date, so a score can be compared fairly. Share run copies a line like NEON FURNACE · DAILY 2026-11-20 · Wave 9 · 96,400 · x6 max.

Medals and the Armory

Each level has four medals: no damage, all secrets, par time and 100% kills. Twenty in all, and they unlock weapon finishes: Chrome, Toxic, Gold and, for all twenty, Glitch.

Scoring

Every kill scores, and kills within three seconds of each other build a combo up to ×8. Style pays on top: multi kill for more than one with a shot, rail pierce, close call when you are nearly dead, long shot, point blank with the scattergun, and friendly fire when the machines take each other out. Finishing under par, at 100% or untouched adds a bonus, scaled by the skill you chose.

How it works

The renderer

For each of the 320 screen columns the engine casts a ray and walks the level grid with a DDA until it hits a wall, then draws one textured strip whose height comes from the perpendicular distance, which keeps the walls flat instead of fish-eyed. Floors and ceilings are cast per pixel row, and where a level is open to the sky, a skyline painted once at load wraps round you and turns as you do.

Neon, fog and glow

Light falls off with distance through 32 levels, fading into each level's own fog colour rather than black. Any texel bright and saturated enough is marked emissive when the art is generated, and emissive pixels ignore distance, so signs and strips stay lit across a dark room. A quarter-size bright pass is blurred and added back on top for the glow.

Doors that behave

Each door is a thin plane at the middle of its cell, so while it slides open a ray can pass through the gap and you see the room beyond before you can walk into it.

Art from code

There is not a single image file. Every wall, sign, terminal and floor is painted into an offscreen canvas at load. Guards and hackers come from one parametric figure; drones, the mech and the Warden have their own drawing functions. Weapon finishes are just a different palette passed to the same drawings.

Levels that are provably finishable

Each level is a grid of characters run through a checker before it ships: a solid border, one exit, and a flood fill that collects keycards as it goes to prove every key is reachable before its door, and that every enemy, item and secret can be reached, so 100% is always possible. Three hundred Overdrive arenas are generated and checked the same way.

The feel

A shot shakes the screen by the weapon's weight, a kill freezes the action for a few hundredths of a second, and the muzzle flash lights the walls around you for a moment. Taking a hit slips slices of the frame sideways.

How the music works

There is no audio file. The soundtrack is a 16-step techno sequencer written with Web Audio, scheduling notes a tenth of a second ahead so the timing holds steady. The kick is a sine wave swept down from 160 Hz; hats and claps are filtered noise; the bassline is a sawtooth through a resonant filter with an envelope on the cutoff, in the style of an acid line, with a sine an octave below for weight. Pads are detuned chords through a dotted-eighth echo, and everything except the kick ducks under it, the way a sidechained mix breathes.

Each level has its own key, tempo (128 to 140 BPM) and bassline, generated from a seed. The music listens to the fight: exploring gets a stripped-back groove, being spotted brings in the claps and opens the filter, and a real fight adds the arpeggio and sixteenth hats. Changes only land on a bar line, a riser leads into the bigger sections, and the Warden gets a faster, darker track of its own. Music and effects have separate volumes.

Phone

  • Turn the phone sideways. The game takes the whole screen.
  • Left thumb anywhere on the left moves you; push further to run.
  • Right thumb drags to look.
  • FIRE, USE and WPN sit on the right; II pauses.

Worth knowing

  • A wall that looks slightly wrong is worth a press of use. Secret doors match the wall they sit in.
  • Armour absorbs a third of incoming damage until it runs out.
  • Charged runoff hurts about five health a second. It is never the only way through.
  • Firing is loud. Anything within about thirteen metres comes looking.
  • There are cheat codes. The first is a synonym for invulnerable skin.
NEON FURNACE is an original game. Every level, sprite, texture, sound and note of music is made by its own code in your browser. It uses the techniques of early-1990s first-person shooters (grid raycasting, billboard sprites, distance-shaded light), none of their content, and it is not affiliated with any other game or studio.
