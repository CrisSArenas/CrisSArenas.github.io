Guide — Portable Alpha Simulator

1 · The idea

A traditional 60/40 fund buys stocks and bonds with all of its capital. A portable alpha programme gets the same 60/40 exposure with index futures (or total-return swaps), which only require a few percent of initial margin. The freed-up capital is invested in an alpha sleeve (a hedge-fund style, low-beta return stream) and the remaining cash / T-bills earns the cash rate while serving as collateral. With a 100% notional the identity is simply PA = Benchmark + A·(alpha net of fees) − financing frictions: the alpha is "ported" on top of the benchmark.

2 · Beta sleeve

Futures prices already embed financing at the cash rate, so the sleeve earns N · (Benchmark − Rf). On top of that the model charges the financing spread (implied repo above SOFR, 30–60 bp for equity index futures in recent years), the roll execution cost and a zero-mean basis noise. Setting the notional above 100% is levered beta, financed at the same cash + spread on the extra notional.

3 · Alpha sleeve

The sleeve behaves like a diversified hedge-fund book: gross monthly return = Rf + alpha + βα · (Equity − Rf), where alpha is drawn with mean IR × vol / 12 and standard deviation vol / √12. A management fee accrues monthly and a performance fee is taken on gains above the sleeve's high-water mark. Expected alpha decays linearly with the alpha decay rate to mimic crowding. Regime behaviour is fixed and stylised on 2008 and 2022:

With the default 6% vol sleeve a 12-month crisis produces a hedge-fund style drawdown of roughly −20%, in line with the 2008 experience of hedge-fund composites.

4 · Collateral & liquidity

Initial margin = margin requirement × notional, grossed up for the collateral haircut. It is posted from the cash that is not in the alpha sleeve, and that cash keeps earning Rf. What remains is free cash; the programme wants it above the liquidity buffer. Each month the futures settle variation margin equal to N × benchmark return. If a month's loss exceeds free cash, the model records a margin call and funds the shortfall for that month at cash + spread + 100 bp. If free cash is negative even before any move, the structure cannot post its margin and the shortfall is permanently borrowed at cash + spread. Forced liquidation of the alpha sleeve is not modelled; in reality it is the main risk of these programmes.

5 · Reading the results

  • Growth / drawdown charts show the benchmark and the programme on the same seeded path; shaded bands are stress regimes.
  • Tracking error = annualised standard deviation of (PA − benchmark) monthly returns; information ratio = annualised active return / tracking error; Sharpe = (annualised arithmetic return − Rf) / annualised volatility; max drawdown = worst peak-to-trough fall of the $100 path.
  • Return decomposition sums the arithmetic annualised monthly contributions; the net differs slightly from the geometric CAGR in the table. The alpha sleeve gross bar includes the Rf earned inside the sleeve, the collateral bar the Rf on the rest.

6 · Stress & health

The stress table applies one-month shocks calibrated on October 2008, March 2020 and September 2022, with an idiosyncratic alpha shock, a 1.5× alpha beta, an adverse one-sigma basis move and a check of whether the month's variation margin exceeds free cash. The health panel verifies that the realised alpha vol, beta and regime shares match their targets, and reports margin posting, free cash and margin-call months. All numbers are synthetic and reproducible from the seed.

Methodology & assumptions

What this models

A 180-month (15-year) Monte-Carlo comparison of a stock/bond benchmark with a portable-alpha programme on the same seeded path. Market months come from a three-state Markov chain (calm, 2008-type crisis, 2022-type rate shock) with regime-specific means, volatilities and equity–bond correlation. The programme holds the benchmark synthetically through index futures with notional N, keeps A of NAV in a fee-paying hedge-fund style alpha sleeve with a stated equity beta, earns the cash rate on the remaining collateral, and pays financing spread, roll cost, fees and margin-call funding. Growth, drawdown, KPIs, a contribution waterfall, calibration checks and one-month stress scenarios are shown.

Key assumptions

  • Calm regime: equity +1.10%/mo, σ 3.7%/mo; bonds +0.45%/mo, σ 1.3%/mo; correlation +0.10. Unconditionally (15% stress) equities ≈ 7% arithmetic / 15% vol, bonds ≈ 4% / 5%, cash 4.0% constant.
  • Crisis (mean 12 months): equity −3.0%/mo, σ 7.0%; bonds +0.6%/mo, σ 1.8%; correlation −0.35; alpha drift −0.4σ/mo, alpha vol ×2, alpha beta ×1.5.
  • Rate shock (mean 9 months): equity −1.0%/mo, σ 5.0%; bonds −1.2%/mo, σ 1.8%; correlation +0.60; alpha drift ×0.25, vol ×1.3, beta ×1.5.
  • Defaults: 60/40, N = 100%, financing spread 35 bp, roll 5 bp/y, basis 10 bp/mo, A = 50%, alpha vol 6%, gross IR 1.0, alpha beta 0.15, fees 1.5% / 20% above high-water mark, alpha decay 1.0%/y, drag 15 bp/y, initial margin 8% of notional, haircut 2%, liquidity buffer 5%.
  • Monthly rebalancing to target weights; the alpha sleeve is assumed to offer monthly liquidity; shocks are Gaussian; the cash rate is constant.

Formulas & references

  • PA = (1−A)·Rf + N·(Bench − Rf − spread − roll + basis) + A·(Rf + α + βα(Eq − Rf) − mgmt − perf) − A·drag − liquidity, all monthly.
  • Performance fee = rate × max(0, sleeve NAV − high-water mark), accrued monthly; the mark is reset to the post-fee NAV.
  • Margin call when N·max(0, −Bench) > (1−A) − margin·N/(1−haircut); the shortfall is funded for one month at Rf + spread + 100 bp.
  • Sharpe = (12·mean − Rf) / (σ√12); tracking error = σ(PA − Bench)·√12; information ratio = 12·mean(PA − Bench) / TE; beta = cov / var; max drawdown on the cumulative path.
  • Kung & Pohlman (2004), Portable Alpha: Philosophy, Process, and Performance; Grinold & Kahn (2000), Active Portfolio Management; Fung & Hsieh (2004), Hedge Fund Benchmarks: A Risk-Based Approach.

Limitations

  • Synthetic Gaussian regime mixture, not historical data; fat tails only arise from regime switching.
  • Constant cash rate and spreads; no term structure, no swap alternative, no taxes or dividend-withholding effects on futures.
  • Single blended margin rate and a monthly settlement approximation of daily variation margin; forced de-leveraging and gating of the alpha sleeve are not modelled.
  • The alpha sleeve is one composite stream; manager dispersion, lock-ups, and performance-fee crystallisation timing (monthly here, usually annual) are simplified.
  • Regime parameters are hand-set stylisations of 2008 and 2022, not estimated.
