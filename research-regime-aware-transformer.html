01 Abstract

We investigate end-to-end regime-aware portfolio optimization through a novel Cross-Attention Regime Discovery (CARD) mechanism that enables transformer architectures to jointly learn regime structure and allocation policies. Unlike traditional two-stage pipelines that classify market regimes and optimize allocations independently, CARD uses learnable regime query vectors that attend to temporal feature representations via cross-attention, producing interpretable soft regime assignments and regime-specific portfolio weights. We evaluate CARD within our proposed Regime-Aware Transformer (RAT) architecture across three synthetic data generating processes with varying regime persistence and signal-to-noise ratio, each tested over multiple random seeds with transaction cost analysis. Our controlled evaluation on known Markov-switching processes reveals that all neural methods substantially outperform traditional baselines across settings, but no single neural architecture dominates. On the base DGP, RAT-CARD achieves a Sharpe ratio of 0.82 ± 0.40 versus 0.29 ± 0.40 for equal weight and 0.44 ± 0.43 for GMM-MVO. However, a simpler LSTM baseline (1.13 ± 0.45) and RAT with a VAE-based regime module (0.99 ± 0.43) perform comparably or better, with no pairwise differences reaching statistical significance. CARD's attention-based regime discovery provides qualitatively different, interpretable regime representations, though explicit GMM models recover true regime labels more accurately. These findings demonstrate that architectural sophistication does not guarantee performance gains, and highlight the gap between regime identification and portfolio-relevant regime awareness.

02 The problem with two-stage regime allocation

Markets behave differently across macroeconomic environments. The standard response is a two-stage pipeline: classify the regime, then optimize the allocation conditional on that label. That interface is where the information dies.

FAILURE 01

Error propagation

A regime misclassification passes straight into the allocation with no mechanism to compensate.

FAILURE 02

Information bottleneck

A discrete label discards regime uncertainty, transition dynamics, and within-regime heterogeneity.

FAILURE 03

Fixed within-regime assumptions

The optimizer treats parameters as stationary inside a regime and ignores gradual shifts.

FAILURE 04

Objective misalignment

The regime model maximizes classification likelihood. The portfolio needs risk-adjusted return. Those objectives are not guaranteed to align.

CARD removes the interface. Regime structure and allocation policy are learned jointly, end to end, against a single portfolio objective. The regime representation is free to be whatever helps the allocation, rather than whatever best reproduces a label.

03 How CARD works

Click any block to see what it does and the exact configuration used in the experiments.

The mechanism in one paragraph

The temporal encoder turns a 12-month lookback window into a sequence of hidden states. CARD holds K = 3 learnable regime query vectors, initialized orthogonally so they start out looking for different things. Each query attends over that sequence by scaled dot-product cross-attention, producing a regime-specific context vector and a soft assignment probability. Each regime owns its own allocation head, and the final portfolio is the probability-weighted blend of those heads, projected back onto the feasible set. Because the attention weights are per-timestep, you can read off which points in the lookback window drove each regime's activation.

OptimizerAdamW, lr 5×10-4, weight decay 10-4, grad clip 1.0, plateau LR halving, early stop at patience 35

Why separate heads per regime

In the limit where the regime probabilities become one-hot, the model collapses to K independent single-regime optimizers, each specialized to its own market conditions. Soft assignments let it interpolate smoothly between them instead of jumping at a classification boundary. The comparison architecture, CREM, instead uses one allocation head modulated by a continuous VAE embedding, which gives less structural support for specialization.

04 Experimental setup

This is a controlled methodological study on synthetic data. Ground truth regimes are known, which permits evaluation that real market data does not allow. Results are not evidence of real-world performance, and the paper says so explicitly.

DGP-A

Base

Three-regime Markov switching, moderate separation. Persistence 0.92 growth, 0.82 contraction, 0.65 crisis.

DGP-B

High persistence

Regimes change less often: 0.97, 0.92, 0.85. Tests whether longer regimes make discovery easier or harder.

DGP-C

Low signal-to-noise

Same transitions as the base, but 50% mean separation, 130% volatility, and 150% feature noise.

  • Universe: 10 assets spanning US large and small cap, international developed, emerging market equity, investment grade and high yield credit, Treasuries, commodities, REITs, and gold, with correlations amplified during crisis.
  • Splits: 720 monthly observations. Train 0–479, validate 480–599, test 600–719. Every number on this page is out-of-sample test performance.
  • Seeds: three ({42, 123, 7}), reported as mean ± standard deviation across seeds.
  • Significance: circular block bootstrap on Sharpe differences, block size 6 months, 10,000 replications.
  • Baselines: equal weight, minimum variance with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, risk parity, hierarchical risk parity, GMM-MVO two-stage, RAT-CREM, LSTM, MLP, plus an infeasible Oracle that is handed the true regime labels.

05 Main results

Every neural allocator beats every traditional baseline, and it does so mostly by taking less risk rather than earning more return. But the paper's own model is not the winner.

Statistical significance

The honest headline of the paper. Circular block bootstrap on Sharpe differences, RAT-CARD against each alternative, DGP-A, seed 42, 10,000 replications.

Bootstrap confidence intervals on Sharpe differences
Positive means RAT-CARD is ahead. An interval crossing the zero line is not distinguishable from no difference.
Only one interval excludes zero: RAT-CARD against hierarchical risk parity, a difference of 0.42 with an interval of 0.01 to 0.82. Every neural-versus-neural comparison is wide and straddles zero. With a 120-month test window, this study does not have the statistical power to separate these architectures, and the paper states that rather than working around it.

06 The regime detection paradox

The method that identifies regimes best allocates worst. This is the most interesting result in the paper and the one with the clearest practical implication.

Regime identification accuracy against portfolio performance
Adjusted Rand Index against true regime labels, on the horizontal axis, versus test Sharpe ratio on the vertical, DGP-A.
The pattern: the Gaussian mixture model recovers the generating regimes far better, with an Adjusted Rand Index of 0.59 against CARD's 0.03, and still produces the worse portfolio, 0.44 against 0.82. Normalized mutual information tells the same story: 0.45 for the mixture model against 0.04 for CARD.

Why this happens

Regime detection accuracy is scored against the discrete labels that generated the data, but the allocation that maximizes risk-adjusted return need not correspond to those categories. The two-stage pipeline also adds a second error source in the optimization step, through estimation error in the conditional moments and sensitivity to risk aversion. And a neural allocator can absorb regime-relevant information implicitly without ever recovering the generative structure.

The practical reading: optimizing directly for the portfolio objective beats optimizing for regime classification, even when the classifier is genuinely better at classifying. A regime model that scores well on labels is not automatically a regime model that helps a portfolio.

07 Ablations: does the architecture earn its complexity

Mostly, no. This is reported plainly rather than buried.

Ablation study, DGP-A
Mean Sharpe over 3 seeds with ±1 standard deviation. The full model is highlighted.
Removing the regime module entirely gives 0.93 against the full model's 0.82. A single transformer layer gives 0.92. Dropping the position constraints gives the best mean Sharpe, 1.06, but at higher turnover. None of these differences are statistically distinguishable, which is itself the finding.
  • Input features already carry the regime. Macro and stress indicators are in the input, so a plain transformer or LSTM can use them without an explicit regime bottleneck. The extra machinery is redundant here.
  • The regime queries face a dual objective. They must discover meaningful structure and produce useful allocation inputs at the same time, which is hard to optimize, especially with an entropy term pulling toward crisp assignments.
  • The test set has almost no crisis. 85 growth months, 30 contraction, 5 crisis. A crisis-specific allocation head cannot be meaningfully trained or evaluated on five observations.
  • Blending dilutes specialization. When regime probabilities stay diffuse rather than crisp, the weighted blend smooths away exactly the specialization the architecture is built to provide.

08 Transaction costs and turnover

Where the constrained architecture actually earns its keep. Costs are applied as proportional charges per unit of turnover.

Sharpe after transaction costs
DGP-A, mean over 3 seeds, at 0, 10, and 25 basis points per unit of turnover.
Equal weight is flat because it never trades. GMM-MVO decays fastest, losing 0.066 of Sharpe by 25 basis points, because regime label switches force abrupt rebalances. RAT-CARD loses 0.066 as well but from a much higher base, and the neural methods generally hold up.
Mean absolute turnover per rebalance
DGP-A, mean over 3 seeds, as a fraction of portfolio value.
The two-stage pipeline trades twice as much as RAT-CARD and forty times as much as risk parity. Soft regime assignments produce smooth weight trajectories; hard regime labels produce jumps. In a live portfolio with real costs and market impact, that difference compounds well beyond what a 25 basis point charge captures.

09 Performance inside each regime

Sharpe ratio conditional on the true regime
DGP-A test set, seed 42. Test composition: 85 growth months, 30 contraction, 5 crisis.
Everything works in growth. The separation appears in contraction, where equal weight collapses to -1.34 while the neural methods hold between -0.27 and -0.46. The two-stage pipeline posts the best contraction number, -0.01, which suggests explicit regime detection does help in downturns even though its full-sample Sharpe is lower. Crisis has five observations, so those bars are noise and should not be read as a result.

10 Computational cost

Parameters against inference latency
Bubble area is proportional to parameter count. Latency is a single forward pass.
All of these are small. RAT-CARD is 24,127 parameters and about 1.0 ms per forward pass. The MLP has the most parameters, 42,826, and the fastest inference, 0.10 ms, because it has no sequential structure. Nothing here is compute-bound; the constraint is data, not throughput.

11 What this means

FINDING 01

End-to-end beats two-stage

Optimizing directly for the portfolio objective outperforms optimizing for regime classification and then allocating, even when the classifier is better at classifying.

FINDING 02

Architecture is not the lever

An LSTM with 16k parameters matches or beats a cross-attention transformer here. Complexity has to be justified by a setting that needs it.

FINDING 03

Constraints buy stability

The unconstrained variant scores higher on raw Sharpe and trades more. Position bounds act as implicit regularizers, and their advantage grows once costs are real.

FINDING 04

Report the null result

Three seeds and 120 test months cannot separate these architectures. Saying so is more useful than picking the seed that wins.

The negative result is the contribution. A large amount of published work in this area compares a new architecture against weak baselines on one data split and declares victory. Running the same protocol across three processes, three seeds, ten alternatives, and a bootstrap test shows that the honest answer is: the neural class wins, the specific architecture does not matter much here, and the regime module cannot be shown to earn its complexity in this setting.

12 Limitations, stated plainly

These are the paper's own limitations, not a summary of them

  1. Synthetic data only. A Markov-switching process is a simplification. Real regimes may not be discrete, transitions may not be Markov, and feature-regime relationships are likely more complex.
  2. Limited DGP variation. Three parameterizations of one generating structure. Different regime counts, non-stationary transitions, or non-Gaussian returns could change the conclusions.
  3. No real-market validation. Nothing here should be read as evidence of real-world portfolio performance.
  4. Modest sample size. 120 test months gives limited statistical power, which is exactly what the wide bootstrap intervals show.
  5. No transaction costs in training. Costs are evaluated but never enter the loss function, so the constrained model's stability advantage is never explicitly rewarded during training.
  6. Simple constraint mechanism. Clamp-and-renormalize is an approximation. Exact differentiable convex constraint layers might give different results at higher computational cost.
  7. Three seeds. Enough to show cross-seed variability, not enough to characterize the true performance distribution.

13 What is next

The questions this study could not answer on synthetic data are the ones worth doing next.

01

Real-market validation

The full protocol on liquid ETF universes with rolling out-of-sample windows and externally dated regimes.

02

Turnover-aware training

Put the cost penalty inside the loss so the constrained model earns its stability advantage explicitly.

03

Non-Markov regimes

Time-varying transition matrices and duration dependence, where soft assignments should adapt and hard classifiers should break.

04

Truly latent regimes

Settings where regime information is not already sitting in observable features. This is the case CARD is actually built for.

05

Larger universes

Fifty or more assets, where regime-specific allocation heads have room to specialize and diversification compounds.

06

Exact constraint layers

Differentiable convex optimization layers in place of clamp-and-renormalize, to separate the architecture from its projection.
